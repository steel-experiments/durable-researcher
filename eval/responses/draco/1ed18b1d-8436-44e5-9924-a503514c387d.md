## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Indian fixed-income market as of December 8, 2025, presents a constructive entry window for fixed-income allocation, driven by sub-7% benchmark sovereign yields, a steep yield curve, and elevated corporate spreads offering 10–14% gross yields on select NCDs. However, the currently available public-issue NCDs are dominated by high-yield fintech lenders and leveraged NBFCs, none of which carry investment-grade (AAA/AA) CRISIL or ICRA ratings in the public issue tranche. For a retiree seeking capital preservation, the risk-reward is asymmetrically skewed toward capital loss on several of these issues. We recommend a selective barbell approach: sovereign/SDL allocation for the anchor, and only the highest-quality corporate NCD tranches for yield enhancement. **Two of five identified issuers carry debt-to-equity ratios exceeding 4.5x and should be flagged for compliance review.**

## 3. POST-TAX YIELD ANALYSIS: GENERIC INVESTOR vs. SENIOR CITIZEN

NCD interest income in India is taxed at the investor's marginal income-tax slab rate under "Income from Other Sources." There is no special tax treatment for NCD interest regardless of issuer type. TDS is deducted at 10% if interest exceeds ₹5,000/year (₹10,000 for certain cases under Section 193), but the actual tax liability is determined by the investor's applicable slab.

### India Income-Tax Slabs — FY 2025-26 (Old vs. New Regime)

| **Taxable Income** | **Old Regime Slab** | **New Regime Slab** |
|---|---|---|
| ₹0 – ₹2.5L | 0% | 0% (up to ₹3L) |
| ₹2.5L – ₹5L | 5% | 5% |
| ₹5L – ₹7.5L | 20% | 10% |
| ₹7.5L – ₹10L | 20% | 15% |
| ₹10L – ₹12.5L | 30% | 20% |
| ₹12.5L – ₹15L | 30% | 25% |
| > ₹15L | 30% | 30% |

*Plus 4% Health & Education Cess on total tax.*

**Senior Citizens (60–80 years):** Additional exemption under Section 80TTB allows deduction of up to ₹50,000 on interest income from deposits (bank/FD/post office), but **this does NOT apply to NCD interest**. NCD interest is fully taxable at slab rate.

For retirees with low taxable income (below ₹5L), NCDs can deliver gross-equivalent yields, making them nominally attractive — but the principal risk is disproportionate.

## 5. HISTORICAL PRECEDENT & PATTERN RECOGNITION

### Indian NBFC Crisis Cycle (2018–2020)

The current landscape echoes structural patterns from the IL&FS default (September 2018) and the subsequent DHFL collapse (2019):

- **IL&FS** defaulted on ₹40,000+ Cr of debt despite holding AA+ ratings from ICRA and CARE until days before default — a catastrophic rating failure.
- **DHFL** had D/E exceeding 8x and a rapidly deteriorating asset quality before its ₹90,000+ Cr default. Rating agencies maintained investment-grade ratings until collapse was imminent.
- **Post-2018 pattern:** NBFCs with D/E >4x, negative free cash flows, and exposure to subprime retail lending have historically experienced **default probabilities 8–12x higher** than AAA-rated corporates.

### Key Lesson for Current NCD Analysis

Several issuers in the current list (Muthoot Capital, Krazybee, Earlysalary, Avanti Finance) exhibit the same pre-stress indicators that preceded the 2018–2020 NBFC crisis: high leverage, thin/eroding margins, and exposure to lower-quality credit segments. **The yield premium being offered (10–14%) is the market's compensation for these exact risks.** Historical precedent suggests that in a rising-rate or economic slowdown scenario, these issuers face:
- Rating downgrades of 2–4 notches within 6–12 months
- Refinancing risk as institutional lenders pull back
- Potential default rates of 5–15% for sub-investment-grade issuers

## 6. REGULATORY LANDSCAPE & POLICY IMPLICATIONS

### Current Regulatory Framework (December 2025)

| **Regulator** | **Jurisdiction** | **Key Policy** |
|---|---|---|
| RBI | NBFCs, Banks, Money Market | Scale-based regulation for NBFCs; tightened asset quality norms; increased capital adequacy requirements |
| SEBI | Securities Markets, NCD Issuance | SEBI (Issue and Listing of Non-Convertible Securities) Regulations, 2021 — mandates minimum rating, disclosures, and debenture trustee appointment |
| RBI (Recent) | FPI Framework | Lifted hedging cost curbs, triggering ₹8,000 Cr FPI bond selloff — creates temporary yield opportunity for domestic buyers |
| Government | Fiscal Policy | 4.4% fiscal deficit target reaffirmed — supports sovereign yield stability |

### Regulatory Risk Factors

1. **RBI NBFC Tightening:** The RBI has progressively tightened NBFC governance since 2018. Any adverse regulatory action against the issuer (e.g., restrictions on lending, additional provisioning requirements) would directly impair their ability to service NCD obligations.
2. **SEBI Listing Requirements:** NCDs must carry a minimum credit rating from SEBI-registered rating agencies. If the rating falls below investment grade post-issuance, there is limited secondary market liquidity, trapping the investor.
3. **Tax Regime Uncertainty:** The new tax regime (default from FY 2023-24) eliminates most deductions. Senior citizens cannot claim 80TTB for NCD interest, removing a potential tax advantage they enjoy with bank deposits.

### Tail Risk Scenarios

1. **Systemic NBFC Stress Event (Probability: 10–15% over 12 months):** A sudden liquidity crunch (similar to 2018) could trigger cascading defaults among leveraged fintech lenders. Investors in unsecured NCDs would face **total capital loss** with limited recovery.

3. **Prepayment / Call Risk:** Some NCDs carry call options. In a declining rate environment, issuers may refinance early, forcing reinvestment at lower yields.
