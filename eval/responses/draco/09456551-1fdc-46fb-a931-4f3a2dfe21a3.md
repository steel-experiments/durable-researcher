# Career Transition into AI Safety Research: A Guide for a Berlin Software Engineer

## Executive Summary

Your background as a backend software engineer positions you well for empirical AI safety research roles — the most accessible entry point in a field that had only ~300 full-time equivalent researchers as of 2022. The most realistic pathway begins with structured upskilling programs in Europe (BlueDot Impact, ARENA, MATS London), followed by empirical contributor roles at European organizations, with the option to pivot to the US later once you've built a research portfolio. Europe offers lower barriers to entry and a collaborative research culture, while the US provides higher funding density and more industry roles — but requires navigating complex visa processes.

---

## 1. Realistic Career Pathways

AI safety research divides into two tracks with different barriers to entry:

### Empirical Research (Your Best Bet)
This involves experimenting with current ML models to test safety properties — work that depends heavily on programming and ML engineering skills. As a backend developer, you likely have the coding chops to apply for **empirical contributor roles immediately**, even without deep ML expertise. The work involves building infrastructure, running experiments, and implementing safety evaluations on frontier models. Exceptionally talented software engineers often transition into this track **in under a year** [1].

### Theoretical Research (Harder Entry)
This track involves conceptual and mathematical work on alignment problems. It requires an exceptionally strong math or theoretical CS background, and roles are fewer, concentrated mainly in academia or nonprofits like the Alignment Research Center. This is likely not the most efficient path unless you have a deep mathematics background [1].

### Research Lead Track (Longer-Term)
If you eventually want to propose projects and lead teams, you'd generally need a PhD from a top machine learning program. This is a 4–6 year commitment but can be pursued after you've established yourself as a contributor [1].

## 2. Structured Upskilling Programs — Your On-Ramp

Three programs stand out as realistic, well-funded transition pathways:

### BlueDot Impact (London/San Francisco)
The leading AI safety talent accelerator. They've trained 7,000+ professionals since 2022, raised $35M, and their alumni work at Anthropic, Google DeepMind, OpenAI, and the UK AI Security Institute. They run courses, provide career support, and organize events globally. This is a low-commitment starting point — you can take their online AI safety fundamentals course while still employed in Berlin [2].

### ARENA — Alignment Research Engineer Accelerator (London)
A 4–5 week in-person bootcamp based at the London Initiative for Safe AI (LISA) in Shoreditch. This is perhaps the **single best-fit program** for your profile. Requirements: strong Python coding and foundational AI math (linear algebra, calculus, probability). Selection involves a coding test and interview. **Crucially, ARENA covers all costs** — travel from Berlin, visa expenses, accommodation, and meals — removing financial risk entirely. Alumni move into roles at Apollo Research (London), Anthropic, and METR. The next cohort (ARENA 8.0) runs May–June 2026 [4].

### MATS — ML Alignment & Theory Scholars (Berkeley & London)
A 12-week research seminar running in both Berkeley (US) and London (UK). Over 527 researchers trained since 2021, with 180+ publications. Fellows receive a **$15,000 stipend**, $12,000 compute budget, housing, and meals. 80% of alumni work in AI alignment/security. The London cohort gives you a European entry point; top performers can extend their research for 6–12 additional months in London with continued funding. MATS explicitly targets "ambitious researchers from a variety of backgrounds" [5].

### Recommended Sequence
1. **Now**: Take BlueDot's online course while working your Berlin job
2. **Apply**: Target ARENA (London) and MATS (London cohort) 
3. **Transition**: Use the portfolio and network from these programs to land empirical contributor roles

## 3. European vs. US Opportunities: A Detailed Comparison

### Research Culture

| Dimension | Europe | United States |
|-----------|--------|---------------|
| **Culture** | Highly collaborative, academia-anchored, reading groups and seminars | More competitive, industry-dominated, faster-paced |
| **Key hubs** | London, Oxford, Cambridge, Zurich (ETH), Berlin, Paris | San Francisco/Berkeley, New York, Seattle |
| **Organizations** | Apollo Research, UK AISI, Conjecture, Safe AI Germany, LISA | OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Redwood Research, METR, ARC |
| **Academic feeders** | Oxford, Cambridge, ETH Zurich | MIT, Stanford, Berkeley, CMU, Princeton |
| **Density** | Growing but distributed; strong community ties | Heavily saturated with elite institutions [1][3][6] |

Europe's ecosystem, while smaller, offers **tighter-knit communities** with active local chapters. In Germany specifically, you have access to Safe AI Germany (national research initiative), Frankfurt AI Safety (fortnightly meetups), and the broader European Network for AI Safety (ENAIS) connecting researchers across the continent [3]. The Berlin AI safety community, while not as prominent as London's, benefits from Germany's strong general tech ecosystem.

### Funding

**US advantages**: The US has significantly higher institutional funding density. Major AI labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepMind's US operations) are headquartered there, and philanthropic funding from Open Philanthropy and other donors flows predominantly to US-based organizations. MATS alone provides $27,000+ per fellow in stipend and compute [5].

**European advantages**: Programs like ARENA are **fully funded** (covering travel, accommodation, and meals), eliminating financial barriers entirely [4]. The UK AI Security Institute (AISI) is government-funded, representing a growing institutional commitment. Post-Brexit, the UK has positioned itself as a regulatory leader in AI safety, creating new funding channels. European PhD programs are often tuition-free or low-cost compared to US programs.

**Bottom line**: The US has more total dollars, but Europe offers more accessible entry-level funding that doesn't require you to already be established.

### Visa Requirements

**Moving to the US** is the harder path. Two primary visa routes:

- **O-1A ("Extraordinary Ability")**: Requires proving you're in the "small percentage who have risen to the very top of the field" with at least three types of evidence (major awards, significant publications, original contributions, high salary). As a pivoting backend engineer without a research portfolio, **this is likely not immediately viable**. You'd need to build a publication record and recognition first — potentially using European positions as a stepping stone [7].

- **H-1B (Specialty Occupation)**: Common for tech workers but subject to an annual lottery with roughly 25-35% selection rates. Requires a US employer sponsor. Processing times can be lengthy, and the annual cap means you may need to wait until the next lottery cycle.

**Staying in Europe** is dramatically simpler:
- As an EU citizen working in Berlin, you already have the right to work anywhere in the EU/EEA.
- **Germany**: Stay where you are; join local AI safety groups while upskilling.
- **UK (post-Brexit)**: Requires a visa, but the UK's Global Talent Visa is specifically designed for researchers and is far more accessible than the O-1A. Many AI safety organizations (Apollo Research, UK AISI, ARENA) are London-based and familiar with sponsoring these visas.
- **Switzerland (ETH Zurich)**: EU citizens have simplified admission.

## 4. Concrete Action Plan

### Immediate (0–3 months)
- Enroll in BlueDot Impact's AI Safety Fundamentals course
- Join the AI Alignment Slack, Alignment Forum, and LessWrong
- Connect with Safe AI Germany and explore Berlin-area EA/AI safety meetups
- Start working through ARENA's free online curriculum (Python-based)
- Begin building a public portfolio: blog posts analyzing safety papers, small interpretability projects

### Near-term (3–12 months)
- Apply to ARENA (London) — your strongest low-risk entry point
- Apply to MATS London cohort as a backup/complement
- Participate in Apart Research's Alignment Jams (hackathons designed to bridge engineering into research)
- Target 1–2 concrete research outputs (even small ones) to build your CV

### Medium-term (1–3 years)
- Land an empirical contributor role at a European org (Apollo Research, UK AISI, or similar)
- Consider whether a PhD at Oxford, Cambridge, or ETH Zurich aligns with your goals
- If drawn to the US ecosystem, use your European research portfolio to strengthen an O-1A or Global Talent Visa application

## 5. Key Uncertainties and Gaps

- **Exact funding figures**: Precise dollar comparisons of European vs. US AI safety philanthropic funding were not available from the sources consulted. The qualitative picture (US has more total funding, Europe has more accessible entry-level funding) is well-supported.
- **Berlin-specific AI safety community size**: While Safe AI Germany exists, the exact size and activity level of Berlin's local AI safety scene is unclear. You may need to be proactive in building connections or traveling to London/Zurich.
- **Salary expectations**: The compensation differential between European and US AI safety roles was not quantified. US roles (especially at frontier labs) likely pay significantly more, but cost of living and visa precarity offset some of that advantage.
- **AI safety job market dynamics in 2025–2026**: The field is growing rapidly, and the landscape may shift significantly with new policy developments, particularly around EU AI Act implementation.
