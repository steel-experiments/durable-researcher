# Independent Directors Under NASDAQ Listing Standards: A Comprehensive Guide

---

## Executive Summary

Under NASDAQ Listing Rule 5605, an independent director is defined as a board member who has no material relationship with the listed company—whether directly, as a partner, shareholder, or officer of an organization that has a relationship with the company—other than service as a director. NASDAQ imposes both a general "material relationship" test (subject to the board's good-faith determination) and a series of specific bright-line disqualification criteria. All NASDAQ-listed companies (with limited exceptions such as controlled companies) must maintain a board composed of a majority of independent directors, along with fully independent audit, compensation, and nominating/governance committees.

---

## 1. Definition of an Independent Director

Under **NASDAQ Listing Rule 5605(a)(2)**, an "independent director" is a person other than an executive officer or employee of the company or its subsidiaries, or any other individual having a relationship that, in the opinion of the company's board of directors, would interfere with the exercise of independent judgment in carrying out the responsibilities of a director.

The core principle is the absence of any **material relationship** with the company. The board of directors is responsible for making an affirmative determination that a director has no material relationship with the listed company, and must disclose this determination in the company's annual proxy statement or annual meeting of shareholders. The board must consider all relevant facts and circumstances, but NASDAQ's rules also provide specific **bright-line disqualification tests** that automatically preclude a finding of independence.

---

## 2. Disqualification Criteria (Who Cannot Serve as Independent)

NASDAQ Listing Rule 5605(b) sets forth the following categorical disqualifications. A director **cannot** be considered independent if:

### A. Employment Relationship
- The director is, or at any time during the past **three years** was, an **executive officer or employee** of the company or any of its parent or subsidiary entities.

### B. Interlocking Directorates
- The director accepts (or has an immediate family member who accepts) any payments from the company in excess of **$120,000** during any twelve-month period within the preceding three fiscal years, other than:
  - Compensation for board or committee service
  - Payments arising solely from investments in the company's securities
  - Payments under non-discretionary employee benefit plans
  - Certain other permitted categories
  *(Note: This $120,000 threshold was established via SEC rulemaking and has been subject to adjustment over time.)*

### C. Family Member — Executive Officer
- An **immediate family member** of the director is, or was at any time during the past three years, an executive officer of the company.
  - "Immediate family member" includes a person's spouse, parents, children, siblings, mothers-in-law, fathers-in-law, sons-in-law, daughters-in-law, brothers-in-law, sisters-in-law, and anyone (other than employees) who shares the director's household.

### D. Audit Firm Affiliation
- The director (or an immediate family member) is, or was within the past three years, a **partner or employee of the company's external or internal auditor**, or the director or family member personally worked on the company's audit within that time.

### E. Business Relationships
- The director is (or was within the past three years) an **executive officer of an entity** that makes payments to, or receives payments from, the listed company for property or services in an amount exceeding the greater of **$200,000 or 5%** of the other entity's consolidated gross revenues (the "bright-line" business relationship test).

### F. Interlocking Compensation Committee
- The director is (or was within the past three years) an executive officer of another entity where any of the company's current executive officers at the same time serves or served on that other entity's **compensation committee**.

### G. Additional Audit Committee Standards (Rule 5605(c)(2))
In addition to the general independence criteria above, members of the **audit committee** must also satisfy heightened independence requirements under **SEC Rule 10A-3**, which adds:
- The director may not accept any consulting, advisory, or other compensatory fee from the issuer (other than board/committee fees), and
- May not be an "affiliated person" of the issuer or any subsidiary.

### H. Additional Compensation Committee Standards (Rule 5605(d))
**Compensation committee** members must meet the general independence tests and also:
- May not accept any consulting, advisory, or other compensatory fee from the company (other than board/committee fees)
- Must not be an affiliated person of the company or its subsidiary
- Must satisfy any additional independence requirements the board deems appropriate

## 3. Eligibility Criteria (Who Qualifies)

To qualify as an independent director under NASDAQ standards, a person must:

1. **Have no material relationship** with the company (financial, familial, consulting, legal, or commercial) as determined in good faith by the board.
2. **Not be a current or former (within 3 years) executive officer or employee** of the company or its parent/subsidiaries.
3. **Not have received** more than $120,000 in non-board-related compensation from the company in any 12-month period during the prior three fiscal years.
4. **Not have an immediate family member** who served as an executive officer of the company within the past three years.
5. **Not be affiliated with the company's auditor** (as partner or employee) within the past three years, nor have had an immediate family member so affiliated.
6. **Not be an executive officer** of an entity with significant business dealings (exceeding the $200,000/5% threshold) with the company within the past three years.
7. **Be affirmatively determined** by the board of directors to be independent, with that determination disclosed to shareholders.

The board must make its independence determination based on all relevant facts and circumstances, not merely the absence of bright-line disqualifications.

## 4. Companies Required to Have Independent Directors

### A. Majority Independent Board (Rule 5605(b)(1))
**All NASDAQ-listed companies** (other than the exceptions below) must have a board of directors composed of a **majority of independent directors**.

### B. Audit Committee (Rule 5605(c))
- Each listed company must have an **audit committee** composed of at least **three members**, all of whom must be independent directors (meeting both NASDAQ and SEC Rule 10A-3 standards).
- At least one member must have financial sophistication (as determined by the board).
- At least one member must be an "audit committee financial expert" (under SEC rules).

### C. Compensation Committee (Rule 5605(d))
- Each listed company must have a **compensation committee** (or equivalent) composed of at least **two independent directors**.
- The committee must have a written charter addressing its purpose, duties, and annual performance evaluation.

### D. Nominating/Governance Committee (Rule 5605(d))
- Each listed company must have a **nominating and governance committee** (or equivalent) composed of at least **two independent directors**.
- Must have a written charter covering director nominee selection, governance policies, and annual self-evaluation.

### E. Exceptions — Companies NOT Required to Comply

| Exception | Description |
|---|---|
| **Controlled Companies** (Rule 5615(c)) | A company of which more than 50% of the voting power is held by an individual, group, or another company may rely on exemptions from the majority-independent board requirement, compensation committee independence, and nominating committee independence requirements. Must still have an independent audit committee. |
| **Smaller Reporting Companies** | Reduced audit committee requirements (e.g., only one member must be an audit committee financial expert rather than all members needing full financial sophistication). |
| **Limited Partnerships / LLCs** | Alternative listing standards may apply under Rule 5615. |
| **Foreign Private Issuers** | May follow home-country governance practices with certain disclosures, though audit committee independence still generally required under SEC rules. |
| **Companies in Bankruptcy** | May qualify for temporary relief from certain corporate governance requirements. |
| **Newly Listed Companies** | Generally granted a transition period (typically one year or the next annual meeting) to achieve compliance with board and committee independence requirements. |

### F. Special Requirements for Smaller Companies
- **Small Market Capitalization Companies** may be eligible for a phase-in period for board independence and committee requirements.
- Companies listing on the **NASDAQ Capital Market** (as opposed to the NASDAQ Global Select Market or NASDAQ Global Market) may have certain scaled requirements.

---

## 5. Enforcement and Compliance

NASDAQ monitors compliance through:
- **Annual confirmations** filed by listed companies through the NASDAQ Listing Center
- **Continuing listing standards** reviewed on an ongoing basis
- **Delisting** as the ultimate remedy for persistent non-compliance, though as noted by The Conference Board, "other than delisting a company… there really is no penalty" for failing to maintain independent directors

Companies must **disclose** the basis for each independence determination in their proxy statements or annual meeting materials.
