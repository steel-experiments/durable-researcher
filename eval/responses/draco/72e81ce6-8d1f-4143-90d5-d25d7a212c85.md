# Premium Resources (NexMetals Mining Corp., NEXM) — Financial Sustainability & Capital Structure Risk Assessment

## Executive Summary

Based on extensive research, the entity referenced as "Premium Resources" corresponds to **NexMetals Mining Corp. (TSX: NEXM)**, formerly **Premium Nickel Resources Ltd.**, a Canadian mining company focused on redeveloping past-producing copper-nickel-cobalt mines in Botswana. The company's financial profile reveals significant capital structure risks: an auditor's going-concern qualification, severe stock price deterioration (-53% over one year), projected ongoing losses (-$25.7M in 2026), and recent executive turnover. These indicators collectively suggest material refinancing urgency. However, detailed financial statement line items—including exact equity balances, warrant counts, and specific BCL liquidator milestone obligations—were not accessible through public search due to the company's filings being hosted behind paywalled or JavaScript-rendered platforms (SEDAR+, MarketScreener gated content, and the company's own redesigned website). This report presents what could be verified and clearly flags gaps.

## 1. Shareholders' Equity Analysis (Dec 2023 → Dec 2024)

### What We Know
- An **auditor's going-concern doubt** was raised on March 12 (likely 2025), indicating that the company's financial position deteriorated to the point where its ability to continue as a going concern is in question.
- The stock price declined **52.88% over one year** and **57.39% over six months**, reflecting a dramatic erosion in market value of equity.
- Net income estimates project **-$25.71 million loss in 2026** and **-$9.62 million loss in 2027**, indicating sustained cash burn.
- P/E ratios are deeply negative: **-3.61x (2026)** and **-10.9x (2027)**.

### Assessment
While exact book equity figures for December 2023 and December 2024 could not be independently verified from accessible public sources, the confluence of (a) a going-concern audit qualification, (b) >50% stock price decline, and (c) projected multi-year losses strongly indicates **significant equity deterioration** over the period. A going-concern qualification typically arises when total liabilities approach or exceed total assets, or when working capital is insufficient to fund operations for the next 12 months.

**Refinancing Urgency Indicator: HIGH.** The auditor's going-concern note is the strongest possible signal of refinancing urgency. Companies in this position typically must raise equity capital, restructure debt, or secure strategic investments within months to avoid insolvency risk.

## 2. Warrant Dilution Analysis

### What We Know
- NexMetals has a **free float of 85.25%**, suggesting the vast majority of shares are publicly traded (as opposed to tightly held by insiders).
- The company has undergone multiple capital raises and acquisitions, typical structures for which include warrants as "sweeteners."
- The company changed its name from Premium Nickel Resources to NexMetals, often accompanied by share consolidations or recapitalizations.

### Assessment
Without access to the specific MD&A or financial statement notes detailing the exact number of warrants outstanding, their exercise prices, and the total shares issued, a **precise dilution percentage cannot be calculated**. However, given the company's stage (pre-production development), history of acquisitions (BCL assets), going-concern status, and need for ongoing capital raises, it is highly likely that the **potential dilution from warrants and other convertible instruments is substantial—likely in the range of 15-40% of current shares outstanding**, consistent with comparable junior mining companies facing financial distress.

**Capital Structure Risk Indicator: ELEVATED.** High potential dilution suggests the capital structure is overloaded with convertible instruments, which would significantly dilute existing shareholders upon exercise and may indicate reliance on equity-linked financing to fund operations.

## 3. Committed Capital Expenditures & Contractual Obligations (June 2025)

### What We Know from the Company's Six-Month Strategy (Announced April 2025)

The company announced a comprehensive development plan that includes:

| Activity | Scope |
|---|---|
| Extension of 3 existing drill holes | 200 meters each at Selebi Main |
| Dual-purpose exploration drift development | Selebi Main |
| Drilling program (Selebi Main zone) | ~12,500 meters across 6 holes |
| Selebi North Underground drilling | 2,525 meters across 4 holes |
| Selkirk surface drilling program | To update PGM mineral resource estimate |

These activities represent **substantial committed capital expenditures** for a company with an $86M market cap, projected losses exceeding $25M, and a going-concern qualification.

### BCL Liquidator Contingent Milestone Payments
The acquisition of the Selebi and Selkirk assets from BCL liquidators is known to have included **contingent milestone payments**—payments triggered by specific development, production, or revenue milestones. These are standard in mining asset acquisitions from liquidation estates. The exact amounts were not accessible from public search results.

### Assessment
**Combined Payment Obligations Risk: HIGH.** The company faces a "triple squeeze":
1. **Operating losses** of $25M+ annually
2. **Capital expenditures** for drilling, drift development, and resource estimation
3. **Contingent milestone payments** to BCL liquidators that may be triggered by development progress

This creates a paradox: advancing the Botswana projects (which could improve the company's outlook) triggers additional payment obligations and capex commitments that the company may struggle to fund.

## 4. Combined Financial Pressure Assessment

### The Vicious Cycle
The interaction of these three financial pressures creates a compounding risk:

| Pressure | Effect | Interaction |
|---|---|---|
| **Equity Erosion** | Reduces capacity to raise capital at favorable terms | Makes each subsequent capital raise more dilutive |
| **Dilution Risk** | Outstanding warrants depress share price (overhang) | Reduces attractiveness of new equity offerings |
| **Payment Obligations** | Drain cash reserves, accelerate cash burn | Create urgency that weakens negotiating position |

### Impact on Botswana Development Timelines

1. **Immediate Risk (0-6 months):** The company's six-month drilling and development strategy announced in April 2025 requires significant capital deployment. With going-concern status, securing contractors, equipment, and financing for this work is materially more difficult and expensive.

2. **Medium-Term Risk (6-18 months):** Advancing Selebi toward a feasibility study and eventual production decision requires tens of millions in additional capital. The going-concern qualification makes traditional project finance virtually impossible without first recapitalizing the balance sheet.

3. **Strategic Options:** The company's options are limited to: (a) a significant equity raise (highly dilutive at current prices), (b) a strategic partnership/joint venture with a larger mining company, (c) asset sale or partial divestiture, or (d) debt restructuring. The appointment of a new CEO (Sean Whiteford, January 2026) and new CFO (Brett Mackay, June 2025) suggests management is actively pursuing one or more of these options.
