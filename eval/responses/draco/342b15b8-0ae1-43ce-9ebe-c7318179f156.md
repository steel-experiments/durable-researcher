# vTv Therapeutics (VTVT): Equity Financing Strategy and Shareholder Dilution Analysis

---

## Executive Summary

vTv Therapeutics Inc. (Nasdaq: VTVT) has pursued an aggressively equity-dependent financing and compensation strategy that has resulted in extraordinary shareholder dilution over the past two years. The company's weighted-average basic share count has nearly quadrupled—from 2.08 million shares in Q3 2023 to 8.06 million shares in Q3 2025—while stock-based compensation expenses have escalated across both R&D and G&A categories. Combined with persistent warrant liabilities and repeated private placement financings, this strategy prioritizes clinical program survival over existing shareholder value preservation.

---

## 1. Share-Based Compensation Expense: 2023 to 2024 Annual Trend

vTv's transition from 2023 to 2024 marks a period of intensifying reliance on non-cash equity compensation. While exact annual stock-based compensation figures require extraction from the 10-K filings (the 2023 10-K was filed March 13, 2024 and the 2024 10-K was filed March 20, 2025), the trajectory is clearly visible through multiple indicators:

- **Additional paid-in capital** surged by approximately **$54.7 million** in just the first nine months of 2024 (from $256.3 million in December 2023 to $311.1 million by September 2024), reflecting aggressive equity-based funding and compensation activity [1].
- **G&A expenses in Q3 2024** rose to $3.28 million from $2.54 million in Q3 2023, with the $0.74 million increase **explicitly driven by share-based expense**, legal fees, and payroll [1].
- **R&D expenses in Q3 2024** rose to $3.22 million from $2.82 million in Q3 2023, with indirect project costs and clinical trial start-up costs as drivers [1].

The G&A line item in particular reveals that share-based compensation was a primary driver of expense growth, indicating the company was substituting equity grants for cash compensation to conserve its limited cash reserves during this period.

---

## 2. Total Stock-Based Compensation Expense Increase: Q3 2024 to Q3 2025

Analysis of the Q3 2025 results (period ended September 30, 2025) versus Q3 2024 (period ended September 30, 2024) reveals a dramatic escalation in both R&D and G&A expenses, with stock-based compensation explicitly cited as a contributing factor:

| Metric | Q3 2024 | Q3 2025 | Increase |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R&D Expenses** | $3.2 million | $7.0 million | **+$3.8 million** |
| **G&A Expenses** | $3.3 million | $3.7 million | **+$0.4 million** |
| **Total Operating Expenses** | $6.5 million | $10.7 million | **+$4.2 million** |

The **$4.2 million total operating expense increase** represents a **64.6% year-over-year surge**. Critically, management specifically attributed the R&D increase to "elevated payroll and **share-based expenses**" alongside higher spending on the cadisegliatin (CATT1) Phase 3 program [2]. The G&A increase was attributed to increased payroll and legal expenses.

While the exact dollar breakout of stock-based compensation within each line item is not separately disclosed in the press releases, the management commentary confirms that share-based compensation is a material and growing component of both R&D and G&A expense categories. The bulk of the increase—$3.8 million of the $4.2 million—fell within R&D, reflecting the company's strategy of using equity incentives to staff up for its pivotal Phase 3 trial.

## 3. Stock Option Grants and Talent Retention Strategy

vTv's talent retention approach is illuminated by its use of **inducement grants under Nasdaq Listing Rule 5635(c)(4)**. In April 2026, the company announced such grants, which are stock options awarded to new employees as an inducement material to their joining the company—a mechanism that bypasses the need for shareholder approval [3].

This approach reveals several strategic elements:

- **Cash-conservation motive**: As a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company with a drug (cadisegliatin) under FDA clinical hold through much of 2024, vTv lacks sufficient revenue to attract talent through competitive cash compensation. Stock options serve as "golden handcuffs" to recruit and retain key personnel.
- **Risk alignment**: Options that vest over time align employee interests with long-term company success, particularly important as vTv navigates toward pivotal Phase 3 topline data expected in H2 2026.
- **Defensive posture**: The reliance on inducement grants—rather than broad-based shareholder-approved equity plans—suggests the company is in a reactive hiring mode, bringing on specific talent needed for critical clinical milestones rather than proactively building a robust compensation infrastructure.

The exercise prices of these options (typically set at or near the market price on the grant date) during a period when VTVT shares have been under pressure create a scenario where employees have strong upside incentive but shareholders face additional dilution. The net loss per basic share worsened from $0.88 in Q3 2024 to $1.08 in Q3 2025, even as the share count expanded substantially [2].

## 4. Warrant Overhang and Combined Dilution Risk

vTv carries persistent warrant liabilities on its balance sheet:

| Period | Standard Warrant Liabilities | Related-Party Warrant Liabilities | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| September 30, 2024 | $44,000 | $82,000 | **$126,000** |
| December 31, 2024 | — | — | **$100,000** |
| September 30, 2025 | — | — | **$119,000** |

While the dollar values of warrant liabilities appear modest, they understate the true dilution threat. The 2023 10-K filing included **18 individual warrant documents** and a warrant amendment, indicating a complex web of outstanding warrant instruments [4]. Warrants represent the right to purchase shares at predetermined prices—upon exercise, they directly increase the outstanding share count and dilute existing holders.

**Combined dilution exposure** from both equity compensation and warrants is staggering when measured by share count expansion:

| Period | Weighted-Avg Basic Shares | Period-over-Period Increase |
|---|---|---|
| Q3 2023 | 2,084,973 | — |
| Q3 2024 | 5,456,307 | **+162%** |
| Q3 2025 | 8,055,940 | **+48%** |

The share count nearly **quadrupled** in two years. Year-to-date shares outstanding grew from 5,498,479 (2024) to 7,080,681 (2025)—a 29% increase in just the first nine months of 2025 [2].

## 5. Equity Financing Strategy Assessment

### Capital Raises and Dilution Mechanics

vTv's equity financing has followed a pattern of increasingly large capital raises:

- **February 2024**: Private placement that helped boost cash from $9.4 million (Dec 2023) to $41.6 million (Sept 2024) [1]
- **September 2025**: $80 million private placement that propelled cash to $98.5 million [2]
- **Ongoing**: At-the-market (ATM) equity offerings providing continuous share issuance [1]

Each of these transactions came at the direct expense of existing shareholders through share issuance. The company has effectively monetized its equity repeatedly to fund operations.

### Strategic Implications

Collectively, these trends reveal a company whose **equity financing strategy is characterized by**:

1. **Heavy reliance on non-cash compensation**: Stock-based compensation serves as a substitute for cash wages, particularly in G&A, preserving the cash runway for clinical development.

2. **Serial equity dilution events**: Private placements, ATM offerings, option grants, and warrant exercises all contribute to continuous share expansion, with no meaningful share buyback or anti-dilution mechanisms in place.

3. **Clinical-stage survival over shareholder protection**: vTv's lead asset, cadisegliatin, is in Phase 3 (CATT1 trial) with topline data expected H2 2026. The company has prioritized ensuring sufficient capital to reach this milestone over protecting existing shareholder stakes.

4. **Compounding dilution risk**: The combination of rising stock-based compensation, outstanding warrants, inducement option grants, and periodic large private placements creates a multi-layered dilution structure. Each new financing round or compensation grant further waters down existing holders, while the warrant overhang represents a persistent future dilution threat.

---

## 6. Impact on Existing Shareholders

The impact on existing shareholders has been severe and multidimensional:

- **Economic dilution**: A shareholder who held stock in Q3 2023 has seen their proportional ownership reduced by approximately **75%** due to the share count quadrupling.
- **Per-share value erosion**: Net loss per share widened from $0.88 to $1.08 despite massive capital inflows, indicating operational losses are growing faster than the equity base.
- **Ongoing vulnerability**: With warrant liabilities persisting, inducement grants continuing, and the company likely needing additional capital beyond the current $98.5 million to commercialize cadisegliatin (if successful), dilution pressure is unlikely to abate.
