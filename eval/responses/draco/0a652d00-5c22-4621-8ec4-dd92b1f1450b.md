# Cyber Insurance Comparison Report: Chubb, Coalition & Cowbell Cyber
### For a 15-Employee Austin, TX Business with Dual PCI-DSS/HIPAA Exposure ($2–5M Revenue)

---

## Executive Summary

For a small business in Austin, Texas handling both customer payment data (PCI-DSS) and employee health records (HIPAA), the cyber insurance market offers meaningfully different value propositions from its three leading carriers. **Coalition** stands out for using full policy limits (not sublimits) for regulatory fines and offering the most integrated incident response platform. **Cowbell Cyber** provides the lowest entry-level premiums with optional regulatory endorsements up to $5M. **Chubb** offers the deepest claims-paying reputation and broadest defense-cost coverage, but at the highest premium and with the most conservative posture on punitive fines. Texas law is relatively favorable for insureds seeking regulatory fine coverage, which is a meaningful advantage for an Austin-based business.

## 1. Regulatory Fine Coverage & Sublimits

This is the most critical differentiator among the three carriers for a business with dual PCI-DSS and HIPAA exposure.

### How Regulatory Sublimits Work

Most cyber policies set a **separate, lower sublimit** for regulatory fines and penalties — meaning even if you purchase a $1M policy, the insurer may only cover $250K–$1M in regulatory fines, with the remainder available for other breach costs (forensics, notification, business interruption). This matters because HIPAA violations can reach up to **$1.9 million per year per identical violation category**, and PCI-DSS penalties flow contractually through acquiring banks and can be substantial.

### Carrier-by-Carrier Breakdown

| Carrier | Regulatory Fine Approach | Sublimit Structure | Premium Range (Small Tech, 2024) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coalition** | Full policy limits available for regulatory fines — no separate sublimit required | Uses the aggregate policy limit (e.g., $1M limit = $1M available for fines) | $6,800–$11,900/yr |
| **Chubb** | Covers fines only where "insurable by law" | Sublimits vary by policy form; more conservative adjudication of claims | $9,200–$15,500/yr |
| **Cowbell Cyber** | Regulatory coverage via **optional endorsement** | Up to $5M via endorsement (must be specifically added) | $6,000–$10,500/yr |

**Coalition's approach is uniquely advantageous** for dual-exposure businesses. By not imposing a separate regulatory sublimit, Coalition allows the full policy limit to be applied toward HIPAA or PCI-DSS fines if needed. Coalition also offers a **"Manuscript CA Fines Endorsement"** that can be adapted for strict jurisdictions, though Texas law is already favorable on this front.

**Chubb's track record is mixed.** Chubb paid $20M toward Morgan Stanley's $35M SEC hardware-disposal fine (demonstrating willingness to cover regulatory defense), but **denied an $18M punitive CCPA fine for Facebook**, illustrating their line-drawing on punitive vs. compensatory fines.

**Cowbell Cyber requires proactive action** — the regulatory fine endorsement must be elected at purchase; it is not included in the base policy.

### Insurability of HIPAA and PCI-DSS Fines

A critical legal nuance: **cyber policies generally cover only "compensatory" fines, not "punitive" ones.** This is governed by state public policy. In Texas, the legal environment is insurer-friendly and does **not statutorily bar coverage for punitive damages** under certain circumstances, giving Austin-based businesses broader protection than, say, California (where Insurance Code §533 restricts this heavily). HIPAA civil monetary penalties levied by HHS/OCR are more likely to be treated as compensatory and thus insurable, while criminal penalties for "willful neglect" are universally excluded across all carriers.

PCI-DSS fines are a different animal — they are **contractual penalties** passed through payment card brands and acquiring banks. These are generally insurable as contractual liability, but the policy must explicitly include "privacy regulatory defense" or a contractual liability carve-back.

**Recommendation:** Ensure your policy includes a **"most favorable jurisdiction" endorsement** or explicit regulatory defense carve-back to maximize the odds of fine coverage, regardless of carrier.

## 2. Incident Response Services & SLAs

### Coalition — Active Insurance Model (Strongest Integrated Offering)

Coalition operates a proprietary incident response arm called **Coalition Incident Response (CIR)**, which functions as both a pre-claim hotline and a full digital forensics and incident response (DFIR) service.

- **Three-step SLA process:** (1) **Investigate** — in-house experts analyze forensic artifacts to determine threat actor tactics; (2) **Remediate** — runs concurrently with investigation to minimize damage, prioritizing restoring offline backups over paying ransoms; (3) **Communicate** — CIR acts as technical expert to guide internal/external communications and ensures compliance with applicable state breach notification laws.
- **No published response-time SLAs** (e.g., no guaranteed "4-hour response" commitment documented publicly), though the process is described as immediate.
- **Cost advantage:** Coalition reports that CIR **resolved 46% of incidents without additional costs or using up policyholder deductibles** in their most recent reported year.
- **Pre-incident capabilities:** Coalition runs an "Active Risk Assessment" (dark web scanning pre-bind) and "Active Protection" (continuous monitoring post-bind). Policyholders experience **less than one-third the claim frequency** of the broader cyber insurance market (per 2020/2021 NAIC data).
- **Post-incident:** CIR offers enhanced cybersecurity services including Managed Detection & Response (MDR), IR Tabletop Exercises, and Customized Security Assessments.

### Chubb — Traditional Claims Model

Chubb does not publish detailed incident response SLAs publicly. Their model relies on a traditional **claims-adjuster approach** with pre-approved vendor panels (forensic firms, breach counsel, PR firms). Chubb's strength is its **claims-paying reputation and global capacity**, but it lacks Coalition's integrated, tech-native IR platform. Response times and vendor selection are coordinated case-by-case through the claims process.

### Cowbell Cyber — Closed-Loop Approach

Cowbell markets a **"closed-loop" approach: Assess → Insure → Respond → Improve**, emphasizing continuous protection:

- **Real-time risk intelligence** tailored to each insured organization
- **Expert claims support** described as responsive "when it matters most"
- Coverage described as "designed for real-world breaches"
- Post-incident, Cowbell cycles back to the "improve" phase to strengthen resilience

No specific SLA response times are published. Cowbell's model is more consultative and adaptive than Coalition's hands-on CIR, but less detailed in publicly documented IR workflows.

| IR Model | In-house CIR team + vendor panel | Claims adjuster + pre-approved vendor panel | Closed-loop advisory model |
| Published SLAs | No specific time guarantees | No public SLAs | No public SLAs |

## 3. Credit Monitoring & Legal Defense Coverage

### Credit Monitoring

All three carriers cover **credit monitoring and notification expenses** as part of breach remediation costs. These are typically categorized as "Public Relations & Remediation Costs" within the policy. For context, credit monitoring for a breach involving 1,000 records typically costs $150K–$300K (12–24 months of monitoring at $10–$25/individual), so for a 15-employee business handling customer payment data, exposure will depend on customer volume.

- **Coalition** and **Cowbell** tend to include credit monitoring within the broader incident response cost bucket without a separate sublimit.
- **Chubb** may impose specific sublimits depending on the policy form.

### Legal Defense

Legal defense against regulatory actions is covered only if the policy includes an explicit **privacy regulatory defense clause or carve-back.**

- **Coalition:** Legal defense is included within the full policy limit structure. Coalition's CIR team collaborates with the insured's legal counsel during investigations.
- **Chubb:** Has demonstrated willingness to fund major regulatory defense (e.g., the $20M Morgan Stanley contribution). Defense costs are typically "inside the limit" (i.e., they reduce the available policy limit).
- **Cowbell Cyber:** Defense costs are covered under the regulatory endorsement, if elected. Without the endorsement, coverage may be limited.

**Key insight:** The Zoom FTC settlement (covered by Beazley) demonstrated that even when regulators impose no cash fine, **defense costs alone can be substantial** — the policy covered the full cost of responding to the FTC investigation. This underscores why legal defense coverage matters as much as fine coverage itself.

## 4. Policy Exclusions for E-Commerce Businesses

### Universal Exclusions Across All Three Carriers

All three carriers include these standard exclusions:

1. **Intentional or fraudulent acts:** No coverage for fines resulting from willful misconduct or deliberate data misuse.
2. **Punitive/exemplary damages:** Excluded unless explicitly added via endorsement (e.g., "most favorable jurisdiction" endorsement).
3. **Prior acts/known breaches:** Losses from incidents that occurred or were known before the policy inception date.
4. **Contractual liability assumed beyond standard PCI-DSS:** Custom indemnification clauses in vendor agreements may not be covered.
5. **Infrastructure failure:** Outages caused by cloud providers or ISPs, unless specifically endorsed.

### E-Commerce–Specific Exclusions to Watch

For a business processing online payments:

- **Failure to maintain PCI-DSS compliance:** If the business cannot demonstrate it was PCI-compliant at the time of breach, carriers may **deny the claim entirely** or significantly reduce the payout. This is a near-universal exclusion.
- **Social engineering / funds transfer fraud:** Often sublimited or excluded from base cyber policies; may require a separate endorsement or crime policy.
- **Nation-state attacks:** Some policies exclude or sublimit losses from nation-state-sponsored cyberattacks (escalating concern in 2024–2025).
- **General liability umbrellas will NOT cover privacy fines** — cyber-specific policies are essential.

### Carrier-Specific Notes

- **Chubb** is the most conservative in adjudicating punitive vs. compensatory fines, which could disadvantage an e-commerce business facing aggressive state AG actions.
- **Coalition's** full-limit approach to regulatory fines provides the broadest safety net for a dual-exposure e-commerce business.
- **Cowbell Cyber's** optional endorsement model means you must be diligent about selecting regulatory coverage at purchase.

## 5. Premium Drivers for $2–5M Revenue Businesses with Dual PCI/HIPAA Exposure

### Market Benchmarks

For companies with under $50M in revenue, the **median cyber limit purchased is $1M**, with an **average annual premium of ~$11,200**. Regulatory sublimits in this tier typically range from **$250K to $1M**.

### Specific Premium Ranges for Small Business

| Carrier | Estimated Annual Premium | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Coalition | $6,800–$11,900 | Full regulatory limits included |
| Chubb | $9,200–$15,500 | Most conservative underwriting |
| Cowbell Cyber | $6,000–$10,500 | Lowest entry point; regulatory endorsement adds cost |

### Key Factors Driving Your Premiums

1. **Dual regulatory exposure (HIPAA + PCI-DSS):** Handling both protected health information and payment card data inherently elevates your risk profile. This is a **premium multiplier**, not just an additive factor. Expect to be priced at the higher end of the ranges above.

2. **Revenue ($2–5M):** Revenue is the primary rating base. At $5M, you'll pay more than at $2M, all else equal. The $2–5M range typically corresponds to $1M in recommended coverage limits.

3. **Data volume and records count:** The number of customer payment records and employee health records you hold directly impacts exposure. A business with 10,000 customer cards will be rated differently than one with 100,000.

4. **Security posture and compliance maturity:** This is the **single largest variable** you control:
   - Businesses that pass independent compliance audits can receive **5%–15% premium credits**
   - Proactive security measures (MFA, EDR, network segmentation, offline backups) can reduce premiums by **5%–20%**
   - Demonstrated compliance can reduce regulatory fines by up to **40%** in the event of a breach (HIPAA recognizes "good faith" compliance efforts)

5. **Industry vertical:** Texas-based businesses in technology, healthcare, and financial services are rated in higher-risk classes. Austin's tech-heavy market means underwriters see elevated threat frequency.

6. **Claims history:** First-time buyers typically face standard rates; prior claims can increase premiums 25%–50%.

7. **Texas legal environment (favorable):** Texas does not statutorily bar punitive damage coverage, which can actually *reduce* premiums slightly compared to California businesses, since insurers face less uncertainty about coverage enforceability.

8. **Claims severity trends:** Cyber claims severity for small businesses **rose 56%** in the most recent reported year, creating upward pressure on all premiums industry-wide.

### Bottom Line for Your Business

For a 15-employee Austin business with dual PCI-DSS/HIPAA exposure and $2–5M in revenue, **Coalition offers the best balance of coverage breadth, incident response capability, and cost** — particularly because its full-limit approach to regulatory fines eliminates the sublimit gap that could leave you exposed to a $1.9M HIPAA penalty with only $250K in coverage. However, if budget is the primary constraint and you have strong security controls, **Cowbell Cyber** with the regulatory endorsement provides the lowest entry point. **Chubb** is best suited if you value the financial strength and claims reputation of the world's largest P&C insurer and can absorb the higher premium.

**All three carriers should be quoted through an independent cyber insurance broker** who can negotiate manuscript endorsements specific to your dual-exposure profile. Ensure any policy you select includes: (1) regulatory defense carve-back, (2) most favorable jurisdiction endorsement, (3) PCI-DSS contractual liability coverage, and (4) credit monitoring within remediation costs without a separate sublimit.
