# Educational Options-Trading Framework for a $700 Account

> **DISCLAIMER:** This document is for educational and illustrative purposes only. It does not constitute personalized financial advice, investment recommendations, or a solicitation to trade. Options trading involves substantial risk of loss and is not suitable for all investors. Targeting $200–300/week on a $700 account implies a ~29–43% weekly return, which is extraordinarily aggressive and not sustainable for the vast majority of traders. Past hypothetical results do not predict future performance.

---

## 1. Position-Sizing Rules & Maximum Risk Per Trade

### Account Parameters
| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Starting Account Balance | $700 |
| Maximum Risk Per Trade | **$35–$49** (5–7% of account) |
| Maximum Concurrent Positions | **1–2** (no more than 10–14% of account at risk at any time) |
| Maximum Weekly Risk Exposure | **~$100–$150** (roughly 15–20% of account across all open trades) |
| Minimum Cash Reserve | **$150–$200** kept uncommitted at all times |

### Position-Sizing Rules

1. **5% Hard Cap Rule:** Never risk more than $35 (5% of $700) on a single trade. This is the absolute ceiling; many traders prefer 2–3% ($14–$21) for greater survivability during losing streaks.

2. **Defined Risk Only:** Every trade must have a mathematically calculable maximum loss known *before* entry. This rules out naked short options and naked short straddles/strangles, which carry unlimited or very large risk.

3. **Spread Width Sizing:** For vertical spreads, the maximum loss equals (Strike Width × 100) − Net Premium Received (for credit spreads) or Net Debit Paid (for debit spreads). The strike width must be chosen so that the maximum loss stays within the $35–$49 envelope.

4. **Drawdown Circuit Breaker:** If the account drops 20% ($140 loss, bringing the account to $560), reduce position size by 50% until the account recovers to its starting balance. If the account drops 30% ($210), halt trading entirely and review strategy.

## 2. Permitted Option Strategies

Only **defined-risk** strategies are permitted within this framework:

| Strategy | Structure | Max Profit | Max Loss | Capital Efficiency for $700 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull Call Debit Spread** | Buy lower-strike call / Sell higher-strike call | Strike width − net debit paid | Net debit paid | ✅ Good — risk = debit paid |
| **Bear Put Debit Spread** | Buy higher-strike put / Sell lower-strike put | Strike width − net debit paid | Net debit paid | ✅ Good — risk = debit paid |
| **Bull Put Credit Spread** | Sell higher-strike put / Buy lower-strike put | Net credit received | Strike width × 100 − credit | ✅ Good if $1–$2 wide strikes |
| **Bear Call Credit Spread** | Sell lower-strike call / Buy higher-strike call | Net credit received | Strike width × 100 − credit | ✅ Good if $1–$2 wide strikes |
| **Single-Leg Long Call** | Buy a call | Theoretically unlimited | Premium paid | ⚠️ Permitted but discouraged — high theta decay |
| **Single-Leg Long Put** | Buy a put | Strike − premium paid | Premium paid | ⚠️ Permitted but discouraged — high theta decay |

**Prohibited strategies** (for this framework): naked short calls, naked short puts, uncovered straddles/strangles, ratio spreads with naked legs, and any strategy where maximum loss is undefined or exceeds the $49/trade limit.

**Preferred approach for $700:** Narrow ($1 or $2 wide) credit spreads and debit spreads. A $1-wide spread risks $100 minus the credit (or costs a small debit), while a $2-wide spread risks $200 minus the credit. To stay within the $35–$49 risk envelope, **$1-wide credit spreads with narrow strikes** are the most capital-efficient choice, though this limits per-trade profit to a few dollars per contract.

## 3. Stock Selection Criteria (Technicals)

### Universe Filter
- **Price range:** $10–$200 (avoid penny stocks and ultra-high-priced names where $1–$2 strike spreads are insignificant).
- **Average daily volume:** ≥ 2,000,000 shares.
- **Options availability:** Weekly and standard expiration cycles available.
- **Market cap:** ≥ $1 billion (large/mid-cap for institutional liquidity).

### Technical Entry Criteria

| Indicator | Requirement | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **RSI (14-day)** | RSI between 30–40 for bullish entries (near oversold); RSI between 60–70 for bearish entries (near overbought) | Identifies potential reversal zones |
| **MACD** | Bullish: MACD line crosses above signal line. Bearish: MACD line crosses below signal line | Confirms momentum direction |
| **Support/Resistance** | Clear horizontal or trend-line levels visible on daily and 4-hour charts. For bullish trades, price should be within 1–2% of a tested support level. For bearish trades, price should be rejecting resistance. | Provides defined inflection points |
| **Volume confirmation** | On the entry day, volume should be ≥ 120% of the 20-day average volume | Confirms institutional participation |
| **Moving Averages** | Price above the 20-day EMA for bullish; price below the 20-day EMA for bearish | Confirms short-term trend |
| **VIX / Implied Volatility** | Prefer entries when IV rank is ≥ 30 (credit spreads benefit from higher IV) or IV rank < 30 for debit spreads (cheaper premiums) | Ensures options are priced advantageously |

### Watchlist Construction
Maintain a watchlist of 10–20 high-liquidity names (e.g., AAPL, MSFT, TSLA, AMZN, META, NVDA, AMD, BAC, XOM, SPY, QQQ, IWM) and scan daily for technical setups matching the criteria above.

## 4. Strike & Expiry Selection Rules (3–21 Day Holds)

### Expiry Selection
| DTE Range | Use Case | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **3–7 DTE** | Aggressive entries with strong momentum confirmation | Fast theta decay (beneficial for credit spreads); high gamma risk; must monitor closely |
| **8–14 DTE** | Preferred window — balances theta decay with manageable gamma | Sweet spot for swing-trading spreads |
| **15–21 DTE** | More conservative, allows time for the thesis to play out | Slower theta; wider breakeven buffer |

**Rule:** Never trade 0–2 DTE unless prepared for binary-outcome risk. Avoid holding through earnings announcements.

### Strike Selection

**For Credit Spreads (sell the option closer to the money):**
- Sell strikes with a **delta of 0.15–0.25** (roughly 75–85% probability of expiring OTM per standard options pricing models).
- Buy the next strike $1–$2 further OTM for protection.
- Example: If stock is at $100 and you're bearish, sell the $105 call and buy the $107 call (for a $2-wide bear call spread).

**For Debit Spreads (buy the option closer to the money):**
- Buy strikes with a **delta of 0.50–0.70** (near-the-money to ITM).
- Sell a strike $1–$2 further OTM to reduce cost.
- Example: If stock is at $100 and you're bullish, buy the $99 call and sell the $101 call (for a $2-wide bull call spread).

## 5. Liquidity Filters

These filters ensure that entries and exits occur at fair prices with minimal slippage:

| Filter | Minimum Threshold | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Open Interest (per strike)** | ≥ 100 contracts | Ensures an active secondary market. Higher OI = tighter spreads and easier fills. (Wikipedia's Open Interest article confirms: *"When options have large open interest, they have a large number of buyers and sellers… the larger the open interest, the easier it will be to trade that option at a reasonable spread."*) |
| **Daily Option Volume** | ≥ 50 contracts on the specific strike | Confirms intraday liquidity |
| **Bid-Ask Spread** | ≤ $0.10 for options priced under $2.00; ≤ $0.20 for options priced $2.00–$5.00 | Limits slippage to ≤ 5–10% of premium |
| **Bid Price** | > $0.05 | Avoids options so cheap that commissions eat all returns |
| **Volume-to-OI Ratio** | Ideally > 0.5 | Confirms active participation, not stale positions |

**Execution rule:** Always use **limit orders** at the mid-point of the bid-ask spread, or $0.01–$0.05 better than the natural price. Never use market orders on options.

### Example Trade 1: Bull Call Debit Spread on "TechCo" (Ticker: TC)

**Setup:**
- TC is trading at **$52.00**
- Technicals: RSI(14) = 38 (near oversold); MACD just crossed bullish; price bouncing off support at $51.50; volume 150% of 20-day average.
- IV Rank: 25% (moderate — favors debit strategies)

**Trade Structure:**
| Component | Detail |
|---|---|
| Strategy | Bull Call Debit Spread |
| Buy | 1 × TC $51 Call (13 DTE) @ $2.10 |
| Sell | 1 × TC $53 Call (13 DTE) @ $0.95 |
| Net Debit | **$1.15 per share = $115 total** |
| Strike Width | $2.00 ($200 max value) |
| Max Loss (Net Debit Paid) | **$115** |
| Max Profit | $200 − $115 = **$85** |
| Breakeven at Expiry | $51.00 + $1.15 = **$52.15** |
| Liquidity Check | OI: 850 / Volume: 320 / Bid-Ask: $1.10–$1.20 ✅ |

> ⚠️ **Position-size note:** A $115 max loss exceeds the strict $49 risk cap for a $700 account. To comply, the trader could: (a) use a $0.50-wide spread instead, or (b) accept that this example stretches the framework. For illustration, we proceed with the $2-wide spread.

**P/L Scenarios at Expiry (13 days later):**

| TC Price at Expiry | Spread Value | Net P/L | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| $55.00 (strong rally) | $2.00 ($200) | **+$85** (74% return on risk) | Max profit achieved |
| $53.50 (moderate rally) | $2.00 ($200) | **+$85** | Max profit (capped at $53) |
| $53.00 (at short strike) | $2.00 ($200) | **+$85** | Max profit |
| $52.50 (slight rally) | $1.50 ($150) | **+$35** | Partial profit |
| $52.15 (breakeven) | $1.15 ($115) | **$0** | Breakeven |
| $51.00 (at long strike) | $0 ($0) | **−$115** | Max loss |
| $49.00 (decline) | $0 ($0) | **−$115** | Max loss |

**Exit Management:** Set a profit target at 50% of max profit (~$43 gain, close spread when it reaches ~$1.58 debit value). Set a stop-loss at 50% of max loss (close if spread value drops to ~$0.58, locking in ~$57 loss).

## 7. Win-Rate & Probability Analysis for $200–300/Week Target

This is the most sobering part of the framework. Let us model what is mathematically required.

### Scenario: Using $1-Wide Credit Spreads (Example 2 style)

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Average credit per trade | $0.35/share = $35/trade |
| Average risk per trade | $65/trade (max loss) |
| Target profit per trade | $25 (take profit at ~70% of max credit) |
| Average loss per trade (stopped out) | $35 (stop at 50% of max risk) |
| Trades per week | 5 |

**Expected value per trade:** EV = (Win Rate × $25) − (Loss Rate × $35)

To achieve **$200/week with 5 trades:**

| Required Win Rate | Calculation | Weekly EV |
|---|---|---|
| 60% | (0.60 × $25 × 5) − (0.40 × $35 × 5) = $75 − $70 = **$5** | Insufficient |
| 70% | (0.70 × $25 × 5) − (0.30 × $35 × 5) = $87.50 − $52.50 = **$35** | Insufficient |
| 80% | (0.80 × $25 × 5) − (0.20 × $35 × 5) = $100 − $35 = **$65** | Insufficient |
| 90% | (0.90 × $25 × 5) − (0.10 × $35 × 5) = $112.50 − $17.50 = **$95** | Insufficient |
| 95% | (0.95 × $25 × 5) − (0.05 × $35 × 5) = $118.75 − $8.75 = **$110** | Insufficient |

**This shows that with $1-wide credit spreads on a $700 account, the $200–300/week target is mathematically unreachable** at any realistic win rate, because the per-trade dollar payoff is simply too small.

### Scenario: Using $2-Wide Debit Spreads (Example 1 style)

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Average debit per trade | $115/trade |
| Average max profit | $85/trade |
| Target profit per trade (50% of max) | ~$43 |
| Average loss per trade (50% stop) | ~$57 |
| Trades per week | 3 (fewer trades due to larger risk) |

To achieve **$200/week with 3 trades:**

| Required Win Rate | Calculation | Weekly EV |
|---|---|---|
| 60% | (0.60 × $43 × 3) − (0.40 × $57 × 3) = $77.40 − $68.40 = **$9** | Insufficient |
| 70% | (0.70 × $43 × 3) − (0.30 × $57 × 3) = $90.30 − $51.30 = **$39** | Insufficient |
| 80% | (0.80 × $43 × 3) − (0.20 × $57 × 3) = $103.20 − $34.20 = **$69** | Insufficient |
| 90% | (0.90 × $43 × 3) − (0.10 × $57 × 3) = $116.10 − $17.10 = **$99** | Insufficient |
| 95% | (0.95 × $43 × 3) − (0.05 × $57 × 3) = $122.55 − $8.55 = **$114** | Insufficient |

**Even with a 95% win rate (which is extraordinary and unrealistic over time), this framework cannot generate $200–300/week on a $700 account while following prudent position-sizing rules.**

### Required Math to Hit $250/Week

Working backwards from a $250 weekly target using the credit spread approach:

- If average profit = $25/trade and average loss = $35/trade at 75% win rate:
  - Weekly EV per trade = (0.75 × $25) − (0.25 × $35) = $10/trade
  - **Trades needed: 25/week** (5/day) to reach $250
  - But at $65 risk/trade × 25 trades = $1,625 total risk exposure on a $700 account — **impossible without extreme overleveraging.**

- To reach $250/week with 5 trades:
  - Required: $50 average net profit per trade
  - With a 70% win rate and 1:1 reward/risk: ($0.70 × $W) − ($0.30 × $L) = $50
  - If W = L = $100: $70 − $30 = $40/trade. Still short.
  - If risking $200/trade with W=$200, L=$200: $140 − $60 = $80/trade × 5 = $400/week. But risking $200/trade on a $700 account = 28.6% risk per trade — **reckless by any standard.**

### Honest Conclusion on the Return Target

| Metric | Reality Check |
|---|---|
| $200–300/week on $700 | **29–43% weekly return** |
| Annualized equivalent | **1,500–2,200%+ per year** |
| Sustainable? | **No.** No established risk-management framework supports this. |
| What *is* realistic? | A skilled options trader might target **2–5% per week** ($14–$35/week on $700), and even that is ambitious with significant drawdown risk |

## Uncertainty & Gaps

- **The $200–300/week target is mathematically incompatible** with prudent position sizing on a $700 account. The framework honestly shows this; any trader claiming otherwise is likely overleveraging, omitting losses, or misrepresenting results.
- **Commissions and fees** were excluded from the examples but would further erode returns, especially on $1-wide spreads where a $0.65/contract commission represents ~2% of the spread's notional value.
- **Slippage in fast markets** can widen effective spreads beyond the $0.10 filter, particularly during volatile opens.
- **Tax implications** (short-term capital gains on options held < 1 year) would reduce net returns further.
- The win-rate and probability modeling above assumes independent, identically distributed trades — real trading has serial correlation (trending markets, regime changes) that makes actual outcomes more volatile than the model suggests.
