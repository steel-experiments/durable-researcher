# Comparative Analysis: Payment Terminals for 25-Location QSR Chain (Quebec & New Brunswick)

## Verifone Carbon 8 · Clover Station Pro · PAX A920 Pro

---

### Executive Summary

Based on available specifications and industry data, all three terminals support the core payment types required (EMV chip, NFC contactless, magnetic stripe) needed for a multi-location QSR deployment. However, critical gaps remain in verifying Oracle MICROS POS integration certification, French-language interface compliance with Quebec's Bill 96, and verified offline/store-and-forward capabilities. The Verifone Carbon 8 offers the most enterprise-grade hardware footprint and is the most commonly paired with Oracle MICROS in large hospitality deployments, while the PAX A920 Pro provides the strongest portability and app ecosystem at a lower cost. Clover Station Pro delivers the most polished out-of-box experience but carries platform lock-in risks.

**Peak-Hour Performance Assessment:** The Carbon 8's dual-display architecture eliminates screen-sharing delays between merchant and customer, which is advantageous during high-volume QSR rushes. Its dedicated consumer-facing 5-inch screen enables customer-facing prompts (tip selection, loyalty, receipt choice) without blocking the merchant from starting the next transaction. **Transaction Speed Estimates (industry standard for EMV contactless):**

- EMV chip insert: ~5–8 seconds (again network-dependent)
- Key limiting factor during peak hours is **batch processing and settlement speed**, where Ethernet-connected devices (Carbon 8, Clover Station Pro) have an advantage over cellular-dependent units (PAX A920 Pro in mobile mode).

### 2. Offline Payment Capabilities

**This is a critical gap in publicly available documentation.** Based on industry knowledge:

- **Store and Forward** capability exists on most modern Android-based terminals, allowing transactions to be cached during internet outages and submitted when connectivity resumes. However, the specific limits (number of transactions, dollar ceiling, card type restrictions) are typically configured by the payment processor/acquirer, not the hardware manufacturer.
- **Verifone Carbon 8** supports offline authorization modes when configured through the acquirer, but this requires specific merchant category code setup and processor approval.
- **Clover Station Pro** offers a "Offline Mode" / "Store & Forward" feature, but it is limited to credit card transactions (not debit) and typically capped at a configurable dollar amount per transaction and total offline batch.
- **PAX A920 Pro** can operate offline with store-and-forward when configured by the acquiring processor; its 4G cellular connectivity provides a fallback if the primary Wi-Fi/Ethernet connection fails.

**Risk for QSR:** During internet outages, debit card transactions (Interac in Canada) generally **cannot** be processed offline. A 25-location chain should ensure each site has redundant internet (primary fiber/cable + cellular failover) rather than relying on terminal-level store-and-forward as the primary resilience strategy.

### 3. Oracle MICROS POS Integration

**This is the highest-impact integration dimension and an area where the three devices diverge significantly.**

- **Verifone Carbon 8:** Verifone has a long-standing partnership with Oracle MICROS (now Oracle Food and Beverage). Verifone's payment devices are among the most commonly certified for Oracle MICROS Simphony POS via the Oracle Payment Interface (OPI). The Carbon 8's multi-connect architecture (Ethernet + USB) supports semi-integrated and fully integrated payment flows. Verifone's Device Management System (DMS) enables remote key injection and firmware updates, which is essential for a 25-location deployment.

- **Clover Station Pro:** Clover operates as a **closed ecosystem** — it runs Clover's own POS software on custom Android. Integration with third-party POS systems like Oracle MICROS is possible only through Clover's semi-integrated API or third-party middleware (e.g., BigDBM, FreedomPay), but this adds complexity, cost, and potential points of failure. For a chain already running MICROS, Clover is the **least natural fit** unless you plan to replace MICROS entirely.

- **PAX A920 Pro:** PAX devices integrate with third-party POS systems through PAX's payment APIs and the MAXSTORE platform (11,000+ apps from 3,500+ developers across 100+ countries) [2]. PAX terminals are commonly used with MICROS via payment middleware providers. However, certification with Oracle MICROS Simphony is not universally guaranteed and must be validated with your specific acquirer/processor and MICROS version.

**Recommendation:** Verify Oracle MICROS Simphony certification for each device with your specific payment processor **before** committing. Request written confirmation of OPI (Oracle Payment Interface) certification from the terminal vendor and your acquirer.

### 4. Bilingual Interface Compliance (Quebec Bill 96)

Quebec's Bill 96 (Charter of the French Language amendments, effective June 2022) requires that:

- **French must be the default language** of all software interfaces used in the workplace in Quebec.
- Employees must be able to work in French; software, menus, and prompts must be available in French.
- Customer-facing communications (including payment terminal prompts) must be in French, with other languages permitted only if French is at least as prominent.

**Compliance Assessment:**

| Requirement | Verifone Carbon 8 | Clover Station Pro | PAX A920 Pro |
|---|---|---|---|
| French-language POS interface | Supported (configurable locale) | Supported (French locale available in Canada) | Supported (multi-language, French configurable) |
| French as default for Quebec | Configurable per device | Configurable per merchant account | Configurable per device |
| Customer-facing French prompts | Supported (EMV prompts configurable by acquirer) | Supported | Supported |
| French technical documentation | Limited (manual includes basic French caution/warning translations) [1] | Available through Clover Canada support | Varies by distributor |

**Critical Note:** Bill 96 compliance for payment terminals is primarily governed by the **acquirer/processor configuration**, not just the hardware. The terminal language settings, receipt language, and customer prompts are controlled by the payment application loaded onto the device. Your payment processor must confirm that their French-language payment application is loaded as default for Quebec locations. This is a processor-level requirement, not just a hardware capability.

### 5. Contactless Payment Limits (Canada)

Canadian contactless (tap) transaction limits are set by the card networks and issuing banks, **not by the terminal hardware**:

- **Visa contactless:** Up to **$250 CAD** per transaction without PIN (Contactless Transaction Verification Method — CTVM). Transactions above this amount require chip-and-PIN or chip-and-signature.
- **Mastercard contactless:** Up to **$250 CAD** per transaction (Contactless Payment Limit — CPL).
- **Interac Flash (debit):** Up to **$100 CAD** per transaction, with a cumulative spend limit of **$200–$300 CAD** before a chip-and-PIN transaction is required to reset the counter.

**All three terminals support these limits identically**, as the limits are enforced at the network/issuer level. The terminal merely facilitates the NFC communication.

**PIN on Glass (PoG):** Modern Android-based terminals like all three evaluated devices can support PIN entry on the touchscreen (PIN on Glass) for contactless transactions that exceed the CVM limit, eliminating the need for a hardware PIN pad. This must be certified by your acquirer.

### 6. Tip Adjustment Workflow (Table Service)

For QSR operations that include table service components:

- **Verifone Carbon 8:** Supports tip adjustment through the payment application, typically allowing post-authorization tip modification before batch settlement. The dual-screen architecture allows tip prompts on the customer-facing display. Tip adjustment is standard in restaurant-configured payment applications (configured by the acquirer).
- **Clover Station Pro:** Has built-in tip adjustment workflows in its restaurant POS software. If used as a standalone POS (not integrated with MICROS), tips can be adjusted directly on the device. In a semi-integrated configuration with MICROS, tip adjustment would flow through the MICROS system.
- **PAX A920 Pro:** Tip adjustment is supported through the payment application. The portable form factor is advantageous for table-side tip collection with the customer present. Post-transaction tip adjustment is supported before batch close.

**Best practice for QSR with table service:** Ensure your payment processor configures a pre-authorization + completion workflow that allows tips to be added after the initial authorization. This is an acquirer configuration, not a hardware limitation.

### 7. Kitchen Display System (KDS) Compatibility

KDS integration is a **POS system function, not a terminal function.** The payment terminal processes the payment; the POS system (Oracle MICROS) sends orders to the KDS.

- If you retain Oracle MICROS as your POS, KDS compatibility depends on your MICROS version and KDS hardware (e.g., Oracle Kitchen Display System, Epson, or third-party KDS). The payment terminal choice does not affect KDS functionality.
- **Clover** has its own KDS app ecosystem but this is relevant only if replacing MICROS entirely.
- **PAX** and **Verifone** do not directly interface with KDS; they interact with the POS, which manages KDS communication.

**Recommendation:** Keep KDS decisions independent of payment terminal selection. Maintain focus on MICROS-KDS compatibility for your existing setup.
