# Evaluating Modern Text-to-Speech (TTS) Models: A Comprehensive Research Report (Up to 2025)

## Executive Summary

Evaluating modern TTS systems has become fundamentally more complex as 2024–2025 models achieve speech quality indistinguishable from human recordings. The field has converged on a multi-axis evaluation paradigm spanning intelligibility (WER/CER), speaker fidelity (SECS, SIM), prosodic and emotional expressiveness (SER accuracy, intensity scaling), and distributional naturalness (TTSDS2). Emerging tasks—zero-shot voice cloning, cross-lingual voice transfer, and emotion-conditioned synthesis—require dedicated benchmarks and novel metrics that go far beyond traditional Mean Opinion Score (MOS) ratings.

---

## 1. The Evaluation Landscape: Why Traditional Metrics Fall Short

Mean Opinion Score (MOS) has long been the gold standard for subjective speech quality assessment. However, Minixhofer et al. (2025) demonstrate in **TTSDS2**—presented at ICLR 2026—that MOS suffers from poor cross-study comparability and instability when evaluating near-human or super-human synthetic speech. Standard objective metrics like PESQ and MCD were designed for older, lower-quality systems and fail to reliably differentiate between state-of-the-art neural TTS models. Among 16 evaluated metrics, TTSDS2 was the only one achieving Spearman correlations above 0.50 across every domain tested (clean, noisy, spontaneous, and child speech), with an average correlation of approximately 0.67 against 11,000+ subjective human ratings across 14 languages [1][2].

This failure of legacy metrics has driven the community toward **multi-dimensional evaluation frameworks** that assess speech along several orthogonal perceptual axes simultaneously.

---

## 2. Core Evaluation Dimensions and Metrics

### 2.1 Intelligibility and Content Accuracy

**Word Error Rate (WER)** and **Character Error Rate (CER)** remain foundational. They measure how faithfully the synthesized speech conveys the intended text by running ASR (typically Whisper or wav2vec 2.0) on generated audio and comparing against the input transcript. Current competitive benchmarks on Seed-TTS-Eval show:

| Model | CER | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| MiniMax (closed-source) | 0.83 | Commercial leader |
| GLM-TTS (open-source) | 0.89 | Lowest open-source CER via RL optimization |
| VoxCPM | 0.93 | Competitive open-source |
| CosyVoice2 | 1.38 | Moderate |
| F5-TTS | 1.53 | Higher CER despite strong cloning |

GLM-TTS reduced its CER from 1.03 to 0.89 (a 13.6% improvement) by using CER directly as a reward signal during multi-reward reinforcement learning training [3].

### 2.2 Speaker Similarity and Voice Cloning Fidelity

For zero-shot voice cloning—where a model replicates a voice from a single short audio prompt (3–10 seconds) without fine-tuning—the primary metrics are:

- **Speaker Embedding Cosine Similarity (SECS / SIM)**: Extracts high-dimensional speaker representations (using models like CamPlus ONNX, WeSpeaker, or d-vectors) and computes cosine similarity between reference and generated embeddings. Scores on Seed-TTS-Eval cluster tightly among leaders: Seed-TTS (79.6), MiniMax (78.3), VoxCPM (77.2), GLM-TTS (76.4), F5-TTS (76.0), CosyVoice2 (75.7) [3][4].

- **Equal Error Rate (EER)**: Borrowed from speaker verification and anti-spoofing, EER identifies the threshold where false acceptance equals false rejection, quantifying how indistinguishable a clone is from the genuine speaker. This metric bridges TTS evaluation with deepfake detection concerns [4].

### 2.3 Naturalness and Distributional Quality

**TTSDS2** represents the current state of the art in objective naturalness evaluation. It computes the 2-Wasserstein distance between synthetic and real speech distributions across five perceptual sub-scores:

1. **Generic acoustic properties** — via SSL representations (wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT)
2. **Speaker identity** — via d-vectors and WeSpeaker embeddings
3. **Prosody** — via F0 contours, token durations, and SSL-based prosody vectors
4. **Intelligibility** — via WER from ASR backends
5. **Environment** — via SNR and PESQ

A composite score above 50 indicates synthetic speech is closer to real human speech than to noise. The distribution-based approach captures the inherent one-to-many variability of speech (multiple valid realizations of the same text), avoiding the instability of point-estimate MOS predictors [1][2].

### 2.4 Prosody and Emotion Evaluation

Evaluating emotional expressiveness requires specialized approaches beyond naturalness:

- **Emotion Classification Accuracy**: A pre-trained Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) model (e.g., wav2vec2-SER) predicts categorical emotion labels from synthesized speech, verifying that the intended emotion is acoustically present [5].

- **Emotion Similarity (Cosine)**: Measures how closely synthesized emotion embeddings match reference emotional speech in latent space, using clustering and contrastive distance metrics to verify inter-class separation [5].

- **Intensity Scaling (α)**: The **EMOD** framework introduces a continuous intensity parameter α ∈ [0,1] derived from pitch deviation (ΔF0), energy deviation (ΔE), and duration deviation (ΔD). Human MOS evaluations confirm that varying α produces perceptually linear, monotonic increases in emotional strength without distorting linguistic content or corrupting speaker timbre [5].

- **MOS for Emotional Speech**: Dedicated subjective evaluation rates samples on 1–5 scales for perceptual naturalness, speaker similarity, and emotional expressiveness simultaneously, ensuring emotional enhancement doesn't degrade base quality [5].

### 2.5 Key Benchmark Datasets

| Benchmark | Focus | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Seed-TTS-Eval** | Zero-shot cloning (SIM, CER) | Standard benchmark comparing open/closed-source models |
| **CV3-Eval** | Zero-shot against authentic references | Designed specifically for ZS-TTS evaluation |
| **Deepfake-Eval-2024** | Real-world robustness | 56.5 hours of social media deepfakes |
| **TTSDS2 Benchmark** | Multi-domain naturalness | 11,000+ subjective ratings, 14 languages, 20+ TTS systems |

## 3. Architectural Innovations Enabling New Evaluation Paradigms

The diversity of modern TTS architectures directly shapes what and how we evaluate:

**Flow Matching Models (F5-TTS):** Non-autoregressive diffusion transformers that pad text with filler tokens to match speech length. Evaluation focuses on cloning fidelity (high SIM) and intelligibility (WER), with F5-TTS excelling at multi-speaker generation and voice cloning accuracy [6].

**Codec Language Models (SparkTTS):** Use "BiCodec" to decompose speech into semantic tokens (content) and global tokens (speaker attributes), combined with LLM reasoning for prosody control. Evaluation must assess coarse and fine-grained prosody manipulation (gender, style, pitch, rate), though practical evaluation reveals challenges with accent injection and latency [6].

**Multimodal Conversational Models (Sesame CSM):** Dual-transformer architecture using residual vector quantization. CSM demands new evaluation dimensions: quality of non-verbal vocal cues (laughter, pauses, tone shifts), conversational naturalness, and real-time latency—areas where traditional metrics are inadequate [6].

**RL-Optimized Models (GLM-TTS):** Multi-reward reinforcement learning (GRPO algorithm) simultaneously optimizes sound quality, emotional expression, and pronunciation. Evaluation must track trade-offs across these competing objectives. GLM-TTS uses separate reward functions for each dimension during training, making the evaluation inherently multi-objective [3].

## 4. Emerging Evaluation Tasks

### 4.1 Zero-Shot Voice Cloning

The central challenge is evaluating cloning from a single 3–10 second prompt without fine-tuning. The consensus metric suite is: SIM/SECS for timbre preservation, CER for content integrity, and MOS for holistic quality. The industry is pushing toward **real-world robustness** evaluation—testing with noisy prompts, varied recording conditions, and accented speech [4].

### 4.2 Cross-Lingual Voice Transfer

Evaluation must verify that speaker identity transfers across language boundaries. TTSDS2 explicitly addresses this by evaluating speaker identity sub-scores across languages using language-agnostic speaker embeddings. The EMOD framework creates a shared emotion space across 9 languages (Tamil, Malayalam, Hindi, English, Kannada, Telugu, Assamese, Marathi, Punjabi) using only 10–20 hours of speech per language, with orthogonality constraints preventing speaker identity leakage across language boundaries [2][5].

### 4.3 Emotion and Prosody Transfer

The **EMOD** framework represents best practices: a 192-dimensional feature representation integrating F0, energy, phoneme duration, mel-spectrograms, and HuBERT embeddings, projected into a 256-dimensional emotion embedding disentangled from speaker identity via adversarial training. Evaluation combines objective SER classification accuracy with subjective MOS for naturalness under varying emotional intensities [5].

---

## 5. Current Gaps and Open Challenges

- **No universal benchmark for emotion/prosody**: While TTSDS2 captures prosody as one sub-score, there is no widely adopted standardized benchmark specifically for emotional expressiveness comparable to Seed-TTS-Eval for cloning.

- **Cross-lingual evaluation is underdeveloped**: Most benchmarks prioritize English and Chinese. TTSDS2 covers 14 languages, but evaluation of low-resource language synthesis quality remains sparse.

- **Conversational speech evaluation**: Models like Sesame CSM that synthesize non-verbal cues (laughter, hesitations, backchannels) lack standardized metrics. MOS is insufficient for capturing conversational naturalness.

- **Computational cost of objective metrics**: TTSDS2's distribution-based approach is computationally expensive, limiting adoption for rapid model iteration.

- **Safety and ethical evaluation**: Voice cloning benchmarks like Deepfake-Eval-2024 are emerging, but standardized evaluation of misuse potential, consent verification, and watermark detectability remains nascent.

## Uncertainty & Gaps

- **Emotion/prosody benchmarks**: Multiple searches for standardized emotional TTS evaluation benchmarks returned limited results. The field appears to rely on task-specific MOS studies rather than a unified benchmark, suggesting this is a genuine gap rather than a search limitation.

- **Cross-lingual transfer metrics**: Specific quantitative results for cross-lingual voice transfer (e.g., SIM scores when transferring a Chinese speaker's voice to English) were not found in browsed sources. TTSDS2 addresses this in principle but detailed cross-lingual benchmarking data is sparse.

- **Proprietary model evaluations**: Many closed-source systems (ElevenLabs, Google, Azure) publish selective metrics that are difficult to independently verify. The benchmarks above primarily compare models with publicly available code.

- **SpeechSim and similar neural metrics**: Initial searches for SpeechSim and newer neural speech similarity metrics returned no results, suggesting either these tools are not yet widely adopted or are known under different names in the evaluation community.
