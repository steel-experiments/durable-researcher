# Business Model Analysis: Instacart (CART) vs. DoorDash (DASH)
## A Literature-Based Comparative Review

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Instacart (CART) and DoorDash (DASH) are the two dominant platforms digitizing local commerce in North America, yet they operate fundamentally different business models. DoorDash is a logistics-heavy, scale-driven platform commanding 67% of US restaurant delivery with $10.7B in 2024 revenue. Instacart is an asset-light grocery technology company generating $3.4B in revenue with gross margins nearly double DoorDash's (75% vs. 48%). While DoorDash dominates in scale and growth velocity, Instacart holds the structurally superior business model—its high-margin advertising revenue, capital-light fulfillment, and entrenched grocery partnerships produce profitability metrics that DoorDash cannot match at the margin level. **The better investment depends on the investor's time horizon and thesis: margin-focused investors should prefer CART; growth-at-scale investors should prefer DASH.**

## 2. Business Model Comparison

### 2.1 Instacart (CART): The Asset-Light Grocery Technology Platform

Instacart operates a **three-sided marketplace** connecting consumers, retail partners (1,500+ banners across 85,000+ stores), and approximately 600,000 independent shoppers who pick, pack, and deliver orders [1][3]. Its revenue model has three pillars:

| Revenue Stream | Description | Margin Profile |
|---|---|---|
| **Marketplace Transaction Fees** | Commissions on orders fulfilled through the platform | Moderate |
| **Retail Media / Advertising** | CPG brands pay for sponsored placements at point-of-purchase | **70–90% gross margin** |
| **Enterprise Technology (Platform)** | White-label e-commerce, fulfillment tools, and analytics for retailers | High (software-like) |

The critical differentiator is **Instacart Ads**, which monetizes first-party grocery purchase data by connecting consumer packaged goods (CPG) brands directly to consumers. This advertising segment generates gross margins of 70–90% and is Instacart's fastest-growing revenue stream [1][4]. Because Instacart leverages existing retailer infrastructure for picking rather than operating its own fulfillment centers, the model is inherently capital-light.

### 2.2 DoorDash (DASH): The Local Commerce Logistics Engine

DoorDash operates a **localized three-sided logistics marketplace** connecting consumers, merchants (restaurants, grocery stores, convenience retailers), and dashers (delivery drivers). Its revenue comes from:

| Revenue Stream | Description | Margin Profile |
|---|---|---|
| **Merchant Commissions** | Percentage of order value from restaurant/retail partners | Lower (shared with logistics) |
| **Consumer Fees** | Delivery fees, service fees, small order fees | Moderate |
| **DashPass Subscriptions** | Monthly subscription for reduced delivery fees | Recurring/deferred |
| **Emerging Advertising** | Sponsored placements for merchants (early stage) | Growing, high-margin |

DoorDash's model is fundamentally **logistics-intensive**: it manages a massive fleet of independent contractors, complex last-mile routing, and dense urban delivery networks. Its competitive advantage lies in **scale economies**—with 67% US restaurant delivery market share, it achieves denser routing, lower per-order delivery costs, and higher driver utilization [2][5].

### 2.3 Convergence: Entering Each Other's Territory

Both companies are converging. DoorDash has expanded aggressively into grocery delivery, convenience, alcohol, and retail categories. Instacart has moved into ready-to-eat meals, broader retail, and enterprise technology services. This convergence intensifies competition but also validates the size of the combined opportunity.

## 3. Drivers Behind the Margin Gap

The gross margin gap between the two companies is enormous and structural:

| Metric | Instacart (CART) | DoorDash (DASH) | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gross Margin (FY2024)** | ~75.3% | 48.3% | **+27 pp** |
| **EBITDA Margin (FY2024)** | 16.5% | 5.4% | **+11 pp** |
| **Net Income Margin (FY2024)** | 13.5% | 1.1% | **+12.4 pp** |
| **FCF Margin (FY2024)** | ~18.4% | 18.9% | ~Flat |

*Sources: [4][6][7]*

Four structural drivers explain this gap:

**1. Revenue Composition — Advertising vs. Logistics**
Instacart's fastest-growing segment is retail media advertising, which scales at near-100% marginal profit. Every additional ad dollar flows almost entirely to the bottom line. DoorDash, by contrast, derives the bulk of revenue from delivery fees and commissions that are tied to variable logistics costs (driver payouts, mileage, insurance). DoorDash's nascent advertising business is growing but years behind Instacart's entrenched position [1][4][5].

**2. Fulfillment Architecture — Retailer Infrastructure vs. Proprietary Network**
Instacart utilizes existing brick-and-mortar store estates for order picking. It does not operate dark stores or autonomous fleets. DoorDash must build and maintain its own last-mile delivery infrastructure. This gives Instacart a fundamentally lower capital intensity ratio [1][3].

**3. Labor Cost Structure**
DoorDash faces direct exposure to courier wage inflation, regulatory minimum-pay requirements, and labor organizing. These costs represent a structural drag estimated at -2.1% on industry CAGR growth [1]. Instacart's shopper model, while also using independent contractors, benefits from the fact that picking occurs within existing retail labor ecosystems and is supplemented by customer fee structures.

**4. Average Order Value (AOV) vs. Frequency**
Instacart benefits from higher AOV inherent in grocery shopping ($50–100+ per order) compared to restaurant delivery ($25–40). Higher AOV spreads fixed platform costs over larger transactions, improving unit economics. DoorDash compensates with higher order frequency, particularly in urban markets, which drives revenue growth but not necessarily margin expansion [2][5].

**Notably, FCF margins are roughly comparable (~18–19%)**, because DoorDash's negative working capital dynamics (it collects from consumers before paying merchants/dashers) generate substantial cash flow despite lower profitability.

## 6. Investment Recommendation: The Elevator Pitch

### **Recommendation: Instacart (CART) for Margin Investors; DoorDash (DASH) for Growth Investors — But CART Is the Superior Risk-Adjusted Opportunity**

**Instacart (CART)** offers the stronger *business model* and is the better risk-adjusted investment. Here's why:

- **Superior unit economics:** A 75% gross margin vs. 48% is not a rounding error—it reflects a structurally different, software-like business model insulated from logistics cost inflation.
- **High-margin advertising flywheel:** Instacart's retail media platform is essentially a digital advertising company embedded inside a grocery marketplace. This is the highest-ROIC segment of either company and is years ahead of DoorDash's nascent ad efforts.
- **Capital-light moat:** By leveraging existing retailer infrastructure rather than building proprietary fulfillment, Instacart avoids the capital-intensive arms race of last-mile logistics.
- **Profitability already achieved:** $457M net income (13.5% margin) and $623M FCF in 2024. DoorDash achieved its first profitable year at just $123M net income (1.1% margin).
- **Massive TAM with minimal penetration:** At only ~2.3% of the US online grocery market, the growth runway is enormous.

**DoorDash (DASH)** is the better choice only if you prioritize:
- **Revenue growth velocity** (24% vs. 11% YoY)
- **Absolute scale and market dominance** (67% share of restaurant delivery)
- **Platform breadth** spanning multiple verticals beyond food

However, DoorDash's path to sustained high profitability remains uncertain. Its business model requires continuous investment in logistics infrastructure, and margin expansion depends on leveraging density efficiencies that may plateau as it pushes into lower-density suburban and rural markets.

**The bottom line:** If forced to choose one, **Instacart (CART) is the superior investment**. It offers the rare combination of a massive underpenetrated TAM, best-in-class margins, a growing high-margin advertising business, proven profitability, and an asset-light model that scales elegantly. DoorDash is a great company, but it operates a harder business.
