# Top Computational Social Science Labs in the US & Industry Career Paths

## Executive Summary

This report identifies specific computational social science (CSS) labs and research groups with named PIs across leading US universities, develops proxy metrics for evaluating undergraduate and graduate research output, and analyzes the top-paying industry roles for computational social scientists along with the educational backgrounds of the people who hold them. The findings reveal a field where formal CSS lab structures are still maturing, where graduate-level training is the dominant pathway into top industry roles, and where reliable metrics for undergrad research impact remain largely absent.

## Part I: Top Computational Social Science Labs in the US

### 1. Penn Computational Social Science Lab (CSSLab)
- **PI:** Duncan J. Watts (Professor, Annenberg School & SEAS, University of Pennsylvania)
- **Website:** css.seas.upenn.edu
- **Focus:** Reconciling competing theories of individual and collective human behavior through computational methods. Watts is one of the most cited network scientists in the world (author of *Six Degrees* and *Everything Is Obvious*), making this lab arguably the most prominent single-PI CSS lab in the US.
- **Graduate Impact Proxy:** Watts's former trainees have placed at Microsoft Research, Yahoo Research, and major business schools. His lab's alumni network in both academia and industry is exceptionally strong.
- **Undergraduate Impact Proxy:** Penn's structured undergrad research programs (through SEAS and Annenberg) provide formal pipelines into CSSLab, though specific publication counts for undergrads are not publicly listed.

### 2. Columbia University Data Science Institute — CSS Working Group
- **PIs:** Andrew Gelman (Statistics/Political Science), David Blei (Statistics/CS), Peter Bearman (Sociology), Kathleen McKeown (CS/DSI Director), Tim Roughgarden (CS), Suresh Naidu (Economics/International Affairs), Julia Hirschberg (CS), Chris Wiggins (Applied Mathematics)
- **Focus:** Applies ML, NLP, speech/image recognition, and causal inference to social science questions. Creates tools and interfaces to make computational methods accessible to domain experts.
- **Graduate Impact Proxy:** Columbia's DSI PhD program is one of the earliest and most established data science programs in the US. Gelman's group alone has produced an extraordinary number of PhDs now in faculty positions and senior industry roles. Blei's former students populate top ML departments. Wiggins co-founded the Harvard Data Science Initiative and served as Chief Data Scientist at the New York Times.
- **Undergraduate Impact Proxy:** Columbia's formal research programs through DSI provide undergrad research opportunities, but specific output metrics are not published.

### 3. University of Michigan School of Information (UMSI) — CSS Cluster
- **PIs:** Eytan Adar (not on browsed page but well-known), Eric Gilbert (Info/EECS), David Jurgens (Info/EECS), Daniel Romero (Info/EECS/Complex Systems), Paul Resnick (Info), Paramveer Dhillon (Info), Dallas Card (Info), Justine Zhang (Info), Misha Teplitskiy (Info), Grant Schoenebeck (Info), Matthew Bui (Info/Digital Studies), Abigail Jacobs (Info/Complex Systems), VG Vinod Vydiswaran (Info/Medical School)
- **Focus:** Broad coverage including NLP, social network analysis, algorithmic auditing, computational journalism, health informatics, and science of science.
- **Graduate Impact Proxy:** Michigan's School of Information has one of the largest concentrations of CSS faculty in the US. Gilbert's and Jurgens's groups have placed PhDs at Google, Meta, and top academic positions. Resnick is one of the most cited researchers in recommender systems and social computing.
- **Undergraduate Impact Proxy:** UMSI has a formal undergraduate program in information science with research opportunities, but no published metrics on undergrad research output.

### 4. Rutgers University — Computational Social Science Lab
- **PIs:** Tawfiq Ammari, Sophia Fu, Kiran Garimella, Shagun Jhaver, Jianing Li, Sarah Shugars, Vivek Singh, Matthew Weber
- **Focus:** Digital platforms, algorithmic audits, NLP, network analysis, and socio-technical systems.
- **Note:** Garimella is notable for prior work at Aalto University and ETH Zurich on social media and political communication before joining Rutgers. Rutgers's lab represents a growing CSS presence at public R1 universities.

### 5. Other Notable Labs (Identified but Not Fully Profiled)
- **Purdue University — CSS Lab** (Department of Political Science): Lab exists but detailed PI and output information was unavailable from browsing.
- **Stanford:** While no single named "CSS Lab" was identified through this research, Stanford has deep CSS strength through individual faculty: Matthew Gentzkow (Economics), Johannes Abeler, Susan Athey (Tech Lab), and Jure Leskovec (CS/network science). The Stanford GSB PhD program places graduates at top business schools and tech firms.
- **MIT:** The MIT Media Lab and CSAIL have relevant groups, particularly the work of Deb Roy (Laboratory for Social Machines, now retired from MIT), and currently Sinan Aral (MIT Sloan, author of *The Hype Machine*), whose group does prominent computational social science on social media.
- **Harvard:** Gary King's research group (Government Department) is one of the most influential quantitative social science groups in the world, producing foundational CSS methods (e.g., CEM, LIWC adaptations, automated text analysis). Though not branded as a "CSS lab," King's IQSS (Institute for Quantitative Social Science) functions as one.

## Part II: Developing Proxy Metrics for Research Output & Impact

Since no standardized metrics exist for undergraduate or graduate CSS research output specifically, I propose the following proxy framework, noting that full quantitative verification was limited by available data:

### For Graduate Research Output:
| Metric | Rationale | Data Availability |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| **PhD placement rate into tenure-track faculty positions** | Measures downstream academic impact | Partially available (Stanford GSB publishes placements) |
| **PhD placement into named industry research roles** | Measures industry impact | Anecdotal; the Kesari et al. (2023) article confirms Meta, Google, Pew as common destinations |
| **Number of graduates publishing in top venues (WWW, ICWSM, CHI, PNAS, Nature Human Behaviour)** | Measures research quality | Requires manual CV analysis |
| **Google Scholar citation counts of lab alumni** | Measures cumulative research impact | Feasible but labor-intensive |

### For Undergraduate Research Output:
| Metric | Rationale | Data Availability |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Co-authored publications with PI before graduation** | Direct output measure | Rarely published by labs |
| **Entry into top PhD programs** | Proxy for research preparation | Not systematically tracked |
| **Undergrad thesis awards and conference presentations** | Measures early research impact | Fragmented across institutions |

**Key Finding:** No US CSS lab publicly tracks or publishes systematic metrics on undergraduate research output. This is a significant gap in the field. The labs most likely to have strong undergrad research output are those at institutions with formal undergraduate research programs (Penn, Michigan, Columbia) and those with large lab structures that can accommodate undergrads (Michigan's UMSI cluster).

## Part III: Top-Paying Industry Jobs for Computational Social Scientists

### Job Title 1: Research Scientist (Meta, Google, Amazon, Microsoft)
- **Salary Range:** $150,000–$300,000+ total compensation (base + bonus + RSUs), with senior roles exceeding $400K. Meta's "Research Scientist, Computational Social Science" roles are among the highest-paying social science jobs in any industry.
- **Typical Educational Background:** These roles overwhelmingly require a **PhD**. The Kesari et al. (2023) article — authored by CSS professionals themselves, including Taylor Brown at Meta — confirms that "top industry positions typically require advanced graduate training" and that "professionals rarely enter straight from undergrad."
- **What they studied:** PhD programs in Political Science, Sociology, or Information Science with computational/methodological focus. Common research areas during grad school: causal inference, NLP applied to social questions, network analysis of social media, algorithmic fairness, experimental design at scale.
- **Undergrad background:** Typically quantitative social science (Economics, Political Science, Statistics) or Computer Science from top-30 universities.

### Job Title 2: Data Scientist / Senior Data Scientist (Tech & Finance)
- **Salary Range:** $120,000–$200,000 base, with total comp reaching $250K+ at FAANG and hedge funds. KDnuggets reports US national average of ~$156,790, with San Francisco averaging $178,636.
- **Educational Background:** This is the most heterogeneous category. A **Master's degree** is the most common entry point, though some enter with just a Bachelor's ($101,455 avg salary vs. $109,454 for Master's per Zippia data). However, for CSS-specific data science roles, a Master's or PhD is typical.
- **What they studied:** Master's programs in Data Science, Statistics, or Information Science. Research during grad school focuses on applied ML, predictive modeling, and increasingly LLM-based methods.

### Job Title 3: Applied Scientist (Amazon, Microsoft)
- **Salary Range:** $160,000–$350,000+ total compensation.
- **Educational Background:** Typically **PhD** required. Applied Scientists bridge research and product development.
- **What they studied:** PhD in a computational field — Information Science, Computer Science, or Computational Social Science. Research focuses on large-scale experiments, recommendation systems, or human-AI interaction.

### Job Title 4: Computational Social Scientist / Data Labs Researcher (Pew Research Center, World Bank)
- **Salary Range:** $90,000–$150,000 (lower than tech but among the highest in nonprofit/policy).
- **Educational Background:** **PhD preferred, Master's sometimes accepted.** Sono Shah at Pew Research (co-author of the Kesari et al. guide) holds a PhD.
- **What they studied:** Political Science or Sociology PhD with computational focus. Research during grad school typically involves public opinion analysis, demography, or policy evaluation using computational methods.

### Job Title 5: Economist / Quantitative Researcher (Federal Reserve, Hedge Funds)
- **Salary Range:** $130,000–$250,000+ (Federal Reserve); $200,000–$500,000+ (hedge funds/prop trading).
- **Educational Background:** **PhD in Economics** is standard. These roles leverage computational social science methods (causal inference, Bayesian modeling, time series) applied to financial or policy questions.
- **What they studied:** Economics PhD with heavy econometrics and computational methods training.

## Part IV: Undergrad vs. Graduate School Entry Analysis

### Evidence-Based Assessment (Non-Random Sample)

The question asks for a "statistically significant sample" analysis of whether CSS industry professionals enter straight from undergrad or after graduate school. No such systematic dataset exists publicly. However, triangulating across the sources reviewed:

**Finding 1: Graduate training is the dominant pathway into top CSS industry roles.**
- The Kesari et al. (2023) article — written by six CSS professionals across Meta, Pew, Code for America, Fordham, Georgetown, and CUNY — explicitly states that CSS industry roles "typically require advanced graduate training" and that "professionals rarely enter straight from undergrad."
- All six co-authors hold PhDs.
- The article frames CSS training as a "hidden curriculum" within social science PhD programs, suggesting the field's industry pipeline is primarily graduate-level.

**Finding 2: The "CSS skills gap" in undergraduate programs means most undergrads lack the combination of (a) domain expertise, (b) research design training, and (c) advanced computational methods that industry demands.**
- The Kesari et al. article recommends that PhD students build standalone "CSS portfolios" with applied projects using Python/R, SQL, Git, and ML — skills rarely developed to industry-standard levels in undergrad alone.

**Finding 3: For general data science roles (not CSS-specific), Bachelor's-only entry is more common but comes with lower salaries.**
- Zippia data shows Bachelor's-only data scientists average $101,455 vs. $109,454 for Master's holders.
- However, these are generic data science roles, not the CSS-specialized positions at Meta, Google, and Pew that command premium compensation.

**Approximate Distribution (Best Estimate):**
| Role | PhD Required? | Master's Sufficient? | Bachelor's Entry Possible? |
|------|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Research Scientist (Meta/Google) | ~90%+ | Rare | Very rare |
| Data Scientist (Tech) | ~40% | ~45% | ~15% |
| Applied Scientist (Amazon) | ~85% | ~10% | ~5% |
| CSS Researcher (Pew/World Bank) | ~70% | ~25% | ~5% |
| Quantitative Researcher (Finance) | ~80% | ~15% | ~5% |

## Uncertainty & Gaps

1. **No public metrics on undergraduate CSS research output.** No lab in the US systematically publishes data on undergraduate co-authorship rates, thesis quality, or post-graduation outcomes specific to CSS. This is a critical gap that would require CV-level analysis of individual labs to fill.

2. **"Statistically significant sample" limitation.** The question requests statistical analysis of educational backgrounds for specific industry roles. No such dataset exists publicly. LinkedIn scraping could produce this, but it falls outside this research scope. The estimates provided are based on triangulation from expert testimony (Kesari et al.) and salary/education correlation data from general data science surveys.

3. **Several top programs insufficiently profiled.** MIT (Sinan Aral's group), Harvard (Gary King's IQSS), Stanford (scattered across multiple departments), UChicago, and Northeastern (Barabási's network science group, though recently departed for Budapest) all have significant CSS activity but could not be fully profiled within the source limit. These should be investigated further.

4. **Salary data is for general data science, not CSS-specific roles.** The compensation figures cited come from data science salary surveys, not from roles specifically titled "computational social scientist." CSS-specific roles at Meta, Google, and Pew likely have different compensation distributions, but these are not publicly reported in aggregated form.

5. **Lab rankings are inherently subjective.** Without citation-count analysis, grant dollar comparisons, or alumni placement statistics, the "top" designation is based on PI prominence, institutional reputation, and visibility in the CSS community rather than quantitative ranking.
