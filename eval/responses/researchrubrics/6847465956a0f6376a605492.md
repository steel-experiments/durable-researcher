# Experimental Plan: Evaluating Vision-Language Models as Automated Labelers for Dermatological Skin Lesion Images

## 1. Objective

Determine whether a modern vision-language model (VLM) can produce diagnostically reliable labels for clinical and dermoscopic skin lesion images at a level statistically non-inferior to board-certified dermatologists. The outcome is a binary decision: the VLM is either accepted as a viable substitute annotator or rejected, with the decision grounded in pre-specified statistical criteria.

## 2. Background & Rationale

Recent VLMs—including general-purpose systems (GPT-4V, Gemini) and domain-specific variants (LLaVA-Med, BiomedCLIP)—have demonstrated strong performance on biomedical visual question-answering benchmarks. LLaVA-Med, for instance, was trained on PubMed Central figure-caption pairs using a curriculum learning strategy (vocabulary alignment → instruction-following) and outperformed prior supervised state-of-the-art models on several biomedical VQA datasets, all within 15 hours on eight A100 GPUs [1][2]. Whether this general biomedical competence transfers to the high-stakes, fine-grained task of dermatological labeling remains an open empirical question.

## 3. Datasets

| Dataset | Images | Key Properties | Role in Study |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ISIC 2018** (Task 3) | 10,015 | 7 diagnostic classes, 5 attribute classes, segmentation masks | Primary test set — multi-class diagnostic labeling |
| **ISIC 2020** | 33,126 | Binary melanoma classification, 3,308 participating teams in challenge | Scale / binary task validation |
| **ISIC 2024** | 401,059 | 3D total-body photo crops, histologically confirmed labels, smartphone-quality close-ups | Generalization probe — telehealth-grade images |
| **HAM10000** | 10,015 | Dermoscopic images, 7 diagnostic categories with histopathology ground truth | Replication / cross-benchmark check |
| **Derm7pt** | ~2,000 | Clinical and dermoscopic image pairs, 7-point checklist criteria | Attribute-level evaluation |

All images will be de-identified. Inclusion criteria: minimum resolution 600×450 pixels, adequate lighting, no heavy artifact (hair occlusion >50%, ruler obstruction). These quality filters are critical because in a non-inferiority design, poor-quality inputs bias toward false success by making both AI and human perform equally poorly [3][4].

## 4. Model Configuration

### 4.1 Candidate Models

| Model | Type | Why Include |
|---|---|---|
| **GPT-4o / GPT-4V** | Closed-source, general-purpose | State-of-the-art multimodal reasoning |
| **LLaVA-Med** | Open-source, biomedical-domain | Purpose-trained for medical image interpretation [1] |
| **BiomedCLIP + LLM** | Specialized encoder + language decoder | Strong biomedical visual representations |
| **Gemini Pro Vision** | Closed-source, general-purpose | Independent proprietary comparison |

### 4.2 Prompting Protocol

Three prompting strategies will be tested in a factorial design:

1. **Zero-shot:** Raw image + a structured prompt requesting diagnosis from the ISIC class list, a confidence score (0–100%), and no chain-of-thought.
2. **Few-shot (k=5):** Same prompt preceded by five labeled exemplar images spanning the diagnostic spectrum.
3. **Chain-of-thought (CoT):** Prompt requires the model to first describe morphological features (asymmetry, border irregularity, color variation, diameter, evolution — the ABCDE criteria), then output a diagnosis grounded in those features.

All prompts will be version-controlled and pilot-tested on a 200-image calibration set.

## 5. Human Specialist Protocol

### 5.1 Expert Panel Composition

- **Primary annotators:** ≥ 3 board-certified dermatologists per image.
- **Qualifications:** Minimum 5 years post-residency clinical experience.
- **Blinding:** Annotators work independently, blinded to each other's labels and to the source (human vs. AI) of any comparison labels.

### 5.2 Ground Truth Establishment

Ground truth for each image is set by **majority vote** of the 3+ dermatologists. For images where no majority is reached (i.e., all three disagree), a senior adjudicator (dermatopathologist with >10 years experience) provides a tie-breaking label. Images with persistent disagreement are flagged for the edge-case analysis (Section 8).

### 5.3 Human Inter-Rater Baseline

Before any VLM comparison, inter-rater reliability among the human panel is measured using **Fleiss' κ** (multi-rater) and pairwise **Cohen's κ**. This establishes the human performance ceiling and defines the M1 margin (see Section 6). Historical literature reports dermatologist inter-rater κ in the range of 0.59–0.82 depending on task difficulty and class granularity.

## 6. Statistical Framework: Non-Inferiority Design

The study is framed as a **non-inferiority (NI) trial**, following FDA 2016 guidance (ICH E9/E10) adapted for SaMD (Software as a Medical Device) evaluation [3][4].

### 6.1 Primary Endpoint

**Cohen's κ** between each annotator (human or VLM) and the ground truth consensus.

### 6.2 Non-Inferiority Margin

| Parameter | Definition | Value |
|---|---|---|
| **M1** | Historical human-to-human agreement (assay sensitivity) | κ_human–human from pilot (expected ~0.70–0.80) |
| **M2** | Maximum clinically acceptable loss below human baseline | Δ = −0.10 on the κ scale |

### 6.3 Hypotheses

- **H₀ (inferiority):** κ_AI − κ_human ≤ −0.10
- **H₁ (non-inferiority):** κ_AI − κ_human > −0.10

### 6.4 Decision Rule

Non-inferiority is declared if the **lower bound of the 95% confidence interval** for (κ_AI − κ_human) lies entirely above −0.10. The CI is computed via bootstrap (10,000 resamples) to account for the non-normal sampling distribution of κ.

### 6.5 Secondary Endpoints & Tests

| Metric | Application | Threshold for Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| **Diagnostic accuracy, sensitivity, specificity** | Per-class and macro-averaged | Sensitivity for melanoma ≥ 90% of human baseline |
| **McNemar's test** | Paired discordance analysis (AI vs. human error patterns) | No systematic directional bias (p > 0.05) |
| **TOST equivalence** | On probability calibration (Brier score) | 90% CI within ±0.05 of human Brier score |
| ** Kendall's τ** | Ordinal severity scales (e.g., benign → atypical → malignant) | τ > 0.80 |

### 6.6 Kappa Interpretation Scale (Landis & Koch, 1977 [5])

| κ Range | Agreement Level | % Reliable Data (κ²) |
|---|---|---|
| < 0.00 | Poor | 0% |
| 0.01–0.20 | Slight | 0–4% |
| 0.21–0.40 | Fair | 4–15% |
| 0.41–0.60 | Moderate | 15–35% |
| 0.61–0.80 | Substantial | 35–63% |
| **0.81–1.00** | **Almost perfect** | **64–100%** |

Per McHugh (2012), κ below 0.60 indicates inadequate agreement for clinical contexts. The non-inferiority threshold of −0.10 below human baseline is therefore conservative: if human κ ≈ 0.80, the VLM must achieve κ ≥ 0.70 ("substantial" agreement) to pass.

## 7. Sample Size Justification

Using a one-sided Z-test for non-inferiority with:
- Δ = −0.10 (NI margin)
- Expected κ_AI ≈ κ_human (no difference under alternative)
- α = 0.025 (one-sided), power = 0.80
- Standard error of κ ≈ 0.02 (estimated from n ≈ 2,000 images with 3+ raters)

A **minimum test set of 2,000 images** is required. An **additional 500 edge-case images** (rare variants, ambiguous lesions, atypical presentations) will be curated separately for failure-mode analysis. Total: **2,500 images**.

## 8. Error Analysis & Failure Modes

All discordant cases (VLM label ≠ ground truth) will undergo:

1. **Categorization:** False negative (missed malignancy), false positive (over-referral), misclassification (wrong lesion type).
2. **Attribution:** Visual processing failure vs. vocabulary/knowledge gap vs. prompt sensitivity.
3. **Severity scoring:** Each error rated on a 4-point clinical harm scale (negligible → life-threatening) by an independent clinical reviewer.
4. **Demographic subgroup analysis:** Performance stratified by skin phototype (Fitzpatrick I–VI), lesion location, and image modality (dermoscopic vs. clinical photography).

## 9. Bias Mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Poor image quality inflating NI conclusion** | Strict inclusion criteria; per-protocol analysis weighted equally or above ITT [4] |
| **Prompt overfitting** | Prompt versions developed on a held-out calibration set (200 images); test set never seen during prompt development |
| **Data leakage** | VLM test images excluded from any fine-tuning or few-shot exemplar sets |
| **Label leakage** | Human annotators blinded; VLM receives no ground truth metadata |
| **Publication bias** | All results (including failures) pre-registered on ClinicalTrials.gov or OSF |

## 10. Efficiency & Cost Benchmarking

As a secondary pragmatic analysis, the study will record:

| Dimension | Human Specialist | VLM |
|---|---|---|
| **Time per image** | 30–90 seconds (estimated) | Inference latency |
| **Total wall-clock time** | Weeks (with scheduling) | Hours (batch inference) |
| **Cost per label** | $15–50 (specialist hourly rate) | Compute cost (API or GPU-hours) |
| **Scalability** | Linear with headcount | Near-zero marginal cost |

Cost-effectiveness is reported descriptively and does not influence the primary non-inferiority decision.

## 12. Limitations & Open Questions

- **Static evaluation:** The plan tests a snapshot of current VLMs; model updates may change performance unpredictably.
- **Task scope limited to labeling:** Diagnostic triage and treatment recommendation are out of scope and require separate validation.
- **Ground truth uncertainty:** Histopathological confirmation is available for a subset; many benign labels are clinical diagnoses with inherent uncertainty.
- **Regulatory alignment:** This plan is a research protocol; deployment as a clinical decision-support tool requires separate regulatory clearance (FDA 510(k) or De Novo pathway for SaMD).
