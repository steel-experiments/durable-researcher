## AI-Powered Personalized Learning Paths: A Complete System Design

### Executive Summary

You need a two-bot system (Content Bot + Coach Bot) built on a **shared student profile**, coordinated through simple rule-based triggers rooted in **Bloom's mastery learning** research. The Coach Bot evaluates performance; the Content Bot adapts material. Below is the full logic, metrics, testing plan, failure recovery, and human teacher override mechanism — assembled from research on multi-agent AI architecture, mastery learning theory, and real-world edtech best practices.

---

### 1. System Architecture: How the Two Bots Work Together

**The Core Pattern: Evaluator-Optimizer Workflow** [1]

Both bots are "augmented" LLMs — each has access to tools, retrieval, and a shared memory. The Coach Bot acts as the *evaluator*, the Content Bot acts as the *optimizer*. They coordinate through a **centralized student profile** (a JSON document or database record containing quiz scores, time-on-page, learning style tags, and current path status) [1][4].

```
┌─────────────┐     writes scores      ┌──────────────────┐
│  STUDENT    │ ──────────────────────→ │  Student Profile  │
│  interacts  │                         │  (shared data)    │
└─────────────┘                         └──────┬───────────┘
                                               │
                              ┌────────────────┼────────────────┐
                              ▼                                 ▼
                    ┌──────────────────┐              ┌──────────────────┐
                    │   COACH BOT      │              │  CONTENT BOT     │
                    │  (Evaluator)     │              │  (Optimizer)     │
                    │                  │              │                  │
                    │ - Grades quizzes │──triggers──→ │ - Finds lessons  │
                    │ - Gives feedback │              │ - Adjusts path   │
                    │ - Generates quiz │←─triggers─── │ - Tags content   │
                    └──────────────────┘              └──────────────────┘
```

**How they communicate:** The bots don't talk to each other directly. They read from and write to the shared student profile. When the Coach Bot logs a low quiz score and flags a topic as `needs_remediation`, the Content Bot picks up that flag, fetches appropriate content, and updates the path status. The Coach Bot then sees the updated path and generates a matching quiz [4][5].

### 2. The Adaptive Logic: Simple Rules That Actually Work

Based on Bloom's mastery learning research (1968), the foundational rule is a **mastery threshold** [6]:

> **A student should demonstrate ~90% accuracy before advancing to new material.** Below that, they get corrective instruction — not a failing grade.

Here are the concrete rules, from simplest to slightly more nuanced:

#### Tier 1 — Quiz Score Rules (the backbone)

| Condition | Action | Bot |
|-----------|--------|-----|
| Quiz score **< 50%** | Content Bot fetches a foundational/prerequisite lesson + easier video. Coach Bot generates a remedial quiz on the *prerequisites*. | Content Bot → Coach Bot |
| Quiz score **50–74%** | Content Bot fetches an alternative explanation (same topic, different angle — e.g., visual instead of text). Coach Bot generates targeted practice on the *specific sub-topics* the student got wrong. | Content Bot → Coach Bot |
| Quiz score **75–89%** | Content Bot stays on current topic but adds a "review" resource. Coach Bot gives a short refresher quiz. | Coach Bot |
| Quiz score **≥ 90%** | Content Bot advances to next topic. Coach Bot generates a *forward-looking* pre-assessment to gauge readiness. | Content Bot → Coach Bot |
| **Two consecutive quizzes < 50%** | System **pauses AI adaptations** and escalates to human teacher. | System (safety valve) |

#### Tier 2 — Engagement Rules (secondary signals)

| Condition | Action | Bot |
|-----------|--------|-----|
| Time on page **> 2× average** + quiz score < 70% | Student is stuck, not just slow. Content Bot fetches a simpler resource. | Content Bot |
| Time on page **< 0.5× average** + quiz score < 70% | Student rushed through. Coach Bot prompts: "Want to review this section before trying again?" | Coach Bot |
| Time on page **< 0.5× average** + quiz score ≥ 90% | Student already knows this. Content Bot advances them. | Content Bot |

#### Tier 3 — Learning Style Adaptation (optional, use carefully)

Research is mixed on whether "learning styles" are a real, measurable construct. Rather than asking students "are you visual or auditory?", **observe their behavior** [4]:

| Observed Behavior | Inference | Adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Student consistently scores higher after video content vs. text | Prefers video | Content Bot prioritizes video resources for this student |
| Student spends more time and scores better on interactive exercises | Prefers active learning | Coach Bot generates more practice problems, fewer passive review sessions |
| Student performs better on short frequent quizzes vs. infrequent long ones | Prefers chunked assessment | Coach Bot breaks assessments into smaller pieces |

**Rule for learning style updates:** Only adjust after **3+ consistent data points**. One good video score doesn't prove anything.

### 3. The Full Loop: A Concrete Example

1. Student takes a quiz on fractions → scores **45%**
2. **Coach Bot** grades it, writes score to profile, flags `fractions: needs_remediation`, identifies that the student specifically struggled with "adding fractions with different denominators"
3. **Content Bot** reads the flag → fetches: (a) a 5-minute foundational video on common denominators, (b) a visual step-by-step walkthrough
4. **Content Bot** updates profile: `path_status: remedial`, `current_topic: common_denominators_review`
5. **Coach Bot** sees the updated path → generates a **4-question practice quiz** on common denominators
6. Student takes practice quiz → scores **80%**
7. **Coach Bot** logs score, removes `needs_remediation` flag, sets `ready_to_advance: true`
8. **Content Bot** reads the flag → restores standard path, moves to next fractions sub-topic
9. **Coach Bot** generates a **new formative quiz** on the next topic

If at step 6 the student scores **< 50% again** (two consecutive failures), the system **pauses** and alerts the human teacher.

### 4. How to Know If It Works: Metrics

Track these **actionable metrics** (not vanity metrics like raw logins) [3][4]:

| Metric | What It Tells You | Target / Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre/post assessment score gap** | Did the student actually learn? | Aim for ≥ 20 percentage point improvement post-intervention |
| **Quiz mastery rate** (% of quizzes where student hits ≥ 90%) | Is the system correctly calibrating difficulty? | Target ≥ 75% of students reaching mastery within 2 attempts |
| **Course completion rate** | Are students staying engaged or dropping out? | Compare to baseline (static path) — aim for ≥ 15% improvement |
| **Average time to mastery** (per topic) | Is the system efficient? | Compare slowest 25% of students to fastest 25% — watch for the 6:1 ratio [6] |
| **Recommendation acceptance rate** (teacher) | Do teachers trust and use the AI's suggestions? | Target ≥ 70% teacher approval of bot recommendations |
| **Score regression rate** | How often does a bad recommendation make things worse? | Should be < 5% of all recommendations |

**Bloom's benchmark:** Mastery learning + 1:1 tutoring improves performance by ~2 standard deviations (moving A-grades from 20% to 80% of students) [6]. Your system won't hit that immediately, but it gives you a long-term north star.

### 5. Testing Plan: From Simulation to Classroom

#### Phase 1: Simulation (Week 1–2)
Create **10–20 synthetic student profiles** with varying characteristics:
- Some who learn fast, some slow
- Some who guess on quizzes, some who take a long time
- Some with specific weak topics

Run these profiles through the system with simulated quiz scores and time-on-page data. **Watch for:**
- Does the Content Bot actually fetch easier content when scores are low?
- Does the Coach Bot correctly generate remedial quizzes after path changes?
- Do the two bots ever get stuck in a loop? (e.g., Content Bot downgrades → Coach Bot quizzes → student scores low → Content Bot downgrades again infinitely)
- Does the escalation rule trigger correctly after 2 consecutive failures?

#### Phase 2: Small Class Pilot (Week 3–8)
- **A/B test**: One class uses the AI system, a matched control class uses the standard static curriculum
- **N = 15–30 students per group** (small enough to monitor closely, large enough for directional signals)
- Run for **at least 4–6 weeks** to see adaptation effects
- **Spot-check proficiency data after every module**, not just at the end
- Give teachers a simple dashboard and an override button from day one

#### Phase 3: Iterate
- Compare pre/post scores between groups
- Survey students: "Did the AI understand what you needed?"
- Survey teachers: "Did the AI's recommendations match your professional judgment?"
- Identify the specific topics/rules where the AI performed worst → refine those rules

### 6. Failure Recovery: When the AI Gives Bad Recommendations

Bad recommendations *will* happen. The system needs three layers of defense [2][4][5]:

**Layer 1 — Automatic Safeguards (code-level):**
```
IF consecutive_quiz_scores < 30% → PAUSE all AI adaptations, alert teacher
IF student_path_changed_3_times_without_improvement → revert to last successful node, alert teacher
IF time_spent_on_topic > 4x_average → flag for teacher review
```

**Layer 2 — Auto-Revert:**
When scores drop after a recommendation, the system **automatically reverts the student to their last successful learning node** — the last topic where they scored ≥ 75%. The AI doesn't keep digging a deeper hole [4].

**Layer 3 — Continuous Improvement:**
Every failed recommendation is logged with:
- What the AI recommended
- What data it based the recommendation on
- What the actual outcome was
- What the teacher did instead

These logs feed back into refining the rules. Over time, patterns emerge ("the AI always overestimates students on topic X") that guide targeted fixes.

### 7. Human Teacher Override: The "ACE" Framework

From the US Department of Education's May 2023 AI report: **"Always Center Educators"** [2]. The system must be:

| Principle | What It Means In Practice |
|---|---|
| **Inspectable** | Teacher dashboard shows every bot decision, the data behind it, and the student's full history. "AI recommended easier video because: quiz score 42% on fractions, time spent 3.2x average" |
| **Explainable** | Every recommendation comes with a plain-language rationale. No black-box mysteries. |
| **Severable** | The system can be turned off without breaking the class. Students revert to a standard curriculum path instantly. |
| **Overridable** | Teacher can: (1) **Lock a student's path** — freeze it so the AI can't change it, (2) **Manually assign content** — override the Content Bot's recommendation, (3) **Skip a quiz** — override the Coach Bot's assessment plan, (4) **Set boundaries** — e.g., "this student should never go below grade level 5" |

**Critical design insight from research on automation bias** [2]: If the AI generates a complete plan and the teacher just clicks "approve," the teacher becomes a rubber stamp — research shows humans agree with AI recommendations ~74% of the time even when the AI is wrong. Instead:

- The teacher should **set the strategy** (e.g., "this student needs to focus on fractions this week")
- The AI **proposes specific resources** within that strategy
- The teacher **reviews the evidence** before the path changes
- For minor adjustments (e.g., swapping one video for another), the AI can act autonomously
- For major changes (e.g., moving a student backward a full unit), the system **requires teacher confirmation**

**Regulatory heads-up:** NYC's Local Law 144 (active since July 2023) already requires bias audits for automated decision tools in education. Colorado's AI Act (key provisions starting February 2026) requires impact assessments. Design with these in mind from the start — your override and audit logs will be essential for compliance [2].
