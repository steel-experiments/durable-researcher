# Relocation Report: Top 3 Cities for a Remote Software Engineer Leaving San Francisco

**Profile:** Remote SWE, $120k salary, currently in San Francisco  
**Non-Negotiables:** ≥30% lower cost of living (esp. rent & healthcare), fast internet, Walk Score >60, nature access, major airport ≤90 min

---

## San Francisco Baseline (What You're Leaving)

Before ranking cities, here's the benchmark. Every candidate is measured against these real numbers:

| Metric | San Francisco |
|---|---|
| Walk Score | 88.7 (#1 in the US) |
| 1-BR rent (city center) | ~$3,413/mo |
| 1-BR rent (outside center) | ~$2,770/mo |
| State income tax | Up to 13.3% marginal (effective ~6–7% on $120k ≈ $7,000–$8,400/yr) |
| Broadband cost | ~$72/mo |
| Healthcare | Among the most expensive in the US |

To hit your 30% cost-of-living reduction, target rent needs to come in under **~$1,900/mo** for a 1-bedroom.

## #1 — Philadelphia, Pennsylvania

**This is your best overall pick.** Philadelphia delivers the closest thing to SF's dense, walkable, culturally rich urban lifestyle at a dramatic discount — and the tax math is the most favorable on this list.

### Cost of Living: ✅ Passes the 30% Test Comfortably

| Metric | Philadelphia | Savings vs. SF |
|---|---|---|
| 1-BR rent (city center) | ~$1,850/mo | **~46% cheaper** |
| 1-BR rent (outside center) | ~$1,334/mo | **~52% cheaper** |
| Median home list price | $289,000 | ~35% below national median |
| Healthcare premiums | 20–30% cheaper than CA via ACA marketplace | ✅ |
| Overall cost of living | **40–45% lower than SF** | ✅ |

Your $120k salary will feel dramatically different here. You could live in Center City (Walk Score 98) for less than what you'd pay for a mediocre studio in SF.

### Walkability & Transit: ✅ Strong

- **Walk Score: 74.8** — "Very Walkable," most errands achievable on foot
- **Transit Score: 67.0** — SEPTA regional rail, buses, subway, and trolleys
- **Bike Score: 66.7** — Expanding bike infrastructure, Indego bike-share
- **Key walkable neighborhoods:** Rittenhouse Square, Old City, Fishtown, East Passyunk, Washington Square West

### Nature & Hiking Access: ✅ Surprisingly Good

- **Wissahickon Valley Park:** Over 50 miles of trails within Philly city limits — you can take SEPTA bus directly to trailheads
- **Fairmount Park:** One of the largest urban park systems in the US (2,000+ acres)
- **Circuit Trails:** 800+ mile regional trail network connecting Philly to suburbs
- **Pocono Mountains:** ~2 hours for serious hiking weekend trips
- **Appalachian Trail:** Accessible within ~90 minutes

### Internet: ✅ Excellent

- **Verizon FiOS** provides fiber-to-the-home across most of the city — reliable gigabit speeds
- Average speeds 300+ Mbps, widely available
- Consistently rated among the best fiber-optic covered East Coast cities

### Airport Access: ✅ Excellent

- **Philadelphia International (PHL):** Major American Airlines hub, direct SEPTA train from Center City in ~25 minutes, ~20 min by car
- Direct flights to virtually anywhere domestic plus major international routes

### State & Local Tax Implications: 🟡 The One Complication

| Tax | Rate | CA Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| PA state income tax | **3.07% flat** | Save ~$3,000–$5,000/yr vs. CA |
| Philadelphia resident wage tax | **~3.75%** (for remote workers) | Additional local burden |
| **Combined effective rate** | **~6.82%** | Still better than CA's ~7–9% effective |

**Important nuance:** Philadelphia levies a wage tax on all resident earnings, even remote work. The combined PA + Philly rate works out to roughly 6.8%, which is still better than California's effective rate on $120k (approximately 7–8%). It's a win, just not as dramatic as the flat 3.07% PA rate alone would suggest. Verify your specific remote-work situation with a tax advisor — some out-of-state employer setups may qualify for partial exemptions.

### Weather

Four distinct seasons. Cold, gray winters (December–February avg high: 40–43°F, occasional snow). Hot, humid summers (July avg high: 87°F). Beautiful spring and fall. You'll trade SF's 65°F year-round consistency for real seasons. It's rainier than you'd expect — pack a proper coat.

### Diversity & Amenities

- **Highly diverse** — roughly 44% Black, 15% Hispanic/Latino, 8% Asian, significant immigrant communities
- **Food scene is world-class** — James Beard winners, Reading Terminal Market, Italian Market, massive Vietnamese and Ethiopian corridors
- **Arts & culture:** Philadelphia Museum of Art, Barnes Foundation, Franklin Institute, thriving indie music and theater scene
- **Major university presence:** Penn, Drexel, Temple keep the city intellectually vibrant
- **Sports town:** Eagles, Phillies, Sixers, Flyers — if that matters to you

### The Trade-Offs

- Winter gray can be draining (Philly gets ~200 cloudy days/yr vs. SF's ~160)
- Some neighborhoods have rough edges and crime — neighborhood selection matters a lot
- Public transit is functional but not as polished as BART/Caltrain
- The wage tax is annoying, even if the net math still favors you over CA

## #2 — Chicago, Illinois

**The most "big city" alternative on this list.** If you want to maintain a genuinely cosmopolitan, major-metro lifestyle with world-class amenities — just much cheaper — Chicago is your play.

### Cost of Living: ✅ Passes the 30% Test

| Metric | Chicago | Savings vs. SF |
|---|---|---|
| 1-BR rent (city center) | ~$2,354/mo | **~31% cheaper** |
| 1-BR rent (outside center) | ~$1,701/mo | **~39% cheaper** |
| Price/sq ft (outside center) | $239 vs. SF's $885 | **73% cheaper** |
| Broadband internet | ~$65/mo | **11% cheaper** |
| Utilities (basic) | ~$166/mo vs. SF's $233 | **29% cheaper** |
| Overall cost of living | **35–40% lower than SF** | ✅ |

The rent savings are strong, and if you're open to living outside the Loop (in neighborhoods like Logan Square, Pilsen, or Uptown), your dollar stretches dramatically further.

### Walkability & Transit: ✅ Best on This List

- **Walk Score: 77.2** — highest of any affordable major city in the US
- **Transit Score: 65.0** — CTA 'L' train system + extensive bus network, 24-hour service on some lines
- **Bike Score: 72.2** — excellent cycling infrastructure, Divvy bike-share
- **The Pedway:** 5-mile underground/indoor pedestrian network downtown — a lifesaver in winter
- **Key walkable neighborhoods:** The Loop, Lakeview, Lincoln Park, Logan Square, Wicker Park, Hyde Park

### Nature & Hiking Access: 🟡 Decent, Not Mountainous

- **18-mile Lakefront Trail:** Paved path along Lake Michigan through the entire city — gorgeous and heavily used
- **Lincoln Park:** 1,200+ acres of urban parkland
- **Indiana Dunes National Park:** ~90 minutes by train (South Shore Line from Millennium Station) — real dunes and beach hiking
- **Forest Preserves of Cook County:** 70,000+ acres of natural areas surrounding the city
- **No mountains** — if you need elevation gain, this is not your spot

### Internet: ✅ Very Good

- Multiple fiber providers (AT&T Fiber, Xfinity) with 300+ Mbps widely available
- Illinois ranks in the top 5 states for broadband access
- Average broadband cost: ~$65/mo, competitive market

### Airport Access: ✅ Outstanding

- **O'Hare (ORD):** Global mega-hub, direct Blue Line 'L' train from downtown (~40 min). One of the busiest airports in the world — you can fly direct to virtually anywhere
- **Midway (MDW):** Southwest-heavy domestic hub, direct Orange Line (~30 min from downtown)
- **Two major airports** give you redundancy and price competition that no other city on this list can match

### State Tax Implications: ✅ Clear Win

| Tax | Rate | CA Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| IL state income tax | **4.95% flat** | Save ~$1,500–$3,500/yr vs. CA |
| No local wage tax | ✅ | Simpler than Philly's wage tax |

On $120k, Illinois taxes you ~$5,940 vs. California's ~$7,000–$8,400. That's $1,000–$2,500 back in your pocket annually, with no municipal wage tax complexity.

### Weather

**This is the big trade-off.** Chicago winters are genuinely brutal: January avg high of 31°F, frequent sub-zero wind chills, heavy lake-effect snow. The city is geared for it (the Pedway, excellent snow removal, attached garages), but it's a shock coming from SF. Summers, by contrast, are spectacular — 75–85°F, the lakefront comes alive, and the city is vibrant. Spring is short, fall is crisp and beautiful.

### Diversity & Amenities

- **Highly diverse** (roughly 33% White, 29% Black, 29% Hispanic, 7% Asian) but also notably **racially segregated** by neighborhood — a real and important caveat
- **Best food scene in the Midwest**, arguably top 5 in the US — deep-dish gets the press, but the real story is incredible Mexican (Pilsen), Chinese (Chinatown), Indian (Devon Ave), and fine dining
- **World-class cultural institutions:** Art Institute of Chicago, Field Museum, Museum of Science and Industry, Steppenwolf Theatre, Second City
- **Music scene:** Historic blues/jazz city, major concert venue circuit, Lollapalooza, Pitchfork
- **Pro sports:** Bears, Cubs, White Sox, Bulls, Blackhawks — huge sports culture

### The Trade-Offs

- **Winter is genuinely hard.** Not a minor inconvenience — a lifestyle adjustment. Seasonal affective disorder is real here
- Segregation means your experience varies enormously by neighborhood
- Property taxes are high (less relevant as a renter, but affects long-term home-buying plans)
- Illinois has fiscal instability issues at the state level — long-term tax policy could shift
- Nature is accessible but flat — no mountains, no dramatic coastline like SF

## #3 — Minneapolis, Minnesota

**The wild-card pick for maximum quality of life and outdoor access.** Minneapolis trades some urban grit for an exceptionally high standard of living, the best parks system of any city on this list, and outstanding internet infrastructure.

### Cost of Living: ✅ Passes the 30% Test

| Metric | Minneapolis | Savings vs. SF |
|---|---|---|
| 1-BR rent | ~$1,200–$1,600/mo | **~48–65% cheaper** |
| Overall cost of living | **~35% lower than SF** | ✅ |
| Healthcare costs | Among the lowest in the nation | ✅ |

Minneapolis is the cheapest city on this list for renters. A $1,200–$1,400 one-bedroom in a nice, walkable neighborhood is realistic. Healthcare costs in Minnesota are notably low — the state consistently ranks among the best for health outcomes and affordable care.

### Walkability & Transit: ✅ Solid

- **Walk Score: 71.4** — "Very Walkable"
- **Bike Score: 83.5** — **Best on this list and one of the best in the US** — Minneapolis is a premier cycling city
- **Transit Score: 55.0** — Light rail (Blue & Green Lines) + bus network, functional but less comprehensive than Chicago or Philly
- **Key walkable neighborhoods:** Uptown, Northeast (NE), Dinkytown, North Loop, Whittier
- **Skyway system:** 9.5 miles of enclosed, elevated walkways connecting 80 blocks downtown — walk in comfort when it's -15°F outside

### Nature & Hiking Access: ✅ Best on This List

- **Chain of Lakes:** Bde Maka Ska, Lake Harriet, Lake Calhoun — swimming, kayaking, and trails literally within the city
- **Mississippi River gorge:** Spectacular hiking and trail running along the river bluffs, accessible by bike
- **Minnehaha Falls:** Iconic urban waterfall and park
- **Theodore Wirth Park:** 759-acre urban wilderness with trails
- **Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness:** ~4 hours north — one of the most pristine wilderness areas in the lower 48
- **State parks:** Afton, Taylor's Falls, and Nerstrand Big Woods all within 60–90 minutes
- **City park system:** Ranked #1 in the US by The Trust for Public Land (multiple years running)

### Internet: ✅ Best on This List

- Minneapolis consistently ranks in the **top 10 US cities for internet speed**, averaging **350–400+ Mbps**
- Widespread fiber coverage (CenturyLink/Lumen fiber, Xfinity)
- US Internet offers fiber in parts of the Twin Cities with symmetrical gigabit speeds
- This is a genuine tech infrastructure advantage for remote work

### Airport Access: ✅ Excellent

- **Minneapolis-Saint Paul International (MSP):** Major Delta hub, 15–20 minutes from downtown via Blue Line light rail
- Consistently rated one of the best-run airports in the US
- Direct flights to virtually everywhere domestic + major international routes
- Delta hub means good flight frequency and pricing

### State Tax Implications: 🟡 The Weakest Point

| Tax | Rate | CA Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| MN state income tax | **5.35%–9.85% progressive** | Marginal savings vs. CA; may be similar |
| On $120k taxable | ~$7,800–$8,500 (effective ~6.5–7%) | **Barely better than CA** |

This is the biggest downside. Minnesota's top bracket of 9.85% kicks in at a relatively low threshold. On $120k, your effective state tax rate is roughly 6.5–7% — only marginally better than California. You're moving for the cost-of-living savings, not the tax savings. If tax optimization is a top priority, Philly or Chicago are better picks.

### Weather

**The harshest winters of any city on this list.** Minneapolis regularly hits -10°F to -20°F with wind chill. January avg high: 24°F. Snow season runs November–April. The city is **built for it** (skyways, indoor malls, excellent plowing, attached garages are standard) but it requires a genuine mindset shift. Summers, however, are glorious — 75–85°F, the lakes are swimmable, the city is green and active. Fall colors are stunning.

### Diversity & Amenities

- **Less diverse** than Chicago or Philly (~63% White, 19% Black, 11% Hispanic, 8% Asian) — this is a real trade-off
- **Strong Somali, Hmong, and East African communities** — unique cultural presence
- **Thriving arts scene:** Guthrie Theater, Walker Art Center, First Avenue (Prince's venue), growing indie music and craft brewery scene
- **Excellent restaurant scene** but less variety and depth than Chicago or Philly
- **High quality of life:** Consistently ranks among the best cities for overall well-being in the US
- **High civic engagement and progressive values** — if that aligns with your politics

### The Trade-Offs

- **The winters are no joke.** This isn't "put on a jacket" cold — it's "plan your life around the season" cold
- **State taxes barely improve on CA** — the financial win is all in rent and healthcare, not in take-home pay
- **Less diverse** and less cosmopolitan than Chicago or Philly — fewer neighborhoods with distinct cultural identities
- Smaller city energy — if you love the density and chaos of SF, Minneapolis will feel quiet
- Limited late-night culture compared to SF or Chicago

## Bottom Line

**Go to Philadelphia** if you want the best all-around balance — the most walkable affordable city with decent tax savings, strong nature access, and a genuinely diverse, culturally rich urban environment. It's the closest thing to SF's vibe at 45% off.

**Go to Chicago** if you want the biggest city energy and the best airport access, and you're willing to endure real winters. The tax situation is clean and favorable, and the transit/walkability is top-tier.

**Go to Minneapolis** if you're an outdoors person who values green space, lakes, and trails above all else, and you want the absolute cheapest rent and best internet. Just know that the tax savings are minimal and the winters are the most extreme.

All three cities meet your non-negotiables. The choice comes down to which trade-offs you're most willing to live with.
