# Around the World in 80 Days — No Planes, Low Budget, US Passport

## Starting & Ending in New York City

---

## Executive Summary

An aviation-free circumnavigation starting and ending in New York is absolutely possible by combining cargo ship voyages, trains, buses, and regional ferries. A US passport gives you visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to roughly 185 countries, which is a major advantage. The primary challenge is the cost of ocean crossings (cargo ships run ~$100/day per cabin) and the rigid scheduling of freighter routes. A realistic ultra-budget attempt targeting 20–30+ countries would cost approximately **$8,000–$12,000** total, depending on how much overland budget travel you do versus paid ship berths.

---

## Critical Legal Constraint: US Cabotage Law

Under US Maritime Cabotage law, passengers **cannot travel by cargo ship from one US port to another** (e.g., Los Angeles to New York). This means your return to the East Coast must be completed overland (bus or train) after you arrive at a US port. This shapes the entire route. [1]

## The Route: Eastbound from New York

### PHASE 1 — Transatlantic Crossing & Western Europe (Days 1–18)

**New York → Europe (by freighter ship)**
- Book a transatlantic cargo ship from the New York/New Jersey area (or nearby Baltimore) to **Hamburg, Germany** or **Antwerp, Belgium**.
- Estimated crossing time: **10–14 days**.
- Cost: **~$1,200–$1,500** (at ~$100/day + $262 deviation insurance + $12.50 customs).
- Book through specialized freighter agents such as **Langsam Reisen** or **NSB**. Deposit of $500 required; full payment 60 days before departure. [1]

**Overland through Western Europe (Days 14–18)**
Once in Hamburg, use budget buses (**FlixBus**, from ~$5–15/segment) or regional trains to hit multiple countries quickly:

| Stop | Country | Mode | Visa for US? |
|------|---------|------|-------------|
| Hamburg | Germany | Starting point | No (Schengen 90 days) |
| Copenhagen | Denmark | Train/bus | No |

| Brussels | Belgium | Bus | No |

| London | UK | Eurostar/ferry + ETA | ETA required (online, ~$10) |

**Phase 1 Country Count: ~7 countries + multiple islands (British Isles)**

**Critical Routing Note:** You can attempt to board a freighter from a Mediterranean or Middle Eastern port (e.g., Piraeus/Greece, Haifa/Israel, or Dubai) that transits the **Suez Canal** and continues to India or Southeast Asia. This is the modern equivalent of Phileas Fogg's route. Inoculations for yellow fever and cholera ($50–60 each) may be required for Suez/Red Sea transit. [1]

**Alternative South Asia routing:** If freighter schedules don't align, travel overland from Turkey through Iran (visa required, difficult for US citizens currently — **skip**), or take a freighter from Dubai directly to **Mumbai, India**.

## Money-Saving Strategies

1. **Crew on a sailboat instead of freighter:** Register on **FindACrew.net** [3] — the world's largest boat/crew network spanning 200+ countries. If you can find a boat needing crew for an Atlantic or Pacific crossing, your passage could be **free** (you contribute labor). This is the single biggest potential savings ($3,000–$5,000).

2. **Repositioning cruises:** Cruise lines reposition ships seasonally (spring: Caribbean→Europe; fall: Europe→Caribbean; spring: Asia→Alaska). These can cost as little as **$30–50/day** including meals — cheaper than freighters with far better amenities. Book through CruiseLine.com or Vacations To Go's "repositioning" section.

3. **Overnight transport = free accommodation:** Always choose overnight buses, trains, or ferries. In Southeast Asia, overnight buses cost $8–15 and save a night's lodging.

4. **Cook your own food:** Many hostels have kitchens. Buy groceries at local markets — especially cheap in Southeast Asia, India, and Turkey.

5. **Free walking tours:** Available in nearly every European and Asian city. Tip $2–5.

## Key Risks & Contingencies

1. **Freighter schedule inflexibility:** Cargo ships run on commercial schedules, not passenger ones. You may wait weeks for a berth. **Buffer at least 10–15 extra days** or have flexible dates.
2. **Geopolitical disruptions:** Suez Canal closures, Red Sea conflicts, or Russia sanctions can force rerouting. Monitor the news and have backup routes (e.g., around Cape of Good Hope instead of Suez).
3. **Seasickness:** You'll spend 40–50 days at sea. Bring medication.
4. **Visa overstays:** Schengen allows 90 days in any 180-day period. Your entire trip is 80 days, so you should be fine, but don't linger too long in Schengen early on.
5. **No internet at sea:** Download offline maps, translation apps, and booking confirmations before each crossing.

## Uncertainty & Gaps

- **Freighter route availability:** Specific port-to-port cargo ship schedules change constantly and must be confirmed with booking agents 2–6 months in advance. The routes described are typical but not guaranteed.
- **Suez Canal transit:** Current geopolitical tensions in the Red Sea may make freighter transit through Suez unreliable. A Cape of Good Hope detour adds 10–14 days and significant cost.
- **Russia/China visas:** Both countries currently have tense diplomatic relations with the US, making tourist visas harder to obtain. The route above avoids both, but this limits the overland options through Central Asia.
- **Exact daily budget figures** for individual countries vary by season and were estimated from general backpacking knowledge rather than real-time 2025 data.
- **Pacific island stopovers** on freighters are not guaranteed and depend entirely on the ship's cargo schedule.
