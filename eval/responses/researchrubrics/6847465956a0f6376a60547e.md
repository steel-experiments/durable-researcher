# Research Scoping Report: Decentralized Heterogeneous Swarm for Search and Rescue in GPS-Denied Environments

---

## Executive Summary

This report scopes the key technical pillars for a research proposal on decentralized control of heterogeneous UAV-UGV swarms performing search and rescue in unstructured, GPS-denied environments. Based on analysis of current state-of-the-art systems and simulation frameworks, the proposal is highly feasible but must carefully integrate four largely independent research threads—multi-sensor SLAM, decentralized task allocation, resilient mesh communication, and formal performance guarantees—into a single coherent architecture. The most significant novelty gap lies in **combining formal resilience guarantees with heterogeneous role allocation over lossy mesh networks**, an area with limited prior work.

---

## 1. Multi-Sensor Fusion for State Estimation and Perception

### Recommended Baseline: LVI-SAM + FAST-LIO2 Hybrid

**LVI-SAM** (Shan et al., ICRA 2021) is the strongest candidate for your primary state estimation backbone. It tightly couples a visual-inertial module (adapted from VINS-Mono) with a LiDAR-inertial module (adapted from LIO-SAM) using GTSAM factor graph optimization. Its critical advantage for your scenario is **mutual failure-mode mitigation**: vision degrades in smoke or darkness (common in collapsed structures), while LiDAR struggles in geometrically degenerate environments (long corridors, open fields). The tight coupling ensures at least one modality keeps the system alive when the other fails [1].

**FAST-LIO2** (HKU-Mars) should be considered as the odometry layer for computationally constrained platforms, especially smaller UAVs. It achieves >100 Hz processing via an incremental KD-tree (ikd-Tree), runs on ARM-based boards (Raspberry Pi 4B, Nvidia TX2), and uses direct scan-to-map matching without feature extraction—making it sensor-agnostic across spinning and solid-state LiDARs. Its extensions (R2LIVE, FAST-LIVO) add visual fusion when camera payload is available [2].

### Dual-Use Perception: Navigation + Victim/Hazard Detection

Your proposal requires the perception stack to serve double duty—SLAM *and* detection. The architecture should layer a **semantic segmentation module** on top of the SLAM backbone. Kimera-Semantics (part of the Kimera ecosystem from MIT SPARK Lab) reconstructs metric-semantic 3D meshes in real-time, labeling surfaces as traversable, obstacle, or potential victim. This is directly compatible with the distributed mapping approach discussed in Section 3. For victim detection specifically, you would need to integrate a thermal or RGB-based object detector (e.g., YOLO-based) whose detections get embedded as semantic labels in the local mesh, then propagated through the swarm's shared map.

### Research Gaps

- Most multi-sensor SLAM systems (LVI-SAM, FAST-LIVO) are validated on structured outdoor or indoor datasets, not collapsed-building rubble or dense forest canopy. **Your contribution would include benchmarking these systems in extreme degeneracy scenarios** (e.g., LiDAR returns from amorphous debris, vision obscured by dust).
- Real-time switching between LiDAR-dominant and vision-dominant modes based on environment conditions remains an open problem. A **confidence-weighted fusion** strategy (where each sensor modality's weight adapts based on its estimated reliability) would be a novel contribution.

## 2. Decentralized Role Assignment and Task Reallocation

### The State of the Art: Market-Based and Consensus Approaches

The most established framework for decentralized multi-robot task allocation is the **Consensus-Based Bundle Algorithm (CBBA)** (Choi, Brunet, and How, IEEE Transactions on Robotics, 2009). CBBA works as follows:

1. Each agent independently builds a "bundle" of tasks it intends to perform, scored by utility.
2. Agents broadcast their bundles to neighbors.
3. A consensus phase resolves conflicts (two agents claiming the same task) by comparing scores and yielding.
4. The process iterates until all agents agree on a conflict-free allocation.

CBBA guarantees **convergence to a conflict-free allocation in polynomial time** and produces solutions within 50% of optimal for general instances (and optimal for special cases). Critically, it operates entirely through local communication—no central coordinator.

### Adapting CBBA for Heterogeneous SAR Roles

Your scenario introduces two complications CBBA was not designed for:

- **Heterogeneous capabilities**: A UAV cannot clear debris; a UGV cannot hover above rubble. This requires **capability-aware utility functions** where each agent scores tasks only within its feasible set. The extension **CBBA with heterogeneous agents** has been explored in follow-up work, where utility is set to -∞ for infeasible task-agent pairings.
- **Dynamic reallocation under failure**: When an agent dies (battery depletion, stuck in debris), its tasks must be redistributed. CBBA naturally handles this if you treat agent loss as a "reset"—surviving agents detect the missing agent (via heartbeat timeout), remove its bundle from consensus, and re-run allocation. **The key research question is bounding the reallocation latency**: how quickly does the swarm converge to a new allocation after losing k agents?

### Recommended Architecture

A **hierarchical task structure** would map well to your scenario:

| Level | Tasks | Assignment Method |
|-------|-------|-------------------|
| **Strategic** | Region assignment (which zone each agent explores) | CBBA with spatial utility |
| **Tactical** | Role assignment (mapper, searcher, debris clearer) | Local auction based on agent capability + battery state |
| **Reactive** | Emergency reallocation (agent failure, new hazard) | Gossip-based triggering of re-auction |

The reactive layer is where your novelty lives. Implementing a **failure-triggered partial re-auction** that only reassigns the lost agent's tasks (rather than re-solving the entire allocation) would be both practical and publishable.

## 3. Communication: Ad-Hoc Mesh Networks and Distributed Mapping

### Distributed Mapping: Kimera-Multi

**Kimera-Multi** (MIT SPARK Lab, IEEE TRO 2022) provides the strongest existing foundation for your distributed mapping layer. It is a fully distributed multi-robot SLAM system where [3]:

- Each agent builds **local trajectory estimates and metric-semantic 3D meshes** independently using Kimera-VIO and Kimera-Semantics.
- Inter-robot loop closures are detected by exchanging lightweight **Bag-of-Words (BoW) vectors** and selected keyframes—avoiding raw data transfer.
- A **distributed pose graph optimization (DPGO)** based on graduated non-convexity periodically aligns trajectories globally.
- After optimization, each robot locally corrects its 3D mesh using mesh deformation (Kimera-PGMO).

Communication is **strictly peer-to-peer over UDP** with no central coordinator. The system transmits lightweight descriptors rather than heavy point clouds or images, making it inherently bandwidth-efficient. This directly addresses your requirement for propagating victim locations, hazards, and blocked passages over a lossy mesh network.

### Handling Intermittent Connectivity

Kimera-Multi's P2P layer assumes eventually connected communication graphs (robots must encounter each other periodically). For your scenario with **chronically sparse connectivity**, you need to augment this with:

- **Delay-tolerant information carriers**: Robots act as "data mules." When a UAV discovers a victim in a connectivity shadow, it carries that information until it re-enters communication range, then floods it via gossip protocol.
- **Priority-based message scheduling**: Not all data is equal. Victim locations are critical; routine map updates are not. Implement an **urgency-weighted broadcast protocol** where critical messages get aggressive flooding (send to all neighbors at every opportunity) while routine map merges use lazy epidemic spreading.

### ROS2 DDS Considerations for Your Simulation

For your ROS2/Gazebo implementation, DDS QoS policies are critical for simulating realistic comms degradation. Research shows that setting `reliability=RELIABLE` with `history=DEPTH 10` can mitigate ~20% packet loss without desync up to 50ms latency spikes. For your scenarios, you would want to intentionally degrade these QoS settings to model realistic disaster-zone mesh networks and measure how your information propagation protocol degrades [4].

### Research Gaps

- Kimera-Multi is visual-inertial only (no LiDAR). **Extending its distributed P2P architecture to a LiDAR-visual-inertial front-end** (e.g., replacing Kimera-VIO with LVI-SAM) would be a significant engineering contribution.
- **No existing system combines distributed semantic mapping with priority-based information routing in lossy networks.** This is a clear novelty target for your proposal.

## 4. Formal and Empirical Performance Guarantees

### The Challenge

Your target guarantee—"90% of reachable victims located within bounded time, even with 30% agent loss"—is ambitious. The multi-robot coverage literature provides partial tools:

### Available Formal Frameworks

1. **Distributed coverage control** (Cortés, Bullo, J. of Mathematical Systems, Estimation, and Control): Proves that Voronoi-based partitioning achieves complete coverage in bounded time for homogeneous teams with perfect communication. This gives you a **baseline bound** from which to measure degradation.

2. **Fault-tolerant consensus** (specifically (f+1)-resilient consensus): Classical results show that with n agents and f Byzantine/crash failures, consensus requires n > 2f+1 (crash) or n > 3f+1 (Byzantine). For your 30% loss target, this means your minimum swarm size must be at least ~5 agents (since 2 can fail and 3 survive, satisfying n > 2×2+1). These bounds give you **formal resilience thresholds** for your architecture.

3. **Probabilistic coverage bounds** from the robotic exploration literature (e.g., frontier-based exploration): These typically show O(n/k) coverage time for n cells and k agents, but assume reliable communication. **Deriving the equivalent bound under intermittent connectivity is an open problem** and would be a strong theoretical contribution.

### Recommended Approach: Layered Guarantees

Given the difficulty of a single unified proof, structure your guarantees as:

| Guarantee Type | Statement | Method |
|----------------|-----------|--------|
| **Coverage completeness** | If the communication graph is eventually connected, all reachable regions are visited | Proof via frontier exploration completeness + eventual connectivity |
| **Resilience** | Task allocation converges after losing ≤k agents if n > 2k+1 | Proof via CBBA convergence + crash-fault consensus bounds |
| **Detection rate** | ≥90% of reachable victims found within time T | Empirical, across Monte Carlo runs with varied failure patterns |
| **Reallocation latency** | Tasks from lost agent reassigned within time τ | Empirical + analytical bound based on gossip convergence rate |

The first two can be formally proven; the latter two are strong empirical claims validated through simulation. This layered approach is publishable and defensible.

## 5. Simulation and Evaluation Framework

### Recommended Stack

| Component | Tool | Justification |
|-----------|------|---------------|
| Physics/Sensors | **Gazebo Harmonic** + ROS2 Humble | Standard for multi-robot SAR; supports LiDAR, IMU, camera plugins |
| SLAM | LVI-SAM or FAST-LIVO (ROS-wrapped) | Proven GPS-denied performance |
| Mesh Comms | DDS QoS degradation + custom P2P layer | Models realistic packet loss and latency |
| Task Allocation | Custom CBBA implementation | Decentralized, provably convergent |
| Analysis | ros2 trace + custom metrics nodes | DDS latency profiling, coverage tracking |

### Scalability Benchmarks

Existing benchmarks from ROS2 multi-robot simulation (50-robot tests) show: rclpy achieves 12ms latency (1.05 Real-Time Factor), system desyncs occur at >50ms latency causing 0.3m/step odometry drift. Gazebo scales to 150-200 agents with headless mode on consumer GPUs but hits physics engine limits at >150 robots due to O(n²) collision checks [4]. For your proposal, targeting a swarm of **8-15 agents** (mix of UAVs and UGVs) is realistic for full-fidelity simulation and sufficient to demonstrate resilience at 30% loss.

### Three Disaster Scenarios

| Scenario | Environment | Complexity Drivers | Metrics Focus |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|---------------|
| **1. Collapsed building** | Multi-floor rubble, narrow passages | Severe GPS denial, confined spaces, dust/smoke | SLAM robustness, victim detection in clutter |
| **2. Dense forest** | Canopy cover, uneven terrain | GPS denial via canopy, LiDAR noise from foliage | Coverage efficiency, mesh connectivity range |
| **3. Urban mixed** | Partially standing structures + open areas + underground | Heterogeneous terrain, mixed connectivity zones | Role reallocation, cross-domain UAV-UGV coordination |

### Required Metrics

- **Coverage rate**: % of traversable area mapped per unit time
- **Victim-finding rate**: % of reachable victims located (target: ≥90%)
- **Rescue latency**: Time from mission start to victim detection (bounded by T)
- **Network overhead**: Bytes transmitted per victim found (efficiency)
- **Resilience score**: Performance degradation curve as function of % agents lost
- **Reallocation time**: Time from agent failure to stable new task assignment

## 6. Key Risks and Mitigation

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|------|----------|------------|
| Gazebo simulation too slow for Monte Carlo runs | High | Use headless mode + cloud instances (AWS g5, ~$50K/yr vs $2.5M physical); pre-compute reduced-order sensor models |
| LVI-SAM too heavy for onboard UGV compute | Medium | Use FAST-LIO2 for UGVs (runs on ARM) and LVI-SAM for UAVs with better compute |
| CBBA reallocation too slow under high loss rates | Medium | Implement partial re-auction (only lost agent's tasks) rather than full re-solve |
| Formal guarantees too weak for reviewers | Medium | Layer analytical bounds (coverage completeness, consensus convergence) with strong empirical Monte Carlo evidence |
| Kimera-Multi integration with LiDAR SLAM non-trivial | High | Budget significant engineering time; consider using only its P2P communication layer with a different SLAM front-end |

## 7. Novelty Summary

Your proposal sits at the intersection of four research areas, each well-studied in isolation but rarely combined:

1. **Multi-modal SLAM for extreme GPS-denied SAR** — extending LVI-SAM/FAST-LIO2 with confidence-weighted fusion and semantic detection
2. **Heterogeneous role allocation with formal resilience** — capability-aware CBBA with bounded reallocation after agent loss
3. **Priority-based distributed mapping over lossy mesh** — extending Kimera-Multi's P2P protocol with urgency routing and delay-tolerant propagation
4. **Integrated formal + empirical guarantees** — layered proofs of coverage and resilience combined with Monte Carlo validation

The **strongest single contribution** is likely item 3: no existing system provides both distributed semantic mapping *and* priority-based information routing that demonstrably works under realistic connectivity loss with heterogeneous agents. Items 1 and 2 are solid engineering contributions with incremental novelty; item 4 provides the rigorous evaluation that elevates the work from a demo to a scientific contribution.

## Uncertainty & Gaps

- **CBBA and formal coverage guarantees**: My analysis draws on well-known results from the multi-robot allocation and coverage literature (Choi et al. 2009, Cortés & Bullo), but I was unable to browse primary sources directly due to paywall and indexing limitations. These references should be verified and cited from the original papers.
- **Victim detection integration**: The question of how to integrate object detection (e.g., YOLO-based thermal victim detection) into a SLAM semantic mesh is architecturally clear but has limited published end-to-end systems in the SAR domain specifically.
- **Formal bound for coverage under intermittent connectivity**: This remains a genuinely open theoretical problem. Your proposal could either (a) derive a weaker bound by assuming periodic connectivity intervals, or (b) rely entirely on empirical Monte Carlo evidence for this dimension.
- **Kimera-Multi + LiDAR fusion**: The feasibility of replacing Kimera's visual-inertial front-end with a LiDAR-visual-inertial system is architecturally sound but untested in published work. This integration effort should be scoped as a significant engineering milestone in your proposal timeline.
