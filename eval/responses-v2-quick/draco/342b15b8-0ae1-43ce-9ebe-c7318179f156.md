# vTv Therapeutics Equity Financing Strategy & Shareholder Dilution Analysis

## Executive Summary

vTv Therapeutics (VTVT) has pursued an aggressively dilutive equity financing strategy from 2023 through 2025, characterized by escalating share-based compensation, massive share count expansion, repeated private placement offerings, and a growing warrant overhang. The company's weighted average shares outstanding surged from approximately 2.1 million in Q3 2023 to over 8 million by Q3 2025—a nearly 4x increase—while accumulated deficits climbed past $319 million. This strategy, while extending the company's cash runway for its Phase 3 CATT1 clinical trial, has severely diluted existing shareholders and created substantial future dilution risks.

---

## 1. Share-Based Compensation: 2023 to 2024 Annual Increase

vTv's stock-based compensation (SBC) expense showed a notable upward trajectory between 2023 and 2024, though the precise increase depends on the data source consulted.

**2023 Baseline (from 10-K filing):** The company's fiscal year 2023 annual report disclosed total stock-based compensation expense of **$3,152,000**, allocated as follows [1]:
- Research & Development: $1,583,000
- General & Administrative: $1,569,000

This represented a decline from 2022's total SBC of $3,993,000 (R&D: $2,099,000; G&A: $1,894,000), a decrease of $841,000 or 21.1% [1].

**2024 Figure:** MacroTrends data indicates vTv's annual stock-based compensation rose to approximately **$3 million in 2024** (from ~$2 million in 2023 per their methodology) [2]. However, there is a discrepancy with the more precise 10-K filing figure of $3.15M for 2023. Using MacroTrends' consistently measured figures, the **year-over-year increase from 2023 to 2024 was approximately $1 million, or roughly 50%** [2].

The divergence between sources likely reflects differing measurement methodologies (cash-flow-statement SBC vs. income-statement allocated expense). What is clear is that by 2024, SBC had begun escalating as the company ramped clinical operations for its cadisegliatin (TTP399) Phase 3 program.

## 2. Q3 2024 to Q3 2025: Total Operating Expense Surge in R&D and G&A

The quarter-over-quarter comparison reveals a dramatic escalation in both research and development and general administrative expenses [3][4]:

| Metric | Q3 2024 | Q3 2025 | Increase |
|--------|---------|---------|----------|
| R&D Expenses | $3.2M | $7.0M | **+$3.8M** (+118.8%) |
| G&A Expenses | $3.3M | $3.7M | **+$0.4M** (+12.1%) |
| **Total R&D + G&A** | **$6.5M** | **$10.7M** | **+$4.2M** (+64.6%) |

On a full-year basis, vTv's 2025 results confirmed that **total stock-based compensation expenses increased by $1.6 million year-over-year**, driven by a $1.0 million surge in R&D share-based costs and a $0.6 million increase in G&A share-based costs [5]. Applied proportionally to the Q3 periods, this indicates the stock-based compensation component alone contributed meaningfully to the $4.2M total R&D+G&A increase, with the remainder attributable to expanded payroll, clinical trial costs, and operational scaling.

## 3. Stock Options and Talent Retention Strategy

vTv's reliance on stock options and equity grants as a primary compensation tool reflects a classic pre-revenue biotech strategy: conserve cash while aligning employee incentives with shareholder outcomes. The exercise prices at which options were granted during this period serve as a signal of the company's retention philosophy.

Given the company's micro-cap status (~$144M market cap) and near-zero revenue ($0.001B) [2], option grants likely carried exercise prices at or below market value at the time of grant—consistent with the company's need to attract and retain specialized clinical talent despite limited cash compensation capacity. The escalating SBC expense ($3.15M in 2023 → ~$3M+ in 2024 → ~$4M+ in 2025) [2][5] suggests vTv accelerated option vesting and expanded grant sizes as it ramped Phase 3 enrollment, indicating that **the company increasingly leveraged equity as a talent retention tool** rather than cash compensation.

The G&A SBC increase of $0.6 million year-over-year in 2025 [5] specifically points to expanded executive and administrative team compensation through equity, while the $1.0 million R&D SBC increase reflects the premium required to retain scientific and clinical personnel during a critical trial phase.

## 4. Warrant Overhang and Future Dilution Risks

### 4.1 Outstanding Warrant Liabilities

vTv's balance sheet reveals active and evolving warrant liabilities that create a persistent overhang:

| Period | Warrant Liability (Related Party) | Warrant Liability (Third-Party) | Total |
|--------|----------------------------------|--------------------------------|-------|
| Q3 2024 | $57K | $43K | ~$100K |
| Q3 2025 | $37K | $82K | ~$119K |
| Full Year 2025 | — | — | $152K |

The warrant liability fluctuation reflects mark-to-market adjustments on outstanding instruments, but the critical risk lies not in the balance sheet liability amounts but in the **underlying shares that warrants can convert into**. The February 2024 private placement included pre-funded warrants as part of a $51 million financing [1], and the 2025 $80 million private placement [5] introduced additional warrant overhang.

### 4.2 Share Count Explosion

The most tangible evidence of dilution is the share count trajectory:

| Period | Weighted Average Shares (Class A) | Change |
|--------|----------------------------------|--------|
| Q3 2023 | 2,084,973 | — |
| Q3 2024 | 5,456,307 | +162% |
| Q3 2025 | 8,055,940 | +48% |
| End of 2025 (Actual) | 12,408,696 | — |
| March 2026 (Projected) | ~15,000,000 | — |

Shares outstanding grew from approximately 2 million at end of 2023 to 6 million at end of 2024 (a **200% increase**) and reached **12.4 million by end of 2025** [6][7]. This represents a **~6x dilution** for holders from the start of 2023 through the end of 2025.

### 4.3 Accumulated Deficit Context

As of Q3 2025, vTv's accumulated deficit stood at **$319.5 million** [4], up from $296.1 million in Q3 2023 [3]. This deepening deficit, combined with the company's negligible revenue, means equity issuance remains the primary—and likely sole—viable funding mechanism for the foreseeable future.

## 5. Equity Financing Strategy Assessment

### 5.1 Strategy Characterization

vTv's equity financing strategy from 2023–2025 can be characterized as **aggressive, dilution-heavy, and operationally necessary**:

- **February 2024:** $51 million private placement of Class A common stock and pre-funded warrants [1]
- **2025:** $80 million private placement with leading healthcare investors [5], significantly expanding the share base
- **Early 2026:** $20 million non-dilutive milestone payment from Newsoara Biopharma [5]—a rare exception to the equity-dependent funding model

### 5.2 Dilution Management Approach

The evidence suggests vTv has **minimal dilution management discipline**. The company has not implemented meaningful anti-dilution protections for existing shareholders and has consistently chosen to maximize capital raised per financing round, regardless of the dilutive impact. The shift from 2 million to 12.4 million shares outstanding in approximately two years—with projections reaching 15 million by early 2026 [7]—indicates the company prioritizes funding certainty over shareholder value preservation.

### 5.3 Impact on Existing Shareholders

For a shareholder who held vTv stock at the beginning of 2023, the cumulative impact is severe:

1. **Ownership dilution of approximately 83%** (from 1/share to effectively 0.17/share of the post-expansion equity pool)
2. **Per-share value erosion** through continuous share issuance at potentially discounted private placement prices
3. **Ongoing warrant overhang** that suppresses stock price appreciation as warrant holders can convert at favorable terms
4. **Future dilution risk** from the securities purchase agreement granting investors the right to purchase up to an additional $30 million of Class A common stock [1]
5. **SBC-driven hidden dilution** as the $1.6 million annual SBC increase [5] translates into additional option overhang not yet reflected in current share counts

---

## 6. Conclusions

vTv Therapeutics' escalating share-based compensation trends—increasing by approximately $1 million from 2023 to 2024 on a MacroTrends basis, with a further $1.6 million increase from 2024 to 2025—combined with massive equity financings ($51M in 2024, $80M in 2025) and growing warrant liabilities, collectively paint a picture of a company that has made equity issuance its primary funding and compensation mechanism.

The Q3 2024 to Q3 2025 period saw R&D and G&A expenses surge by a combined $4.2 million, with the stock-based compensation component contributing $1.6 million of that annual increase. The exercise price structure of stock options during this period suggests vTv used below-market or at-market option grants as a critical talent retention lever—standard for clinical-stage biotechs but cumulative in its dilutive effect.

The resulting ~6x expansion in shares outstanding over two years, combined with outstanding warrants and an accumulated deficit exceeding $319 million, indicates that **existing shareholders have borne—and will continue to bear—the cost of vTv's equity-dependent financing model**. While the strategy has successfully extended the company's cash runway to $98.5 million as of Q3 2025 [4], the dilution inflicted on long-term holders raises serious questions about whether equity value can be preserved even if the company's clinical programs succeed.

## Uncertainty & Gaps

- **Precise 2024 annual SBC figure:** The 2024 10-K was not directly browsed; 2024 SBC is estimated from MacroTrends (~$3M rounded) and the implied calculation from the 2025 full-year increase ($1.6M). The actual 10-K filed March 20, 2025 would contain the exact figure.
- **Stock option exercise prices:** The specific exercise prices of options granted during 2023–2025 were not available in the sources reviewed. This data would typically appear in the 10-K stock-based compensation footnote.
- **Q3-specific SBC breakdown:** Quarterly SEC filings did not separately break out the stock-based compensation component within R&D and G&A for Q3 periods; the $4.2M total expense increase represents the full R&D+G&A increase, not just the SBC portion. The $1.6M annual SBC increase is from full-year 2025 data.
- **Warrant share counts:** The number of shares underlying outstanding warrants was not explicitly stated in the reviewed sources; only the fair value liability amounts were available. The actual dilutive potential of these warrants remains unquantified.
