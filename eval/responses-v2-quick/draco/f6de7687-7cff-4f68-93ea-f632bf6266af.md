# CNC Multi-Tasking Machine Comparison for Ti-6Al-4V Aerospace Machining in Nuevo León

## Executive Summary

All three machines — DMG MORI NLX 2500/700SY, Mazak INTEGREX i-400S, and Okuma Multus U4000 — can process Ti-6Al-4V, but they diverge sharply in spindle torque, thermal compensation architecture, and local service infrastructure in Nuevo León. The Mazak INTEGREX i-400S (and its current i-H successor) offers the highest spindle torque (~1,200 Nm) and the most mature Nuevo León presence with a full Technology Center in Apodaca. The DMG MORI NLX 2500 provides the most structured thermal compensation for ±0.0005" work and box-guideway rigidity ideal for titanium chatter suppression. The Okuma Multus U4000 brings the heaviest platform (44,000 lbs) but the weakest documented Nuevo León service footprint. Critically, the stated 15,000 annual operating hours is impossible for a single machine and must be reinterpreted.

---

## 1. Utilization Clarification: The 15,000-Hour Problem

A single machine cannot exceed **8,760 hours/year** (24 hrs × 365 days). At realistic 85–90% spindle utilization for a three-shift aerospace operation, a single machine delivers ~7,450–7,880 cutting hours annually. The 15,000-hour figure most likely represents one of:

- **A two-machine fleet** (e.g., 2 × ~7,500 hrs each), which is consistent with a production shop running redundant capacity for AS9100D flow.
- **Cumulative spindle-hours across main + sub-spindle** on a multi-tasking machine, where both spindles cut simultaneously (~2 × 7,500 = 15,000).
- **Aggregate shop-floor machining hours** across all CNC assets, not just one unit.

This distinction matters enormously for long-term operating cost modeling, as maintenance intervals, tooling consumption, and energy costs scale linearly with actual spindle hours.

## 2. Spindle Torque & Rigidity for Ti-6Al-4V

Ti-6Al-4V demands high torque at low RPM (typically 60–120 m/min cutting speed) and extreme structural rigidity to resist chatter during aggressive roughing.

| Parameter | DMG MORI NLX 2500/700SY | Mazak INTEGREX i-400S (i-H class) | Okuma Multus U4000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Main Spindle Torque** | 828 Nm (10" chuck); 1,273 Nm (opt. 12") | ~1,200 Nm at 3,300 RPM (40% ED, i-450H class) | Not explicitly published; 22/15 kW motor (30/20 HP) implies ~500–700 Nm range |
| **Main Spindle Power** | 26 kW (34.7 HP) | 37 kW (50 HP) | 22 kW cont / 32 kW peak (30/43 HP) |
| **Chuck Size** | 10" std (A2-8), 12" opt | 12" (A2-8) | 12" (A2-11) |
| **Machine Mass** | ~6,000 kg (13,200 lbs) | ~12,300 kg (27,120 lbs, older 400-II) | ~20,000 kg (44,000 lbs) |
| **Guideway System** | Box guideways (X/Y/Z) — highest damping | Rigid linear roller guides (X/Y/Z) | Flat bed, orthogonal structure |
| **Driven Tool Torque** | 100 Nm (BMT turret, 12,000 RPM) | ~120 Nm (rotary spindle, 12,000 RPM) | CAPTO C6 interface, 22 kW peak |

**Assessment:** For titanium roughing, the **Mazak INTEGREX** leads in raw spindle power and torque, while the **DMG MORI NLX** benefits from box guideways — traditionally preferred for titanium because cast-iron box ways provide superior vibration damping compared to roller guides. The **Okuma Multus U4000** compensates with enormous machine mass (44,000 lbs), which inherently dampens chatter, but its published spindle power (22 kW continuous) is the lowest of the three, which may limit metal removal rates in heavy titanium roughing.

## 3. Recommended Tooling — Ceramic Limitations for Titanium

**Ceramic inserts are generally unsuitable for Ti-6Al-4V machining.** Titanium's low thermal conductivity causes heat to concentrate at the cutting edge, and ceramics' poor thermal shock resistance leads to catastrophic fracture. Titanium also exhibits strong chemical reactivity with most ceramic tool materials (particularly alumina-based and SiAlON ceramics), causing built-up edge and rapid wear.

**Recommended tooling for Ti-6Al-4V instead includes:**

- **Carbide inserts (C2/C3 grades):** Fine-grained WC-Co substrates with TiAlN or AlCrN PVD coatings. These remain the industry standard for titanium turning and milling.
- **PCD (polycrystalline diamond):** Limited applicability — titanium's chemical affinity for carbon causes rapid tool wear. Avoid for roughing; sometimes used for finishing with high coolant volume.
- **CBN (cubic boron nitride):** Can be used for finishing passes on titanium, but cost-effectiveness is marginal vs. coated carbide for most operations.
- **High-pressure coolant (HPC):** Essential at 70–150 bar (1,000–2,200 PSI) to manage heat and aid chip breaking. All three machines offer or can be equipped with through-spindle HPC.

For the BMT/CAPTO milling turrets on these machines, Sandvik Coromant Capto C5/C6 holders with TiAlN-coated carbide inserts (e.g., Sandvik GC1115 or Walter WSM35S grades) are typical aerospace recommendations.

## 4. Thermal Compensation for ±0.0005" (±0.013 mm) Tolerance

Maintaining ±0.0005" in titanium over multi-hour shifts requires active thermal management, as titanium's low thermal conductivity causes significant heat buildup in the workpiece and cutting zone.

| Feature | DMG MORI NLX 2500 | Mazak INTEGREX i-H | Okuma Multus U4000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spindle thermal mgmt** | Spirally arranged oil jacket around spindle | Spindle temperature control system | Thermo-Friendly Concept (Okuma's branded system) |
| **Ball screw cooling** | Internally cooled ball screws | X/Y/Z ball screw core cooling | Implied by OSP thermal algorithms |
| **Bed/structure control** | Bed temperature control | Ai Thermal Shield (AI-driven auto compensation) | Structural thermal deformation compensation |
| **Feedback system** | SmartSCALE direct measuring (optional full closed-loop) | Standard encoders + AI compensation | Standard encoders + OSP compensation |
| **AI/Adaptive comp.** | Not highlighted | Yes — "Ai Thermal Shield" auto-determines compensation | OSP-P500/S300 thermal algorithms |

**Assessment:** The **DMG MORI NLX** has the most physically robust thermal management (oil-jacketed spindle, cooled ball screws, bed temp control, and optional direct glass-scale feedback via SmartSCALE). This hardware-intensive approach is arguably more reliable for ±0.0005" than purely algorithmic compensation. The **Mazak Ai Thermal Shield** is the most technologically advanced software solution. **Okuma's Thermo-Friendly Concept** is well-regarded but the least documented for this specific tolerance class in published literature.

## 5. Siemens NX CAM Integration

All three machines integrate with Siemens NX CAM through the **Post Hub** ecosystem, which offers 1,100+ cloud-based postprocessors covering DMG MORI, Mazak, and Okuma machine/controller combinations.

- **Post Hub** allows direct access from within NX CAM's Post Process dialog, with one-click installation of manufacturer-certified postprocessors.
- **DMG MORI** has additionally partnered with third-party ISVs like EQUVERA for manufacturer-certified NX postprocessors that include machine kinematics models, integrated postprocessors, and CSE (CNC Simulation Engine) drivers for NC code validation.
- **Mazak's** MAZATROL SmoothAi control is proprietary but accepts standard G-code from NX postprocessors. Some shops report needing custom postprocessor tweaks for MAZATROL-specific cycles.
- **Okuma's** OSP-P300/500 control is more G-code native, which generally simplifies NX postprocessor integration.

**Assessment:** All three are viable. DMG MORI has the deepest official NX integration via certified postprocessors and simulation. Okuma's OSP control is the most G-code standard, minimizing postprocessor customization. Mazak's proprietary MAZATROL may require more postprocessor tuning for complex multi-tasking toolpaths.

## 6. AS9100D Traceability via MTConnect / OPC-UA

AS9100D compliance requires documented process control, traceability of machining parameters, and calibration records. MTConnect and OPC-UA provide the data pipeline from machine to QMS.

- **DMG MORI CELOS:** Supports IoT monitoring with standard protocols. The CELOS panel can export spindle load, temperature, tool data, and cycle times. Compatible with MTConnect via DMG MORI's Digital Factory solutions.
- **Mazak MAZATROL SmoothAi:** Supports **OPC-UA and EtherNet/IP** natively, enabling seamless MTConnect gateway integration. Spindle load monitoring, tool life tracking, and thermal compensation data are accessible for continuous AS9100D traceability logging.
- **Okuma OSP-P300/500:** Okuma offers **Connect Plan** (their IoT platform) which supports MTConnect and OPC-UA for machine status, spindle data, and alarm monitoring.

All three platforms can feed data into a shop-floor SCADA/MES system for AS9100D-required process monitoring. The key differentiator is the depth of tool-specific and thermal data available through the standard API — Mazak's OPC-UA implementation is the most explicitly documented for this use case.

## 8. Long-Term Operating Cost Drivers

For 7,500+ annual cutting hours on titanium, the following dominate total cost of ownership:

| Cost Driver | Key Considerations |
|---|---|
| **Tooling consumption** | Ti-6Al-4V wears carbide inserts 3–5× faster than steel. At ~$8–15/insert edge and aggressive roughing cycle times, annual insert cost can reach $50,000–$120,000 per machine. Higher spindle torque (Mazak) allows heavier DOC, potentially reducing passes but not necessarily insert life. |
| **Energy** | 22–37 kW spindle + coolant + hydraulics + HVAC. At ~$0.08–0.12/kWh (Mexico industrial rate), annual electricity per machine runs $15,000–$30,000. The Okuma's heavier mass requires more power to thermally stabilize. |
| **Coolant & fluid management** | Titanium demands high-pressure coolant (70–150 bar). Coolant replacement, filtration, and disposal run $5,000–$10,000/year. All three machines accommodate HPC. |
| **Maintenance & parts** | Spindle rebuilds ($15,000–$40,000), turret servicing, ball screw replacement. Local service proximity (Mazak > DMG MORI >> Okuma in NL) directly affects downtime cost — a day of unplanned downtime on aerospace titanium runs $3,000–$8,000 in lost revenue. |
| **Consumables (filters, way covers, wipers)** | Titanium chips are abrasive and aggressive on way covers and seals. Budget $3,000–$6,000/year. |
| **Calibration & compliance** | AS9100D requires periodic laser calibration and thermal verification. Budget $2,000–$4,000/year per machine. Machines with built-in thermal compensation (all three) reduce the frequency of external calibration interventions. |
| **Capital depreciation** | NLX 2500: ~$150K–$220K; Integrex i-400S/i-H: ~$250K–$400K; Multus U4000: ~$300K–$450K. At 7,500 hrs/year over 10 years, depreciation per cutting hour ranges from $2.00–$6.00 depending on machine and configuration. |

## Uncertainty & Gaps

- **Okuma Multus U4000 spindle torque** was not explicitly published in Nm; the 22/15 kW motor rating implies a torque range but should be confirmed directly with Okuma for precise comparisons.
- **Okuma Nuevo León service**: No direct facility was found. Okuma likely serves Mexico through authorized distributors, but response time and parts availability in NL could not be verified.
- **Mazak Integrex i-400S** specifications are partially inferred from the current i-H series successor and the older 400-II model; the exact i-400S configuration may differ.
- **Ceramic tooling limitations**: While industry knowledge confirms ceramic unsuitability for titanium, no specific research paper was retrieved during this quick-depth search. The recommendation for carbide-grade inserts should be cross-referenced with Sandvik/Walter/Seco application guides for Ti-6Al-4V.
- **Thermal compensation quantification**: None of the three OEMs publish guaranteed thermal drift specifications (e.g., "≤ X μm over 8 hours") that would definitively prove ±0.0005" capability. This should be validated through on-site test cuts during evaluation.
