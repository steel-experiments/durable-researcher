## Executive Summary

As of December 8, 2025, only one NCD public issue is confirmed open for subscription: **Edelweiss Financial Services Limited (EFSL)**, offering coupon rates of 8.85%–10.10% with a CRISIL A+/Stable rating. A second notable issue from **Adani Enterprises** (up to 8.90%, ₹1,000 Cr) opens on January 6, 2026, and warrants forward positioning. Critically, **NCD interest income does not qualify for the Section 80TTB senior-citizen deduction**, making post-tax yields identical for both regular and senior-citizen investors—a frequently overlooked point in retiree portfolio construction. The EFSL NCD carries moderate credit risk (A+ grade, not top-tier AA/AAA) and the issuer shows signs of balance-sheet contraction, which demands careful sizing.

## 3. Post-Tax Yield Analysis: Generic Investor vs. Senior Citizen

### 3.1 Critical Finding — Section 80TTB Does NOT Apply to NCDs

This is the most consequential tax finding for retiree portfolio construction:

**Section 80TTB** provides senior citizens (age 60+) a deduction of up to **₹50,000/year on interest income** — but this applies **exclusively** to:
- Bank fixed deposits (FDs)
- Recurring deposits (RDs)
- Savings accounts
- Post office deposits (SCSS, MIS, TD)

It **explicitly excludes**:
- Interest from corporate bonds or debentures
- Interest from NBFC deposits
- Dividend income

*Source: [7], [8]*

> "Deduction under Section 80TTB is NOT available for: Interest from corporate bonds or debentures. Interest from deposits with Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs)." [8]

### 3.2 Post-Tax Yield Comparison Table

| Tax Slab | Pre-Tax Yield (EFSL 10-Yr) | Post-Tax Yield | Pre-Tax Yield (EFSL 2-Yr) | Post-Tax Yield |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0% (Below ₹3L / ₹3L senior)** | 10.10% | **10.10%** | 8.85% | **8.85%** |
| **5% (₹3–7L, new regime)** | 10.10% | **9.60%** | 8.85% | **8.41%** |
| **20% (₹10–12L)** | 10.10% | **8.08%** | 8.85% | **7.08%** |
| **30% (Above ₹15L)** | 10.10% | **7.07%** | 8.85% | **6.20%** |
| **30% + 4% HEC** | 10.10% | **6.79%** | 8.85% | **5.95%** |

**Key conclusion:** Post-tax yields for NCDs are **identical for senior citizens and regular investors** at the same tax slab. Unlike bank FDs where a senior citizen in the 0% slab effectively earns a higher post-tax return due to the ₹50,000 Section 80TTB deduction, NCDs offer no such advantage.

### 3.3 NCD vs. Bank FD Comparative Post-Tax Yield (Senior Citizen, ₹5L Investment)

| Instrument | Pre-Tax Yield | 80TTB Eligible? | Post-Tax (0% slab) | Post-Tax (20% slab) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EFSL NCD (10-yr) | 10.10% | ❌ No | 10.10% | 8.08% |
| Bank FD (Senior) | ~7.25% | ✅ Yes | ~7.25% | ~7.25%* |

- **Declining total income** (-1% YoY in FY25) despite operating in a high-rate environment
- **Shrinking asset base** over a 3-year period (from ₹41,622.50 Cr), signaling ongoing business consolidation or de-leveraging
- **Marginal PAT growth** of only 1% YoY to ₹535.82 Cr

- The **use of proceeds for "repayment of existing borrowings"** suggests refinancing pressure rather than growth capital

> "Assets have been gradually reducing over a 3-year period." [3]

**Risk Flag: 🟡 MODERATE-HIGH** — The combination of a sub-AA credit rating, shrinking assets, and the need to refinance existing debt through a public NCD issue at 10.10% (well above G-Sec yields) suggests the issuer faces elevated funding costs. This is consistent with higher leverage ratios typical of A+-rated NBFCs. **Institutional investors should request the Prospectus/Draft Offer Document for the exact D/E ratio before committing capital.**

## 6. Comprehensive Risk Assessment

### 6.1 Credit Risk
- **A+ rating = adequate but not strong** — vulnerable to economic downturns
- EFSL is an NBFC exposed to credit cycle risk; any deterioration in asset quality could trigger a downgrade
- Historical precedent: During 2018–2020 IL&FS/DHFL crisis, A-rated NBFC paper suffered severe markdowns

### 6.2 Interest Rate / Duration Risk
- 10-year tenure at 10.10% carries significant mark-to-market risk if rates decline and the bond is sold before maturity
- For retirees holding to maturity, this is mitigated but not eliminated

### 6.3 Liquidity Risk
- NCDs listed on BSE/NSE typically have **thin secondary markets** — exit before maturity may involve steep discounts
- This is a structural issue across Indian corporate bond markets

### 6.4 Tail Risk
- **Concentration risk**: A+ rated NBFC sector has seen clustered defaults in stress scenarios
- **Regulatory risk**: Tightening NBFC norms from RBI could increase compliance costs and compress margins
- **Refinancing risk**: EFSL's use of proceeds for debt repayment indicates reliance on capital markets access

### 6.5 Inflation Risk
- With CPI inflation in India around 4.5–5.5%, the real yield on the 10.10% NCD is approximately 4.6–5.6% — reasonable but not generous

## 7. Regulatory Landscape

### 7.1 Current SEBI Framework (2025)

- **Mandatory exchange listing** for all public NCD issues
- **Comprehensive disclosure requirements**: Term sheets, risk factors, covenants, and financial data
- **CRISIL/ICRA ratings mandatory** with ongoing surveillance
- **Online Bond Platform Providers (OBPPs)** now regulated, improving transparency for retail investors
- **Municipal bonds as repo collateral** (effective Oct 22, 2025) — broadening the fixed-income ecosystem

> "Issuers must provide detailed term sheets, risk factors, covenants, and financial data." [6]

### 7.2 Tax Regulatory Notes
- TDS at 10% on NCD interest exceeding ₹5,000/year
- Senior citizens can file **Form 15H** to avoid TDS if total income is below taxable limit
- No Section 80TTB benefit for NCD interest — this is a **structural disadvantage vs. bank deposits** for retiree portfolios
