# Real-Time Object Detection for Warehouse Inventory: YOLOv8 vs EfficientDet-D4 vs DetectNet_v2 on Jetson AGX Orin

## Executive Summary

For a 200-camera, 30 fps warehouse deployment detecting pallet labels and package damage, **YOLOv8 with TensorRT INT8 optimization on Jetson AGX Orin is the recommended primary framework**, delivering 38–91 FPS (depending on model variant) with only 1.5–4.1% mAP degradation versus FP16. NVIDIA TAO DetectNet_v2 offers the most robust incremental learning pipeline via its structured transfer-learning and pruning workflow, making it the strongest secondary choice for SKU-heavy environments. EfficientDet-D4, while accurate (49.7 mAP on COCO), carries the heaviest computational penalty and the least mature edge deployment toolchain, making it the least practical option.

---

## 1. Inference Latency: INT8 Quantized Performance

### YOLOv8 on Jetson Orin (TensorRT INT8)

The most directly applicable benchmarks come from Jetson Orin hardware testing:

| Model | FP16 FPS | FP16 Latency | INT8 FPS | INT8 Latency | INT8 mAP | FP16 mAP | Degradation |
|-------|----------|-------------|----------|-------------|----------|----------|-------------|
| YOLOv8n | 60 | 7.3 ms | 63 | 5.8 ms | 33.0 | 37.1 | −4.1 pts |
| YOLOv8s | 48 | 11.4 ms | 57 | 8.2 ms | 41.2 | 44.7 | −3.5 pts |
| YOLOv8m | 30 | 23.6 ms | 38 | 17.1 ms | 46.2 | 50.0 | −3.8 pts |

**Critical finding**: A production manufacturing benchmark on the Jetson AGX Orin 64GB achieved **11 ms latency (91 FPS) with YOLOv8m using TensorRT INT8 + dual DLA cores**, with mAP of 0.898 — only ~1.5% degradation from baseline. This was achieved with carefully curated calibration data (800 curated images outperformed 5,000 random ones).

For your 30 fps target across 200 cameras, the AGX Orin can theoretically handle 6–8 camera streams per module (at 38–91 FPS total), requiring **25–34 AGX Orin modules** for the full deployment.

### EfficientDet-D4

EfficientDet-D4 achieves 49.7 mAP on COCO test-dev with 20.7M parameters and 55.2B FLOPs. On a Tesla V100 (without TensorRT), latency is **42.8 ms (23 fps)** for batch size 1. On Jetson AGX Orin, expect latency in the **60–100 ms range** (~10–17 fps) even with TensorRT optimization — well below the 33 ms budget for 30 fps. The Lite variants (EfficientDet-Lite4) retain 42.83 mAP when INT8 quantized but at 260 ms latency on mobile-class hardware, suggesting significant overhead.

**Verdict**: EfficientDet-D4 cannot meet 30 fps single-stream inference on Jetson Orin without aggressive input resolution reduction, which would compromise small-label and damage detection.

### NVIDIA TAO DetectNet_v2

DetectNet_v2 uses a grid-based architecture (16×16 cell grid) outputting coverage and bounding box tensors. NVIDIA reports up to **200 fps for DetectNet models** on Jetson AGX Orin 64GB. With PeopleNet-ResNet18 using QAT (Quantization-Aware Training), INT8 inference achieves **1,517 FPS** (nearly 2× the 762 FPS FP16 baseline) with only 1% accuracy drop (78.06 vs. 78.37 baseline).

However, these benchmark numbers use lightweight backbones (ResNet18) on simpler detection tasks. For pallet label and damage detection with a richer class set, expect **15–25 ms per frame (40–67 fps)** with ResNet34/50 backbones — still comfortably within the 30 fps budget.

## 2. mAP Degradation: INT8 vs FP16

Quantization impact varies dramatically based on technique:

| Framework | PTQ mAP Drop | QAT mAP Drop | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| YOLOv8 (PTQ) | 3.5–4.1 pts | Not widely tested | Calibration quality is critical; poorly calibrated INT8 can yield 0.0 mAP |
| DetectNet_v2 (QAT) | 18+ pts (catastrophic with PTQ on ResNet34) | ~1 pt | TAO QAT workflow recovers nearly all accuracy |
| EfficientDet-D4 | ~4–7 pts (estimated) | ~2–3 pts (estimated) | Less mature INT8 toolchain for edge |

**Key insight**: Post-training quantization (PTQ) alone is risky. The YOLOv8-TensorRT community reports INT8 models returning **0.0 mAP** when calibration is inadequate. NVIDIA's engineering blog demonstrates that PTQ on PeopleNet-ResNet34 crashed from 80.2% to 62.0% accuracy — a catastrophic drop. **Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) is essential** for production deployments, and TAO DetectNet_v2 has the most mature QAT pipeline.

For your 50K warehouse image dataset, allocate **800–1,000 carefully curated images** (representing edge cases: varying lighting, partial occlusion, damaged packages) as the calibration set, rather than random sampling.

## 3. Power Draw Under Sustained Load

The Jetson AGX Orin 64GB has an adjustable TDP of **50–60 Watts**. Real-world production benchmarks show:

- **Idle**: ~8W
- **Sustained 8-camera workload**: 38W average
- **Peak inference bursts**: up to 60W
- **Cooling**: Active fan (80mm + heat pipes) required for sustained multi-camera loads

For 200 cameras with ~30 modules, expect **total rack power of ~1,140–1,800W** (38–60W × 30 modules). This is highly favorable compared to GPU server alternatives.

## 4. TensorRT Optimization Gains

TensorRT acceleration is the single largest performance multiplier:

| Metric | Without TensorRT | With TensorRT INT8 | Gain |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|------|
| YOLOv8m latency | ~89 ms | ~11 ms (with DLA) | **8× speedup** |
| DetectNet_v2 FPS | 762 (FP16) | 1,517 (INT8 QAT) | **2× speedup** |
| Inference per camera | ~33 ms → 3 ms achievable | Enables multi-camera per module |

The dual NVDLA cores on AGX Orin are a key differentiator — offloading inference to DLA frees the GPU for decode/preprocessing, enabling the multi-camera throughput your deployment requires.

## 5. Model Update Pipeline & Incremental Learning

### NVIDIA TAO DetectNet_v2 — **Best for Incremental Learning**

TAO provides the most structured MLOps pipeline:
- **Transfer learning on new target classes** without full retraining — the `target_class` mapping in the training config allows adding new SKU classes incrementally
- **Pruning → QAT retraining → export** workflow reduces model size 60–80%
- **CLI-driven automation** enables scripted monthly update cycles
- Native TFRecord data ingestion supports incremental dataset expansion

### YOLOv8 (Ultralytics) — **Good Flexibility, Less Structure**

- Simple fine-tuning on new data, but lacks built-in catastrophic forgetting mitigation
- Community tools for incremental learning exist but are fragmented
- Export to TensorRT is straightforward via Ultralytics API

### Active Learning Feasibility

Research on edge-based incremental learning (RILOD, ACIL frameworks) demonstrates:
- New object classes can be learned in **minutes** using knowledge distillation from the old model (classification, bbox regression, feature extraction)
- Active Class-Incremental Learning (ACIL) can discover 100% of new classes with **57–59% fewer annotations** using a "Committee of Detectors" approach
- Production practitioners use **A/B OTA deployment**: 10% of cameras receive the new model first, with automatic rollback if latency exceeds 2× baseline

**To maintain detection precision above 92% without full retraining:**
1. Run continuous inference on all 200 cameras with confidence thresholding
2. Auto-capture low-confidence detections as candidate training samples
3. Human-in-the-loop annotation of ~200–500 samples per new SKU
4. QAT retraining with knowledge distillation from the existing model
5. Monthly A/B deployment cycle via OTA

## 6. Model Compression for OTA Updates (Sub-100MB)

| Model | FP16 Size (est.) | INT8 Size (est.) | Pruned + INT8 | Fits <100MB? |
|-------|-----------------|-----------------|---------------|-------------|
| YOLOv8s | ~22 MB | ~11 MB | ~5–8 MB | ✅ Easily |
| YOLOv8m | ~52 MB | ~26 MB | ~10–15 MB | ✅ Easily |
| YOLOv8l | ~87 MB | ~44 MB | ~18–25 MB | ✅ Yes |
| DetectNet_v2 (ResNet34) | ~85 MB | ~22 MB | ~8–15 MB | ✅ Yes |
| EfficientDet-D4 | ~83 MB | ~21 MB | ~12–18 MB | ✅ Yes |

All three frameworks produce sub-100MB engines when INT8 quantized and pruned. **The practical accuracy floor when compressing below 100MB is approximately 3–5% mAP loss** from the uncompressed FP32 baseline, provided QAT is used. Without QAT, expect 10–20% degradation or catastrophic failure.

Over cellular backhaul (typical 5–20 Mbps uplink), a 25 MB model update takes **10–40 seconds** — well within practical OTA windows. Production deployments recommend **atomic A/B partition** schemes where the device maintains two model slots and switches only after validated download.

## 7. Recommendation

| Criterion | YOLOv8 | EfficientDet-D4 | DetectNet_v2 (TAO) |
|-----------|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| 30 fps on Orin | ✅ Excellent | ❌ Marginal | ✅ Good |
| INT8 mAP retention | ⚠️ 3.5–4 pt drop (PTQ) | ⚠️ ~4–7 pt drop | ✅ ~1 pt drop (QAT) |
| Power efficiency | ✅ Good | ❌ Heavier | ✅ Best |
| Incremental learning | ⚠️ Community tools | ❌ Minimal | ✅ Native TAO pipeline |
| OTA model size | ✅ 5–26 MB | ✅ 12–21 MB | ✅ 8–22 MB |
| TensorRT integration | ✅ Mature | ⚠️ Less mature | ✅ Native |

**Primary recommendation**: Deploy **YOLOv8m** with TensorRT INT8 for inference throughput, managed alongside **NVIDIA TAO DetectNet_v2** as the model retraining and update backbone. Use TAO's QAT pipeline for monthly SKU additions, export to TensorRT, and push OTA updates of 10–25 MB models via A/B partitioned deployment. This hybrid approach gives you the inference speed of YOLOv8 with the enterprise-grade lifecycle management of TAO.

**Achievable precision**: With QAT, curated calibration data, and monthly active learning cycles capturing ~500 new samples per SKU class, maintaining **92%+ detection precision is realistic** without full retraining — provided you implement knowledge distillation to prevent catastrophic forgetting of existing classes.

## Uncertainty & Gaps

- **EfficientDet-D4 on Jetson Orin**: No direct benchmarks found; latency estimates extrapolated from V100 figures and architectural complexity. Actual performance may differ by ±30%.
- **Custom dataset mAP**: All cited mAP figures are on COCO or domain-specific datasets (PeopleNet, manufacturing defects). Your 50K warehouse image dataset will have different baseline mAP, but the *relative degradation* from quantization should be similar.
- **Active learning precision floor**: The 92% maintenance target is achievable in theory based on RILOD/ACIL research, but no production warehouse deployment was found validating this exact threshold on 200-camera scale.
- **DetectNet_v2 warehouse accuracy**: No warehouse-specific DetectNet_v2 benchmarks were found; the 200 fps claim is for lightweight backbones on simpler tasks.
