# Heat Pump Dryer Comparison: Bosch Serie 8 vs LG 9kg vs Samsung 9kg
## For a Melbourne Heritage Terrace — 6–8 Loads Weekly

---

## Executive Summary

For a Melbourne heritage-listed terrace running 6–8 laundry loads per week, all three heat pump dryers eliminate the need for external venting — the critical heritage overlay constraint. The **Bosch Serie 8 (WTX88M20AU)** offers the lowest annual energy consumption on the AS/NZS test standard (98 kWh/yr at 1 use/week), a self-cleaning condenser that drastically reduces maintenance for high-frequency users, and the quietest confirmed operation at 62 dB(A). However, it is an 8 kg rather than 9 kg machine, and real-world reviews flag issues with its reverse-tumble action causing sheet bundling. The **LG DVH9-09W** provides the largest capacity with an auto-cleaning condenser and 9-star rating, but its per-cycle energy use is the highest of the three on the Australian test (2.48 kWh/cycle). The **Samsung DV90T6240LH** features AI-driven drying and a 20-year motor warranty but lacks a self-cleaning condenser — a significant drawback at 6–8 loads weekly. On total cost of ownership over 10 years, the Bosch holds a meaningful edge driven by its lower energy consumption and self-cleaning condenser, though the LG's included stacking kit and drain hose add value.

## 2. Energy Consumption Analysis at Victorian Electricity Rates

### Test Standard Context

The Bosch and LG figures are tested under **AS/NZS 2442.2**, the Australian standard for tumble dryer energy labelling, using the Cottons Cupboard Dry program at one use per week. The Samsung's 194 kWh annual figure comes from the **EU energy label**, which uses a different test methodology (EN 61121) — making direct comparison imperfect. Where possible, per-cycle AS/NZS figures are used.

### Projected Annual Energy Cost (Victorian Rate: ~$0.28–0.30/kWh)

For a household running **7 loads per week** (midpoint of 6–8), scaled linearly from the AS/NZS per-cycle figures:

| Metric | Bosch (8 kg) | LG (9 kg) | Samsung (9 kg)* |
|---|---|---|---|
| kWh per cycle (AS/NZS) | 1.88 | 2.48 | N/A (EU only) |
| Annual kWh (364 loads) | **684 kWh** | **903 kWh** | ~560–1,358 kWh (range) |

\* Samsung's per-cycle figure varies between 1.54 kWh (EU eco program) and 3.73 kWh (derived from 194 kWh ÷ 52 weeks). Without an AS/NZS 2442 test result, the Samsung energy comparison carries significant uncertainty.

**Key finding**: On comparable AS/NZS data, the Bosch uses approximately **24% less energy per cycle** than the LG. At 7 loads per week over a year, this translates to a saving of roughly **$66/year** in energy costs — meaningful over a 10-year horizon.

## 6. Installation Requirements and Heritage Overlay Compliance

### The Heritage Constraint

Heritage-listed terraces in Melbourne (typically under a Heritage Overlay in the local planning scheme, or managed by Heritage Victoria) generally **prohibit external modifications** including:
- Drilling through external walls for dryer vents
- Installing visible ductwork on heritage facades
- Any alteration to original building fabric visible from the street

### Why All Three Models Qualify

All three are **heat pump condenser dryers** — a fundamentally different technology from vented dryers. As confirmed by a third-party product review: *"Heat pump dryers can be placed anywhere in your home, as they do not require a vent to the outside"* [4]. Furthermore, a user review on a comparable heat pump dryer noted: *"Unlike a conventional dryer, the heat pump model doesn't add humidity to the air"* [1].

In practical terms, this means:
- No external wall penetration is required
- No humid air is expelled into the living space
- The dryer can be positioned anywhere with access to a standard power outlet

This is the single most important advantage for your situation. A vented dryer is essentially not viable in a heritage terrace without planning permit approval, which is unlikely to be granted.

### Specific Installation Notes

- **Bosch**: Dimensions 84.2 × 59.8 × 61.3 cm (65.2 cm with door handle). 145 cm power cord. Right-hinged door (non-reversible). Condensate is collected in a tank; an extendable hose may be available as an accessory for continuous drainage [5][6].
- **LG**: Dimensions 85.0 × 60.0 × 69.0 cm — **deepest unit at 69 cm**, which may be tight in a narrow terrace laundry. Reversible door. Includes stacking kit and drain hose as standard accessories [2].
- **Samsung**: Standard 60 cm width. Reversible glass door. Wi-Fi setup recommended for smart features [3].

### Heritage-Specific Recommendations

1. **Under-counter or cupboard installation**: All three can be freestanding in a cupboard/laundry niche. The Bosch's non-reversible right-hinged door may be a constraint in tight spaces.
2. **Condensate drainage**: For high-frequency use (6–8 loads/week), consider plumbing the condensate drain to a nearby waste pipe rather than manually emptying the tank daily. The LG includes a drain hose kit; the Bosch and Samsung may require an aftermarket solution.
3. **No building permit required**: Since no structural modifications are needed, heritage overlay restrictions are effectively bypassed.

## 7. Condenser Cleaning and Maintenance

This is arguably the **most critical differentiator** for a household running 6–8 loads weekly.

| Model | Condenser Type | Cleaning Frequency | Maintenance Burden |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bosch** | SelfCleaning Condenser (automatic) | None — automatic flush cycle | **Very Low** |
| **LG** | Auto Clean Condenser (claimed) | User reports suggest periodic manual deep-clean needed despite auto function | **Medium-High** (user complaints of dismantling required) |
| **Samsung** | 2-in-1 filter (manual) | Regular manual cleaning required | **Medium** |

At 6–8 loads weekly, lint accumulation is aggressive. The Bosch's genuinely self-cleaning condenser is a substantial advantage — the Bosch specification sheet explicitly states: *"SelfCleaning Condenser: the condenser automatically cleans itself and does not require any manual cleaning"* [5]. This eliminates the most tedious and frequently neglected maintenance task, which directly impacts ongoing energy efficiency (a clogged condenser degrades performance significantly).

The LG's "auto clean" feature appears to help but does **not** fully prevent lint buildup in the condenser matrix, based on user reports of having to dismantle the unit — *"I have had to pull the whole condenser apart to clean out the muck four times now"* [1].
