## Executive Summary

Four NCD public issues are active or opening in India as of December 8, 2025: **Edelweiss Financial Services (EFSL)**, **Muthoot Mercantile**, **KLM Axiva Finvest**, and **Prachay Capital**. The best-documented issue—Edelweiss—offers up to 10.10% effective yield on a 10-year secured NCD rated CRISIL A+/Stable. Post-tax yields for senior citizens (taxed at marginal slab rates on NCD coupon income) range from approximately 7.07% to 10.10% depending on income bracket, with no Section 80TTB benefit available. Credit ratings across these NBFC issuers cluster in the A-to-AA band, implying moderate credit risk that must be weighed against the attractive nominal yields in a falling-rate environment.

## 3. Post-Tax Yield Analysis: Generic Investor vs. Senior Citizen

### 3.1 Tax Treatment of NCD Interest Income

NCD coupon income is classified as **"Income from Other Sources"** under the Income Tax Act and taxed at the investor's **marginal slab rate**. Critically:

- **Section 80TTB** (₹50,000 deduction for senior citizens on interest income) applies **only to bank/FI deposits and post office deposits — NOT to NCD interest**
- **No Section 80C deduction** is available for NCD investment
- **TDS** is deducted at 10% if interest exceeds ₹5,000/year (20% if PAN not furnished)
- Capital gains on listed NCDs sold before maturity are taxed as STCG/LTCG

### 3.2 India Tax Slabs: FY 2025-26 (New Tax Regime — Default)

| Income Slab (₹) | Tax Rate |
|---|---|
| 0 – 4,00,000 | Nil |
| 4,00,001 – 8,00,000 | 5% |
| 8,00,001 – 12,00,000 | 10% |
| 12,00,001 – 16,00,000 | 15% |
| 16,00,001 – 20,00,000 | 20% |
| 20,00,001 – 24,00,000 | 25% |
| Above 24,00,000 | 30% |
| *Surcharge* | 10–25% above ₹50L |
| *Cess* | 4% on tax + surcharge |

**Note:** Senior citizens (60–80 years) and super senior citizens (80+ years) do **not** receive any special slab benefit under the new tax regime. Under the old regime, basic exemption limits are ₹3L (senior) and ₹5L (super senior), but the new regime is now default.

### 3.3 Post-Tax Yield Matrix (Edelweiss EFSL — 10.10% Gross Yield)

Assuming effective marginal rates (including 4% cess, excluding surcharge for typical retiree incomes):

## 5. Historical Precedent and Pattern Recognition

### 5.1 NBFC NCD Market Cycle Context

The current NCD issuance wave follows a well-established pattern from prior Indian credit cycles:

- **2018–2019 IL&FS/DHFL crisis:** NBFC liquidity crunch led to credit spread widening; A-rated NCDs traded at 300–500 bps over G-Sec. Investors who locked into high-yield A-rated NCDs in 2019–2020 earned outsized returns as spreads normalized.
- **2020–2021 COVID era:** RBI emergency rate cuts drove G-Sec yields to historic lows; NCD issuances surged with coupons of 8–10% for A-rated issuers, offering exceptional spread.
- **2022–2024 hiking cycle:** As RBI raised rates to 6.50%, NCD coupons climbed to 10–11.5% (e.g., KLM Axiva's July 2024 issue at 11.3%). Investors who locked in at peak rates benefited.
- **Current environment (late 2025):** With RBI expected to cut rates (repo at 6.50%, expectations of 25–50 bps cuts in 2026), **locking in 10.10% on a 10-year NCD represents a tactical opportunity** to capture elevated credit spreads before they compress.

### 5.2 Pattern: Front-Loading Issuance Before Rate Cuts

Indian NBFCs historically front-load NCD issuances in the quarter preceding expected rate cuts. The December 2025 cluster (4+ simultaneous issues) is consistent with this pattern and suggests issuers anticipate a more accommodative rate environment in H1 2026.

## 7. Comprehensive Risk Assessment

### 7.1 Credit Risk
- EFSL's CRISIL A+ rating places it 4 notches below AAA. Historical 10-year default rates for A-rated Indian issuers are approximately 2–5%, compared to <0.5% for AAA.
- NBFC sector remains vulnerable to asset-liability mismatches, particularly in microfinance and unsecured lending segments.

### 7.2 Interest Rate Risk
- 10-year tenor locks in yield but exposes investor to significant duration risk. A 100 bps rate rise would generate approximately 7–8% mark-to-market loss on the 10-year series.
- **Tail risk:** If RBI reverses to aggressive hiking (inflation surprise), NCD prices on secondary market would decline sharply, though hold-to-maturity investors are insulated.

### 7.3 Liquidity Risk
- NCDs listed on NSE/BSE typically have **thin secondary market volumes**, especially for non-AAA paper. Investors should assume buy-and-hold to maturity.
- No put/call options mentioned in EFSL issue structure — **no early exit mechanism**.

### 7.4 Inflation Risk
- If CPI inflation averages 5–6% over the next decade, the **real post-tax return for a senior citizen at 9.05% nominal** translates to approximately 3.0–4.0% real — adequate but not exceptional.

### 7.5 Regulatory / Tax Risk
- Potential changes to tax slabs in Union Budget 2026 could alter post-tax returns
- SEBI has been tightening NCD issuance norms; any retroactive compliance requirements could impact secondary market valuations
- NBFC regulatory framework (RBI Scale-Based Regulation) may impose additional capital requirements on issuers, potentially affecting credit profiles

### 7.6 Tail Risks
- **Systemic NBFC stress event** (a la IL&FS 2018): Low probability but high impact — would trigger broad-based spread widening and mark-to-market losses
- **Issuer-specific default/restructuring:** A+ rated issuer default probability is non-trivial over 10-year horizon
- **Premature death of investor:** 10-year lock-in creates estate planning complications; nominee inheritance process for NCDs can be administratively burdensome

## 9. Regulatory Landscape

### 9.1 Current Framework
- SEBI regulates public NCD issuances under SEBI (Issue and Listing of Non-Convertible Securities) Regulations, 2021
- All NCDs with maturity >1 year must be credited to the investor's demat account
- Minimum application size: ₹10,000 (typically 10 NCDs of ₹1,000 each)
- Mandatory credit rating from at least one SEBI-registered rating agency (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, etc.)

### 9.2 RBI Scale-Based Regulation (SBR) for NBFCs
- Effective October 2022, NBFCs are classified into four layers (Base, Middle, Upper, Top)
- Upper-layer NBFCs face enhanced capital requirements and governance norms — improving systemic safety
- EFSL, Muthoot group, and KLM Axiva are classified under appropriate SBR layers, affecting their regulatory capital buffers

### 9.3 Taxation Policy
- Union Budget 2025-26 maintained existing slab structure under new regime
- No change to NCD interest taxation proposed
- Senior citizens continue to receive Section 80TTB benefit (₹50K) on bank deposits but NOT on NCDs — this policy asymmetry disadvantages NCDs for retirees relative to bank FDs

### For Senior Citizen / Retiree Fixed-Income Allocation

**RECOMMENDATION: SELECTIVE OVERWEIGHT on EFSL NCD (shorter tenors) + Muthoot Mercantile NCD (pending terms confirmation)**

#### Thesis:
1. **Yield capture ahead of RBI easing cycle:** Current NCD coupons (8.85–10.10%) offer historically attractive spread over G-Sec (~320 bps). Locking in now maximizes carry before the expected 50–75 bps rate cut cycle in 2026.

2. **Risk-adjusted value for retirees:** At a post-tax yield of ~9.05% (for a senior citizen in the 10.4% effective slab), EFSL NCDs offer a **170–210 bps premium over bank FDs and SCSS**, partially compensating for the credit and liquidity risk.

3. **Tenor selection is critical:**
   - **Preferred for retirees:** 36-month tenor (~9.0–9.3% coupon) — balances yield with reinvestment flexibility and lower duration risk
   - **Aggressive option:** 60-month tenor (~9.5–9.8%) — for retirees with longer life expectancy and income certainty needs
   - **Avoid for retirees:** 120-month tenor — 10-year lock-in creates unacceptable liquidity and estate-planning risk for most retirees

4. **Diversification across issuers:** Allocate across EFSL and Muthoot Mercantile (when confirmed) to diversify issuer-specific credit risk. Avoid KLM Axiva for core retiree allocation.

5. **Hedge with sovereign products:** Pair NCD allocation with SCSS (₹30L limit) and RBI Floating Rate Bonds to create a barbell strategy — sovereign safety on one end, NBFC yield premium on the other.
