# Maternal Mortality & Prenatal Care Access: Rohingya Refugees in Cox's Bazar vs. Karen Refugees in Mae La (2017–2023)

## Executive Summary

A comparative analysis of maternal health outcomes among Rohingya refugees in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh, and Karen refugees in camps along the Thailand-Myanmar border reveals that the Thai-integrated healthcare model achieved markedly better antenatal care completion and skilled delivery rates, despite severe geographic isolation. The Rohingya camps—served by a fragmented NGO ecosystem—report maternal mortality ratios between 161 and 295 per 100,000 live births and skilled birth attendance around 69%, while the Karen camps, operating under Thai Ministry of Public Health (MOPH) protocols delivered by a single coordinating NGO, achieved over 90% ANC4+ completion and 80% skilled delivery. Chronic underfunding, exemplified by UNFPA's widening shortfall, has progressively eroded service capacity in both settings, with the Rohingya camps facing acute facility closures and staffing losses.

## 1. Antenatal Care Visit Completion (4+ Visits)

| Indicator | Rohingya (Cox's Bazar) | Karen (Thai Border Camps) |
|---|---|---|
| ANC4+ completion | **71.6%** (2019–2020 data, Camp-4) | **>90%** (2000–2018, Mae Ra Ma Luang & Mae La Oon) |

The roughly 20-percentage-point gap in ANC4+ completion reflects fundamentally different health system architectures. A cross-sectional survey of 415 Rohingya women in Camp-4 (September 2019–January 2020) found that 71.57% completed at least four antenatal care visits [[1]](https://www.academia.edu/144995096/Inequality_in_receiving_maternal_health_care_among_Rohingya_women_living_in_Cox_s_Bazar_refugee_camps_and_its_associated_factors). In the Karen camps, a retrospective analysis of two long-term refugee camps at the Thai-Myanmar border (2000–2018) found that "more than 90% of pregnant women attended four or more antenatal care visits" [[4]](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/primary-health-care-research-and-development/article/mother-child-and-adolescent-health-outcomes-in-two-longterm-refugee-camp-settings-at-the-thaimyanmar-border-20002018-a-retrospective-analysis/5A2A03E5B33226D61F84A028BB229992).

In the Karen camps, a single international NGO (Malteser International) coordinated primary care using Thai MOPH national treatment protocols, creating continuity of care and standardized outreach [[4]](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/primary-health-care-research-and-development/article/mother-child-and-adolescent-health-outcomes-in-two-longterm-refugee-camp-settings-at-the-thaimyanmar-border-20002018-a-retrospective-analysis/5A2A03E5B33226D61F84A028BB229992). In Cox's Bazar, multiple NGOs operate overlapping primary health centers across 33+ camps, leading to fragmented patient tracking and uneven service quality. The Rohingya figure of 71.6% comes from a household survey in Camp-4—a camp with relatively better infrastructure—suggesting the true camp-wide average may be lower [[1]](https://www.academia.edu/144995096/Inequality_in_receiving_maternal_health_care_among_Rohingya_women_living_in_Cox_s_Bazar_refugee_camps_and_its_associated_factors).

## 2. Skilled Birth Attendance (SBA)

| Indicator | Rohingya (Cox's Bazar) | Karen (Thai Border Camps) |
|---|---|---|
| Skilled birth attendance | **69.4%** (2019–2020) | **80%** (2000–2018 average) |
| Facility-based delivery | **59.5%** in adequately equipped facility | Majority in camp clinics, with C-section referral to Thai hospitals |

The Rohingya survey found that "288 (69.4%) reported that the skilled birth attendants (SBAs) were present during the previous delivery" and "247 (59.5%) gave birth to the last baby in a place with an adequate facility" [[1]](https://www.academia.edu/144995096/Inequality_in_receiving_maternal_health_care_among_Rohingya_women_living_in_Cox_s_Bazar_refugee_camps_and_its_associated_factors). In the Karen camps, "80% delivered by a skilled birth attendant" [[4]](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/primary-health-care-research-and-development/article/mother-child-and-adolescent-health-outcomes-in-two-longterm-refugee-camp-settings-at-the-thaimyanmar-border-20002018-a-retrospective-analysis/5A2A03E5B33226D61F84A028BB229992). The Karen camps' 80% SBA rate was achieved despite the nearest Thai district hospital being 80 km away: "The 80 km journey takes between 3.5 and 7 h depending on the season" [[4]](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/primary-health-care-research-and-development/article/mother-child-and-adolescent-health-outcomes-in-two-longterm-refugee-camp-settings-at-the-thaimyanmar-border-20002018-a-retrospective-analysis/5A2A03E5B33226D61F84A028BB229992).

In Cox's Bazar, nighttime camp violence, cultural preferences for home delivery, and transport delays from primary health centers to emergency surgical facilities suppress SBA utilization. The Friendship Hospital at Balukhali had "only six cots available and up to 12 women giving birth here each day, sharing the bed is the best option" [[2]](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2025/jan/02/threadbare-facilities-high-mortality-cats-in-the-corridors-the-realities-of-life-for-new-rohingya-mothers-in-coxs-bazar).

UNFPA reports that by 2023, the number of Rohingya women giving birth in health facilities had doubled, reaching approximately 85%, with UNFPA facilitating "more than 10,600 safe births in 2023 alone" [[6]](https://bangladesh.unfpa.org/en/news/funding-gap-increased-insecurity-jeopardize-lives-rohingya-women-and-girls). This improvement is encouraging but may reflect facility-level data that overrepresents women who reach facilities, as opposed to population-representative survey data.

## 3. Maternal Mortality

| Indicator | Rohingya (Cox's Bazar) | Karen (Thai Border Camps) |
|---|---|---|
| MMR (per 100,000 live births) | **179** (2019–2020 survey); **295** (UNICEF 2023); **161** (IOM 2025, Teknaf) | **126.5** (2000–2018) |

The Rohingya MMR shows considerable variation depending on data source and methodology. The academic survey found "a maternal mortality rate of 179 per 100,000 live births among Rohingya women, which is 2.5 times higher than the global target of 70" [[1]](https://www.academia.edu/144995096/Inequality_in_receiving_maternal_health_care_among_Rohingya_women_living_in_Cox_s_Bazar_refugee_camps_and_its_associated_factors). UNICEF's 2023 camp-wide figure was higher: "Unicef reported 84 maternal deaths in 2023 – 295 for every 100,000 births," with "maternal mortality is 44% higher than elsewhere in Bangladesh" [[2]](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2025/jan/02/threadbare-facilities-high-mortality-cats-in-the-corridors-the-realities-of-life-for-new-rohingya-mothers-in-coxs-bazar). IOM reported a maternal death rate of 161 per 100,000 live births in the Teknaf subset [[7]](https://bangladesh.iom.int/news/iom-brings-first-ever-comprehensive-emergency-obstetric-and-newborn-care-services-coxs-bazar-rohingya-camps).

The Karen camp MMR was lower: "maternal mortality (SDG3.1) remained high at 126.5/100,000 live births" [[4]](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/primary-health-care-research-and-development/article/mother-child-and-adolescent-health-outcomes-in-two-longterm-refugee-camp-settings-at-the-thaimyanmar-border-20002018-a-retrospective-analysis/5A2A03E5B33226D61F84A028BB229992). This is still nearly double the SDG target of 70. The very low C-section rate of 3.7% in the Karen camps—"caesarean section rates rose but remained low at an average of 3.7%" [[4]](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/primary-health-care-research-and-development/article/mother-child-and-adolescent-health-outcomes-in-two-longterm-refugee-camp-settings-at-the-thaimyanmar-border-20002018-a-retrospective-analysis/5A2A03E5B33226D61F84A028BB229992)—signals that surgical obstetric intervention was likely under-accessed, well below the WHO-recommended minimum of 10–15%.

### Causes of Maternal Death

In Cox's Bazar, the primary documented killers are postpartum hemorrhage and pre-eclampsia. "In November, three women died – two from pre-eclampsia and one from postpartum haemorrhage" at a single primary health clinic [[2]](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2025/jan/02/threadbare-facilities-high-mortality-cats-in-the-corridors-the-realities-of-life-for-new-rohingya-mothers-in-coxs-bazar). No population-level postpartum hemorrhage incidence rate was identified in available sources for either setting.

## 4. Neonatal Mortality (Within 28 Days)

| Indicator | Rohingya (Cox's Bazar) | Karen (Thai Border Camps) |
|---|---|---|
| Neonatal mortality rate | **Not available** in sources reviewed | **10.7 per 1,000 live births** (2011, Mae La post-SCBU) |

For Mae La specifically, the establishment of a Special Care Baby Unit (SCBU) between 2008 and 2011 reduced neonatal mortality by 51%. "The neonatal mortality in Maela camp as a whole declined by 51% from 21.8 to 10.7 deaths per 1000 live births over the corresponding period (p=0.04)" [[3]](https://www.academia.edu/98148974/Neonatal_Intensive_Care_in_a_Karen_Refugee_Camp_A_4_Year_Descriptive_Study). This was achieved using locally trained health workers providing basic neonatal resuscitation and supportive care. "There was a significant decrease in mortality from 19.3% (2008) to 4.8% (2011) among the infants admitted for prematurity (p=0.03)" [[3]](https://www.academia.edu/98148974/Neonatal_Intensive_Care_in_a_Karen_Refugee_Camp_A_4_Year_Descriptive_Study).

No comparable neonatal mortality rate for Rohingya camps was found in the sources reviewed. The late arrival of specialized neonatal care in Cox's Bazar—Save the Children opened the first maternal-neonatal hospital only in January 2026, with 59 beds [[5]](https://prod-www.savethechildren.org/us/about-us/media-and-news/2026-press-releases/bangladesh-hospital-mothers-newborns-coxs-bazar-refugee-camp)—suggests neonatal outcomes have been significantly worse.

## 5. Healthcare Delivery Models: NGO Fragmentation vs. Integrated Protocol

### Cox's Bazar: NGO-Operated Field Hospitals

Healthcare in the Rohingya camps is delivered by a constellation of international NGOs under coordination of the Bangladesh government's Civil Surgeon office. Key characteristics:

- **No dedicated maternal-neonatal hospital existed until January 2026**, when "the first hospital specializing in maternal and neonatal care has opened in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh" with 59 beds [[5]](https://prod-www.savethechildren.org/us/about-us/media-and-news/2026-press-releases/bangladesh-hospital-mothers-newborns-coxs-bazar-refugee-camp).
- **First-ever CEmONC services in Teknaf** were launched by IOM in August 2025 at Camp-24, "equipped with a modern operation theatre, specialist doctors in obstetrics and anaesthesia, blood transfusion services with 24/7 power backup" [[7]](https://bangladesh.iom.int/news/iom-brings-first-ever-comprehensive-emergency-obstetric-and-newborn-care-services-coxs-bazar-rohingya-camps).
- Prior to these developments, the 30-bed UNFPA-funded Friendship Hospital at Balukhali was the only facility inside the camps capable of managing complicated obstetric emergencies, including C-sections and blood transfusions [[2]](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2025/jan/02/threadbare-facilities-high-mortality-cats-in-the-corridors-the-realities-of-life-for-new-rohingya-mothers-in-coxs-bazar)[[6]](https://bangladesh.unfpa.org/en/news/funding-gap-increased-insecurity-jeopardize-lives-rohingya-women-and-girls).
- The facility had "only six cots available and up to 12 women giving birth here each day, sharing the bed is the best option" [[2]](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2025/jan/02/threadbare-facilities-high-mortality-cats-in-the-corridors-the-realities-of-life-for-new-rohingya-mothers-in-coxs-bazar).

### Thai Border Camps: MOPH Integration Model

Karen refugee camps operated under a fundamentally different model:

- A **single coordinating NGO** (Malteser International) delivered primary care in Mae Ra Ma Luang and Mae La Oon [[4]](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/primary-health-care-research-and-development/article/mother-child-and-adolescent-health-outcomes-in-two-longterm-refugee-camp-settings-at-the-thaimyanmar-border-20002018-a-retrospective-analysis/5A2A03E5B33226D61F84A028BB229992).
- Thai MOPH national treatment protocols were adopted, ensuring standardized evidence-based care [[4]](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/primary-health-care-research-and-development/article/mother-child-and-adolescent-health-outcomes-in-two-longterm-refugee-camp-settings-at-the-thaimyanmar-border-20002018-a-retrospective-analysis/5A2A03E5B33226D61F84A028BB229992).
- Camp-based midwives and health workers provided ANC, delivery, and postnatal care.
- Emergency obstetric referrals went to Thai public hospitals, though the "80 km journey takes between 3.5 and 7 h depending on the season" [[4]](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/primary-health-care-research-and-development/article/mother-child-and-adolescent-health-outcomes-in-two-longterm-refugee-camp-settings-at-the-thaimyanmar-border-20002018-a-retrospective-analysis/5A2A03E5B33226D61F84A028BB229992).
- Border health organizations provided additional specialized reproductive health services, with data on over 34,000 displaced women (2008–2019) [[5b]](https://www.academia.edu/116147508/Access_to_community_based_reproductive_health_services_and_incidence_of_low_birthweight_delivery_among_refugee_and_displaced_mothers_a_retrospective_study_in_the_Thailand_Myanmar_border_region).

The integrated model's advantages included protocol consistency, clear referral pathways, and long-term institutional memory—factors likely contributing to the superior ANC4+ and SBA rates.

## 6. Midwife-to-Population Ratios and Emergency Obstetric Access

No precise midwife-to-population ratios for either setting were found in the sources reviewed. However, several proxy indicators are revealing:

- UNFPA notes that midwives "can avert two-thirds of maternal and newborn deaths and stillbirths" and that midwives in Rohingya humanitarian settings are active in hard-to-reach communities [[6]](https://bangladesh.unfpa.org/en/news/funding-gap-increased-insecurity-jeopardize-lives-rohingya-women-and-girls).
- The Karen camps' locally trained health workers successfully delivered SCBU-level neonatal care, suggesting a robust community health worker staffing model [[3]](https://www.academia.edu/98148974/Neonatal_Intensive_Care_in_a_Karen_Refugee_Camp_A_4_Year_Descriptive_Study).
- In Cox's Bazar, the critical shortage is not only midwives but the physical infrastructure for them to work in—the 2025–2026 openings of CEmONC and maternal-neonatal facilities highlight how late comprehensive services arrived [[5]](https://prod-www.savethechildren.org/us/about-us/media-and-news/2026-press-releases/bangladesh-hospital-mothers-newborns-coxs-bazar-refugee-camp)[[7]](https://bangladesh.iom.int/news/iom-brings-first-ever-comprehensive-emergency-obstetric-and-newborn-care-services-coxs-bazar-rohingya-camps).

Regarding the 2-hour transport radius for emergency obstetric care:
- In the Karen camps, the nearest Thai hospital was 3.5–7 hours away, exceeding the 2-hour benchmark [[4]](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/primary-health-care-research-and-development/article/mother-child-and-adolescent-health-outcomes-in-two-longterm-refugee-camp-settings-at-the-thaimyanmar-border-20002018-a-retrospective-analysis/5A2A03E5B33226D61F84A028BB229992).
- In Cox's Bazar, the camps are geographically closer to district hospitals, but nighttime violence, transport logistics, and referral delays from primary to CEmONC-level facilities create de facto barriers [[2]](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2025/jan/02/threadbare-facilities-high-mortality-cats-in-the-corridors-the-realities-of-life-for-new-rohingya-mothers-in-coxs-bazar).

## 7. Cultural Mediation and Traditional Birth Attendant Integration

No specific data on traditional birth attendant (TBA) cultural mediation practices was found in the sources reviewed for either setting. The Karen camps' community-driven reproductive health models have been noted to increase long-acting contraceptive uptake in the Mae La setting [[5b]](https://www.academia.edu/116147508/Access_to_community_based_reproductive_health_services_and_incidence_of_low_birthweight_delivery_among_refugee_and_displaced_mothers_a_retrospective_study_in_the_Thailand_Myanmar_border_region), suggesting some degree of successful cultural brokerage. Anecdotal evidence from Cox's Bazar indicates that traditional home-birth preferences contribute to low facility-based delivery rates [[2]](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2025/jan/02/threadbare-facilities-high-mortality-cats-in-the-corridors-the-realities-of-life-for-new-rohingya-mothers-in-coxs-bazar). This dimension requires further primary research.

## 8. Impact of UNFPA Funding Reductions (2021 Onward)

The gradual erosion of humanitarian funding for Rohingya camps has had documented consequences:

- By 2024, "UNFPA's response is only 42% funded" against a $31 million requirement for sexual and reproductive health and GBV services in the camps [[6]](https://bangladesh.unfpa.org/en/news/funding-gap-increased-insecurity-jeopardize-lives-rohingya-women-and-girls).
- Foreign aid cuts forced facility closures: "Cuts to foreign aid in 2025, coupled with an overall reduction in donor funding, has led to the closure of some healthcare centers in Cox's Bazar" [[5]](https://prod-www.savethechildren.org/us/about-us/media-and-news/2026-press-releases/bangladesh-hospital-mothers-newborns-coxs-bazar-refugee-camp).
- UNFPA supports 56 women-friendly spaces and the critical Friendship Hospital—the only CEmONC-capable facility inside the camps. Funding shortfalls threaten the continuity of these services [[6]](https://bangladesh.unfpa.org/en/news/funding-gap-increased-insecurity-jeopardize-lives-rohingya-women-and-girls).
- UN agencies warn that "looming funding cuts could result in reduced functionality of healthcare facilities, loss of healthcare professionals, and disruptions to essential supply chains" [[8]](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/health/uncertainty-aid-commitments-threatens-bangladeshs-progress-maternal-health-unicef).

For the Thai border camps specifically, no direct evidence linking the 2021 UNFPA funding reduction to service disruption was found. The Karen camps' primary funding historically came through the Thai-Burma Border Consortium (TBBC) and bilateral NGO arrangements rather than UNFPA specifically, which may have buffered them from this particular funding shock. The long-term nature of the Karen displacement (2000–2018+) and integration with Thai public health infrastructure provided a more stable funding architecture compared to the annual humanitarian appeal cycle governing Rohingya camp services [[4]](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/primary-health-care-research-and-development/article/mother-child-and-adolescent-health-outcomes-in-two-longterm-refugee-camp-settings-at-the-thaimyanmar-border-20002018-a-retrospective-analysis/5A2A03E5B33226D61F84A028BB229992). However, this inference requires verification.

### Uncertainty & Gaps

- **No postpartum hemorrhage incidence data** was found for either population in specific percentage or rate terms; only case reports (e.g., one death from PPH in a single month at one clinic [[2]](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2025/jan/02/threadbare-facilities-high-mortality-cats-in-the-corridors-the-realities-of-life-for-new-rohingya-mothers-in-coxs-bazar)) were identified.
- **No Rohingya neonatal mortality rate (28-day)** was found; this is a critical missing indicator for comparison.
- **No precise midwife-to-population ratios** were available for either setting.
- **No data on traditional birth attendant cultural mediation practices** was found in the sources reviewed.
- **The Karen camp data [[4]](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/primary-health-care-research-and-development/article/mother-child-and-adolescent-health-outcomes-in-two-longterm-refugee-camp-settings-at-the-thaimyanmar-border-20002018-a-retrospective-analysis/5A2A03E5B33226D61F84A028BB229992) covers Mae Ra Ma Luang and Mae La Oon, not Mae La specifically**, though the SCBU data [[3]](https://www.academia.edu/98148974/Neonatal_Intensive_Care_in_a_Karen_Refugee_Camp_A_4_Year_Descriptive_Study) is from Mae La. Direct Mae La maternal mortality data for 2017–2023 was not found.
- **No MSF operational reports** with specific maternal health indicator data for either setting were located.
- **UNHCR health information system data** was sought but not successfully retrieved; the analysis relies on published academic studies and agency statements.
- The Rohingya MMR varies significantly across sources (161–295), likely reflecting different methodologies, time periods, and camp sub-populations. The true 2017–2023 trend remains unclear.
- The 2021 UNFPA funding reduction's differential impact on Karen vs. Rohingya settings could not be fully established due to limited data on Karen camp funding streams.
